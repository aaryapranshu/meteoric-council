A) Every new client signs build + retainer from January. B) Keep the 3-month build; offer a retainer to clients with results. C) Change nothing.

**Neither A nor B as written. Run a "Compound retainer" pilot on the next two cohorts.** Disclose the retainer at signing as the planned next stage, not a signed commitment. Write the results gate as a number. Make the ask at weeks 6, 8 and 12, owned by someone other than you. Price it as a real ticket. Decide on A as the default by 31 March 2027, on the data.

## Measured against your goals
Your Aug 2026 goal is six figures a month recurring. At an assumed $5K/month that's about 20 live retainers (Sales seat; the fee is assumed). The only data point in the brains says a week-10-only ask converts about 10% (Hormozi, `03`). At 10%, B as written can't get there. The pilot's earlier asks are how it can.

## Where the council agrees
- Don't make a mandatory, full-price retainer the default before renewal rate and month-4 scope exist (CFO, Sales, Fulfilment; Hormozi on "fix activation first").
- Disclose it at signing. Hormozi: "don't be a sneak". Sales: a disclosed next step, not a second signature on the closing call.
- The retainer fee must be a real ticket. About $2K/month sits in Hormozi's 4–6 month dead zone and contradicts "price high, scale by price" (CFO, Devil's advocate, you on the record).
- Sell it as the Compound stage of the Engine, not as an add-on (Hormozi, Fulfilment).

## Where it splits
- **Hormozi vs CFO: opt-out (A) vs opt-in (B).** Cross-examination narrowed it. Both now agree on the week 6/8/12 asks. Hormozi called B's 30–60% take rate fantasy against the 10% week-10 data point. The CFO says opt-out renewal with no result means refunds. **The CFO wins for now**, because there's no measured win by week 6. **Hormozi wins later** if the pilot shows one.
- **Sales vs Hormozi:** Sales wants nothing extra on the closing call. Hormozi wants it disclosed at signing. Resolved: disclose it as the next stage in the pre-sale asset and onboarding, not as a second contract on the call.

## You, on the record
Tension, not contradiction. "Land, deliver, then upsell" supports B. "Act first, prove it twice, then systematize" rules out A as a company-wide rule today. Your Aug 2026 goal means B can't be the end state. What you'd tell a client: *let it earn the ask, run it on the clients who got the win, ask twice before week 10, and see if two cohorts renew. Then make it the default.*

## What could go wrong
1. **The gate collides with your guarantee.** The site promises to keep working at no cost if the plan's goals are missed. Clients who miss the gate are owed free work, and only the happy ones get pitched. Early sign: by week 4 nobody can say who is gate-eligible.
2. **The ask lands on you, and dies in the pause.** Clients run in sprints with a month off. "Let's regroup in a month" kills a week-10 ask. Early sign: pause replies outnumber yeses.
3. **The fee gets pegged to the old ticket.** Early sign: the first retainer quotes are discounts.

## What we don't know
- Renewal rate after month 3, last two quarters: you / Head of Fulfilment
- Current fee, and what one client costs to deliver per month: you / first Money game
- How many clients show a measurable win by week 6: Head of Fulfilment (client board)
- Current close and show rate: sales sheet / EOD games

## If you go ahead
1. Write the gate as a number (what "a result by week 6" means for a founder) and score your last 10 finished clients against it. **Fulfilment lead, by 8 Nov.**
2. Price the Compound retainer, and check delivery cost per client so margin stays above your floor. **You + CFO, at Monday's Money game.**
3. Put the retainer in the pre-sale asset and onboarding as the planned next stage, with asks at weeks 6, 8 and 12 owned by a CSM, not you. **Ops lead, from the first January cohort.**
4. **Checkpoint, 31 March 2027:** if 40%+ of two cohorts take the retainer at a fee that clears the margin floor, make it the default (opt-out, disclosed). Under 20% means the result isn't landing by week 6. Fix delivery before selling continuity.
