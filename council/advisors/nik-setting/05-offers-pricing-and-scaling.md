# 05 — Offers, pricing, delivery model and scaling stages

**When to open this file:** "what should we charge?", "how do we sell $50K deals?", "should we add an offer?", "DFY or DWY?", "should we add a guarantee?", "we're stuck at $X/month", "how big should the team be?", "what margin is healthy?", "how should onboarding work?".
- His examples are info, coaching and consulting businesses. When applying to a DFY studio, read the DFY cards closely: that is the model he left.

---

### Price from what the problem costs (his three questions)
**When to use:** setting or raising a price.
**The idea:** Most people price by counting hours and calls, which is how you end up at $5K. He prices on what it changes for the buyer, so the buyer decides the price, not you.
**How to run it:**
1. What is the problem costing them every year?
2. What is it worth once solved?
3. How sure are they it'll work?
4. Run the same offer at two buyer levels and see who it's cheap for.
**The numbers (his worked example, Oct 2026):** a founder at $200K/month whose bottleneck costs $50K/month = $600K/year; a $50K fee is "less than 10%" of that. The same problem at $20K/month costs $60K/year, so the same $50K "feels expensive" (Tr2WIG6-1VU [10:23–11:06]). "That's why who you talk to decides your price point. It's not you."
**Mistakes he calls out:** copying what others charge and adding a little (Tr2WIG6-1VU [09:41–10:02]); "pricing isn't defined by the amount of resources time or money" (FYN7JcBOGDs [06:12]); "Raise your price before you post more content. Discounting doesn't… fill any spots… It fills them with worse clients." (Tr2WIG6-1VU [15:38]).
**Ask first:** What does the buyer's problem cost per year? What revenue are the last five buyers at?
**Source:** Tr2WIG6-1VU [09:41–11:47], FYN7JcBOGDs [06:12–06:33]

### The $50K offer: less of your time, not more deliverables (UNNAMED)
**When to use:** "how do we go upmarket?", "how do we justify a higher price?".
**The idea:** Most people add calls, modules and access to a $5K offer to justify $50K, and end up with a DFY workload that serves one client. At $50K buyers pay so they need less of your time, because they get there faster. "The more money you pay me, the less time you'll spend with me."
**How to run it:**
1. Write everything in the offer; cross out everything that only takes more of your time (calls, check-ins, modules). What's left is what they pay for (Tr2WIG6-1VU [09:20–09:41]).
2. Rewrite an intangible offer as: who it's for and their stage → what changes because of it → the how in three phases → why this, not what they tried (the belief shift) (Tr2WIG6-1VU [07:36–08:39], his hypnosis-client example).
3. Sell the same knowledge at levels of access: course → group → one-to-one with the team → private one-to-one with the founder; "the top one exists even if only 10 people a month buy it" (Tr2WIG6-1VU [11:47–12:08]).
4. Make the back end better than the front end at that price (Tr2WIG6-1VU [15:59]).
**The numbers:** client price moves he reports: $3K → $10K → $15K PIF in about a month; $5K → $24K → $42K; a $50K PIF; $2M in a client's first three months (Tr2WIG6-1VU [01:01–01:42], client claims). "If your offer currently is hard to explain, it's not hard to sell. It's just not explained yet." (Tr2WIG6-1VU [08:39]).
**Mistakes he calls out:** "When the value is invisible, the price will always feel random" (Tr2WIG6-1VU [07:36]); "If you don't have real expertise, results you can show and deliver that holds. No price is the right price" (Tr2WIG6-1VU [16:19]).
**Ask first:** Strip the offer to outcomes: what's left? What does the founder's best client say they paid for?
**Source:** Tr2WIG6-1VU [06:10–16:39]

### No guarantees, no discounts, one offer marketed (his rules)
**When to use:** someone proposes a guarantee, a promo or a menu of packages.
**The idea:** Guarantees and big promises attract the wrong buyers and create the next constraint. He markets one solution and uses other access levels only on calls (downsell if unqualified). Never Black Friday deals.
**The numbers / statements:** "we never added a guarantee to our offer because I don't want to dilute my brand" (0I_cEtloYb4 [52:50]); "A guarantee attracts the clients who need one." (Tr2WIG6-1VU [15:59]); "I have no guarantee for my offer" (wY5TWeKaJow [06:12]); "We always market one solution. So we're never going to give people options." (eJItxb8Hs-E [28:08]); 0% Black Friday discount, "people that need a profit from Black Friday discounts are people that… have shitty productss" (b5urvngM8wU [11:41–12:01]). Reaching $100K "through a level of force" (guarantees, big promises, survival state) is what stalls founders at $100–250K (0I_cEtloYb4 [14:12]).
**Price-ladder trick:** DIY $4K / DWY $8K / DFY $10K: the 4→8 gap makes DWY the anchor; market only the middle (eJItxb8Hs-E [16:54–17:36], [28:08–28:48]).
**Mistakes he calls out:** "I guarantee an extra X appointments… in Y time" commodity offers, "99% of the market" (FYN7JcBOGDs [05:12]); fake scarcity (b5urvngM8wU [05:10]).
**Source:** 0I_cEtloYb4 [14:12, 52:50], Tr2WIG6-1VU [15:38–15:59], eJItxb8Hs-E [16:54–28:48], b5urvngM8wU [11:41]

### The DFY agency ceiling and the responsibility shift (UNNAMED)
**When to use:** any DFY service business, including a content studio.
**The idea:** DFY at a low price with a long deliverables list puts all responsibility on you: more front-end sales → fulfilment friction → founder buried in delivery → no front-end work and weaker results → churn → no cash for ads → stall at $5–15K. Fixes: move responsibility to the client (DWY or hybrid), or productise delivery so client five costs what client one does.
**How to run it:**
1. Keep the same deliverables but shift who is responsible; that alone let him charge far more ("s to 12K" in captions, probably $7–12K) (FYN7JcBOGDs [14:39–16:43]).
2. Build delivery once: 80% systematised, 20% personalised; one infrastructure per niche (FYN7JcBOGDs [18:49–20:37]).
3. Front end personal, back end systematised: "the front end needs to look like a info business. The back end needs to look like a agency" (njWXZNfCm28 [23:03]; CAxLCrjz5Ss [12:55]).
4. Don't promise one-to-one in marketing and deliver group calls (CAxLCrjz5Ss [13:57]).
**The numbers:** his old DFY agency reached "$107,000 a month" in seven months, then stagnated with 70-hour weeks (FYN7JcBOGDs [00:00–03:07], self-told); "you cannot name five people that run an agency making over 50k a month" (FYN7JcBOGDs [12:34]); "scaling an agency past 250K a month is pretty difficult" (t6jzUsmeUTU [04:34]). A DFY organic-content agency client at $8K/month retainers was capped at 4–5 clients (~$30–40K/month) with ~2-month retention (eJItxb8Hs-E [01:02–03:22]). "That's the reason why done for you offers suck" (17Oc1LRuRKI [14:30]).
**Mistakes he calls out:** scaling the front end first ("I need more appointments… no you don't", FYN7JcBOGDs [06:54]); being dependent on the client instead of the client being dependent on you (wY5TWeKaJow [25:37]).
**Ask first:** If unlimited qualified leads arrived tomorrow, how many clients could delivery take? Who carries responsibility for the outcome in the contract?
**Source:** FYN7JcBOGDs [00:20–25:50], eJItxb8Hs-E [01:02–20:38], 17Oc1LRuRKI [14:30–14:53]

### Agency back end, consulting front end (UNNAMED; "rich vs wealthy")
**When to use:** deciding retainer vs one-time fee.
**The idea:** Consulting gives front-end cash but is volatile; agency retainers give LTV. Combine: consulting front end for cash, agency or infrastructure back end for recurring revenue. Ascend clients after ~4 months by asking what they struggle with now and routing them to an internal "department".
**The numbers:** "34% of our revenue this year has came from back-end cross sales" (QKnBoqLD2Cc [33:10], Oct 2025); upsell rate "81 82%" (eJItxb8Hs-E [27:05]); "we have 25 people recurringly paying us for over a year" (0I_cEtloYb4 [54:55]).
**Quotes:** "clients feel the most safe the moment they understand and they have the feeling they can work with you for years" (FYN7JcBOGDs [25:50]).
**Source:** FYN7JcBOGDs [11:50–25:50], eJItxb8Hs-E [27:05–30:09]

### Performance-based pricing (his real example)
**When to use:** founders at $100K+/month, raising price without raising risk.
**The idea:** Keep the upfront price, add a second payment tied to a result. It raises perceived value and back-end revenue while the yes-rate holds.
**The numbers:** a $12K offer became $24K: "12 grand upfront, 12 grand when you add an extra 100 grand"; took them "from about 400 grand a month to 680 that one month in May" (0I_cEtloYb4 [55:37–57:19], 2026). A client version: $14K = $7K upfront + $7K at $50K cash collected; "Let's say only 30%" pay the second half (_qpdvJz793E [28:58–30:44]). Introduce from $100–250K/month (0I_cEtloYb4 [48:02]).
**Source:** 0I_cEtloYb4 [46:36–57:19], _qpdvJz793E [28:58–30:44]

### Revenue-level ladder: one constraint per stage (UNNAMED; Oct 2026)
**When to use:** "we're stuck at $X/month".
**The idea:** Each band has one dominant constraint, and founders usually misread it as "need more".
**The stages (his opinions):**
- $0→30K "extremely easy"; $30K→100K "even easier": booking rate and setting, not lead flow (`04`).
- $100K→250K "extremely difficult": messaging. Founders got to $100K by force and talk mid-market; talk up market to the best case study, use analogies for reach (`02`). People can slide back "to about 60 to 70 grand a month".
- $250K→400K "pretty easy": imposter syndrome and shiny objects (don't start a software company), delegate by competitive edge, raise prices.
- $400K→$1M "extremely difficult": content decision-making (prepare vs improvise), pond→lake→ocean moves.
**The numbers:** "real freedom" came at $250K/month profit; at $100K revenue keeping $50–70K "you don't have money" (0I_cEtloYb4 [22:53–23:13]); most of his program's clients enter at $75–100K (0I_cEtloYb4 [12:27]); "over 25 companies" his team works with at $250K–2M/month have teams of 30–40 (0I_cEtloYb4 [41:06]).
**Ask first:** What is the founder's current band, and which constraint are they working on instead?
**Source:** 0I_cEtloYb4 [01:00–1:00:46]

### Friction: sell to an existing situation (UNNAMED)
**When to use:** choosing what to sell to a market; a pivot that "feels like a step back".
**The idea:** You can sell to an existing situation or help people create a new one; a new situation adds friction. Sell the version that changes the least about what buyers already do; put bigger offers on the back end once trust exists. Who you are and how you grow is separate from what you sell; he has sold the profile funnel for three years.
**The numbers:** a barber coach's pivot from getting barbers more clients and higher prices to building an online business added "five extra points of friction" (RkKTKPdOK40 [23:43–24:04]).
**Quotes:** "Business is all about releasing friction in the sales process of getting a customer to pay you." (RkKTKPdOK40 [31:15]); "It's always the person that they don't trust." (RkKTKPdOK40 [34:21]).
**Source:** RkKTKPdOK40 [03:10, 11:42–34:21]

### Onboarding: speed to trust, speed to execute, speed to outcome (his named lens)
**When to use:** churn, buyer's remorse, refunds in week one.
**The idea:** Trust comes from marketing before purchase; execution must start within hours, not after a CSM call; the first win must tie to the outcome (tangible for beginners, can be "clarity" for sophisticated buyers).
**How to run it:** a pre-made first module so clients "work on that within the first three hours", starting with messaging and positioning (YOuvbqU8liU [31:00–32:22]); test every CSM call: "is there a purpose for this call? Or… the purpose of the call to figure out the purpose of the call?" (YOuvbqU8liU [34:03]).
**The numbers:** "80% of the time CSM spent… on a coaching call… is wasted", which he calls industry standard (YOuvbqU8liU [33:22], unsourced).
**Related:** people pay for "accountability and confirmation", not information (YOuvbqU8liU [55:23–56:46]; eJItxb8Hs-E [32:10]).
**Source:** YOuvbqU8liU [26:13–43:40, 55:23–56:46]

### Lean team, high margin (UNNAMED)
**When to use:** hiring plans, margin targets, "do we need more people?".
**The idea:** Info and expertise businesses are for cash flow and profit, not headcount. A team that fits round one dinner table. Delegate by competitive edge; "We're never going to delegate things before we automate" (eJItxb8Hs-E [11:49], speaker unclear).
**The numbers:**
- Margin targets: at $30K/month profit at least ~$20K (66%); at $100K/month at least $70K; at $250K at least 50%; team cost ≤20–25% of revenue (7xAo-xh1DwQ [15:00–15:21]).
- His team, May 2026: "Setter, closer, ops manager, two CSMs, creative director, personal assistant… two editors", team cost $819,690 of $3,477,000 over 365 days, 71.1% margin after business costs (cqmikVh9njI [00:07–09:02]).
- Five or six people in Sep 2025: ops manager, setter, closer, content director, filmer (njWXZNfCm28 [04:30]).
- 79–83% margin claims: "83% profit" (xcpy678eggY [00:40], Dec 2024); 79% (QKnBoqLD2Cc [11:48], Oct 2025).
- Paying coaches 30% of revenue is over-compensating; pay a success coach a salary (7xAo-xh1DwQ [13:18–13:38]). Rev share only for people who add growth, on the increment (7xAo-xh1DwQ [15:42–16:03]).
- CSM incentive design: $3K base + 10% per PIF referral + 5% per renewal + $250 per testimonial + $100 per client-win screenshot (_qpdvJz793E [32:46–38:16]).
**Team ops:** daily 30-minute morning meeting and an evening sync in person; Airtable pipeline where each owner moves tasks; "Not tracking… is a bigger consequence than them not doing their work" (YOuvbqU8liU [36:07–39:57]).
**Mistakes he calls out:** businesses at $500K/month making $100K profit because of team complexity (7xAo-xh1DwQ [10:15]).
**Source:** 7xAo-xh1DwQ [10:15–17:25], cqmikVh9njI [00:07–29:24], YOuvbqU8liU [36:07–41:38], _qpdvJz793E [32:46–43:41]

### The chain: offer → acquisition → fulfilment (his metaphor)
**When to use:** revenue swinging month to month.
**The idea:** The components are a chain. When the founder moves 100% into fulfilling new clients, acquisition drops to zero and revenue swings (20K → 80K → 30K). Give every component attention at the same time through a lean team and automation; predictable inputs make predictable revenue.
**The numbers:** coaching businesses hit $100K then drop "back all the way to 20K" (eJItxb8Hs-E [15:54]); info businesses dip 70K → 130K → 80K → 240K → 20K (njWXZNfCm28 [25:45]).
**Leverage list:** write your 10 tasks, reorder by leverage; content is the highest-leverage founder hour (eJItxb8Hs-E [13:32–14:12]; 7xAo-xh1DwQ [17:25]).
**Source:** QKnBoqLD2Cc [33:50–35:11], njWXZNfCm28 [20:59–23:03], eJItxb8Hs-E [10:07–14:12]
