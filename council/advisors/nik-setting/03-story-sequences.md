# 03 — Story sequences, lead magnets and email

**When to open this file:** "what should we post on stories?", "how do we turn followers into DMs?", "how many sequences a week?", "our story views drop off", "should we use close friends?", "lead magnet ideas", "what happens after someone opts in?".
- He calls story sequences the highest-trust content and says most of his recent money came from them. Treat this as his core asset.

---

### Why story sequences (his thesis)
**When to use:** someone treats stories as an afterthought.
**The idea:** "reals find them, but stories sell them". A reel gets a stranger to the profile; stories are where they watch you week after week and decide. A sequence pre-sells, so the client "pays faster, argues less, and stays for longer". Stories nurture; they don't convert by themselves. Few people use them properly, which is why they work.
**The numbers:**
- Most money in the three months before May 2025 came from story sequences; one sequence ≈ $40K in two days (4 × $10K PIF) (8t92OBM83BA [04:46], [10:01]).
- "$200,000 with purely a story launch" in a week of Sep 2025 (eJItxb8Hs-E [15:13]).
- Doubling story views (3K → 6K) coincided with $110K (March) → $216K (June) months (eGWeRi6siNQ [36:53], 2025; correlation he presents, not proof).
- His model: each story watched adds ~1% to buying potential (jgpVvH4rTfI [55:02]; QKnBoqLD2Cc [05:55]; 8t92OBM83BA [15:45]). He calls it data; treat as a heuristic, never a benchmark.
- Path: reel → profile → pin → highlight → story → DM → close (Tr2WIG6-1VU [14:32]).
**Ask first:** How many sequences did the founder post in the last 30 days, and which one produced a DM that paid?
**Source:** Tr2WIG6-1VU [14:12–15:17], jgpVvH4rTfI [35:44–57:29], acqxhHB8a5g [26:09], 8t92OBM83BA [10:01–10:41]

### The sequence types (his lists; they vary by video)
**When to use:** planning a week of stories.
**The idea:** Different sequences do different jobs; rotate them and space CTA days with value days.
**The lists (newest first):**
- **Jun 2026, five types:** Q&A, educational, credibility, value-driven, lead magnet (jgpVvH4rTfI [57:50], recap [1:19:39]).
  - Q&A: open a question box, answer ~12 hours later; mix technical and personal (4+4 of 8); "if you don't get enough questions write your own nobody can see the difference" (jgpVvH4rTfI [58:10–1:00:36]).
  - Educational: change how they think with old-way/new-way across ~6 slides; lead with the outcome, not the process (jgpVvH4rTfI [1:00:58–1:03:41]).
  - Credibility: open with a result, show wins across the board, close with a position. "Sharing one win is luck. Three wins is a pattern… 10 wins is just a proven track record" (jgpVvH4rTfI [1:04:02–1:06:08]).
  - Value-driven: scene, tension, framework; "show your level of thinking" (jgpVvH4rTfI [1:06:49]).
  - Lead magnet: CTA on slide one, ends in the email list (jgpVvH4rTfI [1:12:43–1:13:04]).
- **Jan 2026 (with a client):** value → education → transformational; "stories don't convert people. Stories nurture people" (acqxhHB8a5g [25:27–26:30]).
- **May 2025, three types:** curiosity-driven (first touch for ad traffic; little text, pattern interrupt, "DM the word"); engagement-driven (one continuous story, more text: interrupt → claim → pains → angle → proof → trust); credibility-driven (CTAs + social proof; "the one I make the most amount of money from") (8t92OBM83BA [11:01–17:29]).
- **Dec 2024:** credibility sequences (testimonials back to back), value-driven text-heavy sequences, CTA-driven ("DM me the word YouTube") (b5urvngM8wU [07:32–14:05]).
**The numbers:** credibility sequences: ~33% watch them but "67% of the purchases"; curiosity ~80% view but ~20% potential buyers; engagement ~50% (8t92OBM83BA [16:26–20:09], self-reported). Engagement-driven better for B2C, credibility for B2B (8t92OBM83BA [17:08]).
**Mistakes he calls out:** back-to-back CTA days dropped a client's replies (eGWeRi6siNQ [29:44–30:06]); credibility sequences can run with no CTA (eGWeRi6siNQ [37:13]); "people don't look at your content as good as you do" (8t92OBM83BA [14:44]).
**Ask first:** Does the founder have enough client wins for credibility sequences? If not, start with value and Q&A.
**Source:** as listed

### The slide structure (his 6 steps, Jun 2026)
**When to use:** writing one sequence.
**How to run it:**
1. Curiosity: name the obstacle that blocks them (call out pain, not situation: "the situation hits way less hard than the pain does").
2. Statement backed with proof.
3. Belief shift: reframe.
4. Transition: show the result.
5. Trust close "from abundance" (no pressure, no fake scarcity).
**An alternative beat list (Jul 2025, with a client):** market problem → named mechanism → data → niche solution → CTA to a new video → client proof → credibility close (eGWeRi6siNQ [30:06–35:11]).
**The numbers:** the CTA-driven close he used in Dec 2024: "I'm opening 10 spots to work with me personally" plus a keyword CTA ("info") (b5urvngM8wU [06:51–07:12]), with real, not fake, scarcity (b5urvngM8wU [05:10]).
**Source:** jgpVvH4rTfI [1:23:03–1:24:05], eGWeRi6siNQ [30:06–35:11], b5urvngM8wU [04:28–07:12]

### Drop-off rate: the only story metric that matters (his rule)
**When to use:** reviewing story analytics.
**The idea:** Compare views on the first slide with each following slide. Losing people at slide 2 means the hook failed; losing them late means "your body went sideways". Rewrite any slide that loses too much and improve 1% each time.
**How to run it:** log views per slide for every sequence (a VA or the setter can do it), keep a monthly archive, run the numbers through AI or a Miro board, re-angle the worst slides.
**The numbers (they differ by video):**
- Rewrite any slide with drop-off above 5%; his slides ~2.4%, others ~20% per slide / 80% total, his ~25% total, worst 7.5% (eGWeRi6siNQ [35:11–36:33], Jul 2025).
- 5.5% normal, 12.4% flagged, 14–15% sometimes, once 30% → re-angle (8t92OBM83BA [17:29–19:09], May 2025).
- A credibility sequence: 12%, 6%, 5% steps, ~25–30% first-to-last (b5urvngM8wU [10:39–11:00], Dec 2024).
- Example: 20,000 viewers → ~17,000 at the end, "15% drop off rate" (jgpVvH4rTfI [1:13:04]).
- Dashboard of three numbers: drop-off rate, opt-in rate, save rate. "If you can't show me these three numbers, most likely you don't have a system." (jgpVvH4rTfI [1:58:37–1:59:38]).
**Ask first:** Which slide loses the most viewers in the founder's last five sequences?
**Source:** jgpVvH4rTfI [1:20:20–1:23:03], eGWeRi6siNQ [35:11–36:53], 8t92OBM83BA [17:29–19:09]

### Cadence and who makes them (UNNAMED)
**When to use:** DFY production planning.
**The idea:** Story sequences are the most personal asset, so full automation loses the touch; but the team can build structured storylines for the other days. Daily is the target.
**The numbers:** "Minimum three sequences a week", target five to seven, ideally daily (jgpVvH4rTfI [1:43:15], [1:48:48], [1:50:32]); his split ~3×/week personal, team builds the rest (jgpVvH4rTfI [54:37]); daily stories for ~2 years, never missed a week (QKnBoqLD2Cc [17:38]); he spends 1–1.5 hours a day making stories by hand (8t92OBM83BA [19:09], May 2025). Fallback when there's nothing: a lifestyle picture or a Q&A (jgpVvH4rTfI [52:14]). Make something new in the format every ~3 months (8t92OBM83BA [22:13]).
**Ask first:** Which days does the founder record personally, and who builds the structured days?
**Source:** jgpVvH4rTfI [52:14–54:37, 1:43:15–1:50:32], 8t92OBM83BA [19:09–22:56]

### Close Friends boost (his Dec 2024 technique)
**When to use:** story views are low.
**The idea:** Posting the first story of a sequence to Close Friends moves you to the front of followers' story trays; follow it with normal stories, and wait ~12 hours before the next Close Friends post.
**The numbers:** claims story views up 50–100%, sales up 20–30% (b5urvngM8wU [00:00]); one post: 174 views in 3 minutes, 3.6K in 24 hours, 347 profile visits (b5urvngM8wU [02:06–02:27]). By May 2025 he calls Close Friends a short-term launch tactic (~50% more views) (8t92OBM83BA [21:32–22:13]). Newer view: launch tool, not a daily habit.
**Source:** b5urvngM8wU [00:22–02:27], [14:05–15:29]; 8t92OBM83BA [21:32]

### CTA placement (his rule)
**When to use:** deciding where the ask goes.
**The idea:** Decide what you want. Maximum DMs: CTA early. Most qualified DMs: CTA at the end, because whoever acts after slide 7 cared. For lead magnets: offer first, "then pour value into the ones who stayed". Match the CTA to the viewer's state: after a long video, send to DMs, not a 17-question Calendly.
**The numbers:** losing "10 20 $30,000" because of a long booking form after a long video (jgpVvH4rTfI [1:29:45–1:30:27]).
**Source:** jgpVvH4rTfI [1:13:24–1:15:12], [1:29:45]

### Personalised lead magnet → email sequence (UNNAMED; idea credited to "Alexi")
**When to use:** building a list; "what lead magnet?".
**The idea:** Generic PDFs fail. A personalised magnet asks a few questions and routes people by stage. The email sequence "closes what the story sequence opens". Owning the list hedges against a Meta ban.
**How to run it:**
1. Landing page asks 3 questions: current revenue, desired situation, niche; routes to a stage (jgpVvH4rTfI [12:35–12:55]).
2. Title with outcome, specificity, speed: "the exact 12 hooks I used to make $4 million in client revenue. Yours in 30 seconds" (jgpVvH4rTfI [1:19:17]).
3. 8–9 emails over 14 days: deliver → story behind it → client wins (3 emails) → a value/education email → CTA to DM (jgpVvH4rTfI [1:25:28–1:28:40]).
4. Ad/story version (2025): a ManyChat flow to a value asset behind a 3-question form; the next button appears after 75% watch time; a problem-matched testimonial; then Calendly or DM close (17Oc1LRuRKI [03:04–05:31]).
**The numbers:** ~8,000 emails in ~2 months (jgpVvH4rTfI [1:11:20–1:11:41]); "Same content, different hook, 10 times the optins" (jgpVvH4rTfI [1:19:17]); 1,800 leads from a lead-magnet plus story process (b5urvngM8wU [00:43]).
**Mistakes he calls out:** "This is where 90% of the operators die. They capture the email, then they do nothing with it" (jgpVvH4rTfI [1:24:46]).
**Source:** jgpVvH4rTfI [11:11–13:16, 1:11:20–1:31:28], 17Oc1LRuRKI [03:04–05:51]
