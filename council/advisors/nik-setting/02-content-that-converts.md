# 02 — Content that converts (reels, YouTube, messaging, profile)

**When to open this file:** "what should we post?", "views but no sales", "how often?", "what hooks?", "who should we talk to?", "how do we fix the bio?", "the founder freezes on camera", "how do we come up with ideas?".
- Story sequences have their own file (`03`). Ads creative is in `01`.
- His content advice is mostly about intention, clarity and repetition, not production or volume.

---

### Traffic content vs conversion content (UNNAMED; "two jobs")
**When to use:** any content audit; "lots of views, quiet DMs".
**The idea:** Every business needs traffic and conversion, and both are content jobs. Reels and algorithm content get strangers to follow; story sequences, lead magnets, long-form and bottom-of-funnel posts turn followers into buyers. One piece trying to do both does neither. His flagship split hook: two kinds of content, one grows in number, the other grows the business, "and from the outside, they look identical".
**How to run it:**
1. Open the last 10 posts. For each: was it made to get a follower or a buyer? If you can't answer, that is the problem (jgpVvH4rTfI [33:01–34:01]).
2. Map each format to one job: reels = traffic/awareness, Miro or breakdown reels and stories = nurture, long-form YouTube = conversion, client wins = proof.
3. Judge each by its own metric (see `03` dashboard), not by likes.
**The numbers:** clients with 200–300K followers making nothing vs clients with 2–3K followers at $50–100K/month (jgpVvH4rTfI [03:48–04:09]); illustrative decay 10,000 followers → 3,000 know you → 800 know your name → 100 trust you → 18 pay (jgpVvH4rTfI [06:57–07:19], labelled as an example).
**Mistakes he calls out:** optimising for the visible signal: "the algorithm rewards you for the volume. Your bank account doesn't." (jgpVvH4rTfI [30:38]); lifestyle videos raised views while "buyer potential went down" (jgpVvH4rTfI [07:40–08:01]).
**Ask first:** Which posts brought a buyer, not a follower? Who in the DMs this month could actually pay?
**Source:** 4h3lNc-MCbU [00:00], jgpVvH4rTfI [01:00–10:51, 30:38], rsQQeDm2P0Y [01:22–02:05]

### Buyers are invisible (UNNAMED)
**When to use:** "nobody engages with our stories", "should we change the hook because of a comment?".
**The idea:** The people who pay watch silently for weeks, never like, never comment, then show up decided. Engagement comes mostly from people who will never pay. So judge content by pipeline, not applause, and take feedback from paying clients.
**How to run it:** stop reading comments as market research; ask the last five clients "what made you make the decision to work with me?" and make more of that (Tr2WIG6-1VU [15:38]); read DMs and booked-call reasons before Instagram analytics (YOuvbqU8liU [1:00:33]).
**The numbers:** never more than ~5% of an audience buys, 1% is "already very very good" (jgpVvH4rTfI [27:34–29:37]; his model, not a measured benchmark). A buyer decided "weeks ago… never liking, never commenting" (4h3lNc-MCbU [09:42–10:23]).
**Mistakes he calls out:** "Most of the time the people that like your stories are the exact people that will never pay you." (jgpVvH4rTfI [27:14]); rebuilding a hook because of one non-buyer's comment (Cvo9sGBMLZ4 [16:48]).
**Ask first:** Where does buyer feedback come from today?
**Source:** jgpVvH4rTfI [27:14–29:57], Cvo9sGBMLZ4 [15:46–17:50], Tr2WIG6-1VU [14:55–15:17], 4h3lNc-MCbU [09:42]

### One belief shift per post (from his "36 lessons" list)
**When to use:** writing any reel, carousel or YouTube point.
**The idea:** Information is free. What people are stuck on is a belief. Each post takes one belief they hold and shifts it a little. His example: "You think you need more followers, you actually need more trust." Pair it with one idea per post: if you can't say what it's about in one sentence, it's two posts.
**How to run it:** write the old belief → the new belief → one proof or example → stop.
**Mistakes he calls out:** cramming three or four points into one piece because you fear leaving something out (4h3lNc-MCbU [05:51–06:32]).
**Ask first:** What does the viewer believe now that stops them buying?
**Source:** 4h3lNc-MCbU [10:23–11:03], [05:51–06:32]

### Do less, repeat more (UNNAMED)
**When to use:** "should we post more?", "we're bored of our message", "need new angles every week".
**The idea:** He is against posting volume and against new ideas for their own sake. Trust is built by repetition: you'll be sick of the message long before the audience has heard it once. Consistency of the same message beats more posts.
**How to run it:**
1. Pick one main topic with sub-topics; say it "in different ways for the rest of my life" (jgpVvH4rTfI [42:39]).
2. Reuse your own lines across formats (a YouTube line reappears in a story days later; R2tmuzjs_iE [29:30]).
3. Measure over 30+ days; test a format ~5 times before changing it (p_TyO6Jwdks [58:36–59:37], [1:16:15]).
**The numbers:** he has said "the profile funnel" about 2,000 times (jgpVvH4rTfI [14:37]); repetition multiplier about 10x for him, 3x for clients, ~50x for a weekly viewer (jgpVvH4rTfI [15:01], illustrative); 95% of the people you want haven't heard your message once (4h3lNc-MCbU [05:31]); "under 100 reels… under 25 YouTube videos" while passing ~$2.5–3M (MIPEikK6H3s [13:02]; same passage also says "no more than 60 reels").
**Mistakes he calls out:** "post like five times a day" advice (jgpVvH4rTfI [30:17]); a client's 90 new short-form ideas a month = dilution (R2tmuzjs_iE [30:32–31:14]); "The people who pass you don't post more than you. They ship the same thing every single week for two years." (jgpVvH4rTfI [1:58:15]).
**Nuance:** "focus is zero on volume" (MIPEikK6H3s [14:05]) applies to posts. He still posts stories daily and wants 80–100 warm DMs a day.
**Ask first:** What is the one sentence this founder should be known for in 12 months?
**Source:** 4h3lNc-MCbU [05:31, 19:45–20:06], jgpVvH4rTfI [14:37–16:27, 29:57–30:38], R2tmuzjs_iE [28:48–31:14], MIPEikK6H3s [12:41–15:26]

### Document, don't perform ("undocumented content")
**When to use:** founder freezes on camera, scripts sound forced, "we run out of ideas".
**The idea:** Experts can talk for hours but freeze for 60 seconds on camera because they're performing. Film the work instead: client calls, coaching someone on camera, team meetings, a day with a client. Clip short-form from long recordings. He calls most ideation "a scam" for people who know their subject.
**How to run it:**
1. Involve someone else on camera (client, partner, team member) and genuinely help them; the viewer sits in their seat and feels coached (R2tmuzjs_iE [08:02–11:05], [24:15–24:57]).
2. Roll long: the first minute is always bad; after ~2 minutes the camera disappears (R2tmuzjs_iE [12:48–13:48]).
3. One hour of filming gives many shorts and one long (MIPEikK6H3s [17:51]); a day with a client gives a long-form video, clips and a story sequence (eJItxb8Hs-E [22:20–23:20]).
4. Wait 1–2 days before editing; separate creating from judging (p_TyO6Jwdks [1:10:20]; 4h3lNc-MCbU [13:09–13:50]).
**The numbers:** a client spent ~8 hours a day thinking about content for ~3 shorts and maybe a YouTube video (R2tmuzjs_iE [04:50–05:10]).
**Mistakes he calls out:** "you need to stop being a performer and be a document" (R2tmuzjs_iE [14:08]); "Everyone can write a script and get a teleprompter" (R2tmuzjs_iE [14:29]); batch-recording ten reels on a Friday, which he says is the one thing he disagrees with "Alexi" on (MIPEikK6H3s [14:25]).
**Tension:** for most people, "90% of the people creating content should prepare" until they're seen as credible; improvising comes later (0I_cEtloYb4 [57:39–59:45]). Reconcile: prepare the structure and the first 30 seconds, document the rest.
**Ask first:** What real conversations does the founder already have every week that could be filmed?
**Source:** R2tmuzjs_iE [08:02–16:52], MIPEikK6H3s [04:08–08:53], 4h3lNc-MCbU [02:03–04:49], p_TyO6Jwdks [45:09–48:13]

### Talk up market: the cap is your highest case study (UNNAMED)
**When to use:** "we attract broke leads", "how do we sell bigger deals?", stuck around $100K/month.
**The idea:** Most founders make content for the person they used to be. Talking to the highest tier also attracts mid and low; talking low attracts only low. But you can only claim up to your best real case study.
**How to run it:**
1. List avatars 1, 2, 3; speak verbally and visually to the highest paying one (0I_cEtloYb4 [16:39–18:22]).
2. Interview the top client for 20 minutes: pains when they started, values, why they moved fast (0I_cEtloYb4 [20:07–20:49]).
3. Sort the last 20 DMs: who could pay $50K, $5K, nothing? That ratio is your ceiling (Tr2WIG6-1VU [05:29–05:50]).
4. Rewrite three hooks for the person one level up: "how to get your first client" → "why your business still depends on closing" (Tr2WIG6-1VU [04:48–05:29]).
**The numbers:** "over 75 to 80 people" his team took to $100K/month hit this constraint (0I_cEtloYb4 [17:19]).
**Mistakes he calls out:** claiming a higher tier than your proof ("It's not better if you don't have the proof for it", R2tmuzjs_iE [1:10:33]); messaging to the uncertain middle (R2tmuzjs_iE [40:25–41:07]).
**Ask first:** Who is the single best client result this founder can show, and at what revenue level was that client?
**Source:** 0I_cEtloYb4 [14:12–21:32], Tr2WIG6-1VU [02:03–05:50], R2tmuzjs_iE [33:46–54:14]

### Numbers in messaging get divided by three (UNNAMED)
**When to use:** writing bios, offers and headlines with revenue figures.
**The idea:** Any money figure in your messaging attracts people at about a third of it. So use numbers carefully in positioning, and fix messaging through the standard you set or the pain, not finance. Numbers do still work in titles for packaging and reach.
**The numbers:** "$10 million brand game plan" attracted people at $1–3M; "100 grand a month" attracted people at $30–50K (jgpVvH4rTfI [40:52–41:35]). "90% of the people… try to solve it with a finance element" (R2tmuzjs_iE [43:15–44:17]).
**How to run it:** three ways to fix positioning: (1) finance number, (2) the standard ("you already do good at sales"), (3) a pain point; default to 2 and 3 (R2tmuzjs_iE [43:15]). Use a revenue number above the ICP's current level if you use one at all (MyeU41kG1SY [06:28–08:30]).
**Mistakes he calls out:** "if you're making 10K per month or below, you attract brokies" (MyeU41kG1SY [06:28]); jargon your market doesn't share ("info business") (jgpVvH4rTfI [40:11]).
**Ask first:** What number is in the bio, and who would read themselves into it?
**Source:** jgpVvH4rTfI [39:50–41:35], R2tmuzjs_iE [43:15–44:17, 1:06:40], MyeU41kG1SY [06:07–08:30]

### Who you show is 80% of messaging (UNNAMED)
**When to use:** casting for content, choosing which clients appear, mastermind footage.
**The idea:** The people on camera next to you tell the market who this is for, more than hooks and headlines do. Show the people you want to attract, chosen for spark, not just income. To sell group or community, show groups ("Show 10 people in one shot").
**The numbers:** "This is 80% of everything" compared with hooks and headlines (R2tmuzjs_iE [48:27–50:49]).
**Mistakes he calls out:** putting struggling people on camera whom no one wants to "be"; a founder brand that makes the founder the only vehicle, which blocks moving from 1:1 to group (jgpVvH4rTfI [46:05–47:07]).
**Ask first:** Who appeared in the founder's last 10 posts?
**Source:** R2tmuzjs_iE [48:27–50:49], jgpVvH4rTfI [46:05–48:09], CAxLCrjz5Ss [14:17–15:39]

### Same message, simpler language: analogy for reach (UNNAMED)
**When to use:** need more views without diluting the audience; stuck at $100–250K/month.
**The idea:** To grow reach, don't change topics (the audience's problems haven't changed). Explain the same topic through an analogy so more people understand it.
**The numbers:** an analogy version of a reel got 132,000 views vs 8,000 for the non-analogy version on trial reels (0I_cEtloYb4 [26:37–28:43]).
**Ask first:** What everyday thing is this idea like?
**Source:** 0I_cEtloYb4 [23:53–28:43]

### Pond, lake, ocean (his phrase)
**When to use:** "should we go broader?", "we've run out of people to sell to".
**The idea:** The pond, lake or ocean is the maximum audience your messaging can reach. Win the pond first; going niche to broad is easier than broad to niche. Most people think they're in a lake but haven't captured 5% of their pond.
**How to run it:** move up only when you're the clear leader in the niche, have made real money in the craft, and are known as the best at something specific; expect a dip and an ego hit.
**The numbers:** he moved up at "end of 2025" and took about one month of lower revenue (0I_cEtloYb4 [34:10–35:32]). TAM check: 500,000 people × 1% over five years × $9,000 = "$45 million" before switching niche (RkKTKPdOK40 [18:58–19:18]).
**Mistakes he calls out:** "I've run out of people to sell to" usually means the wrong message (R2tmuzjs_iE [1:08:46]); starting a new account because it "mentally feels like" a new chapter (RkKTKPdOK40 [11:22]).
**Source:** 0I_cEtloYb4 [32:46–39:43], R2tmuzjs_iE [1:07:42–1:09:29], RkKTKPdOK40 [18:58–20:18]

### Profile as landing page: bio, pin, highlights, grid
**When to use:** profile audit.
**The idea:** The profile decides the first phase visually. Credibility, clarity, CTA. One outcome, one CTA, one link.
**How to run it:**
1. Bio with "three C's": credibility (one line), clarity (what you help with), CTA (where to go) (p_TyO6Jwdks [08:53–09:33], team explanation). Example: "DM me info if you want to help build and scale a 50 to 250k a month business" (MyeU41kG1SY [37:01]).
2. Face picture, no Lamborghini for B2B (MyeU41kG1SY [02:41]); B2C is the exception where lifestyle sells (_qpdvJz793E [24:56]).
3. Pinned carousel telling your story; one pinned post credibility-driven (Cvo9sGBMLZ4 [19:31]; eJItxb8Hs-E [22:00]).
4. Three highlight topics: your story, client results, authority (p_TyO6Jwdks [50:58–52:40]); a text-structured "Start Here" (MyeU41kG1SY [05:04]).
5. Link in bio to YouTube; YouTube links back (MyeU41kG1SY [34:17]; _qpdvJz793E [21:54]).
6. Reel grid that repeats a visible pattern of formats (p_TyO6Jwdks [53:44]).
**The numbers:** ~150 bio-link clicks a day from ~6K story viewers → ~2 calls a day (eGWeRi6siNQ [37:34–38:34]); "300 people per day clicking" → "at least… 50 book calls" a month (MyeU41kG1SY [34:17], illustrative).
**Mistakes he calls out:** writing "with proof" instead of showing it (MyeU41kG1SY [36:21]); random highlights that confuse; changing the bio daily: optimise after 30 days of data (team view, p_TyO6Jwdks [1:13:26]).
**Ask first:** In five seconds on the profile, can a stranger say who it's for and what to DM?
**Source:** p_TyO6Jwdks [08:53–09:33, 49:37–54:45], MyeU41kG1SY [02:01–06:07, 34:17–39:26], t6jzUsmeUTU [58:41]

### YouTube as the conversion asset (UNNAMED)
**When to use:** "do we need YouTube?", "what long-form should we make?".
**The idea:** "every reel is a question" and "The long form is the answer to it". Reels can't build enough trust in a minute; YouTube lets buyers see how you think. Judge YouTube by whether the title and thumbnail set the right expectation and whether the right people watch, not views.
**How to run it:**
1. Formats: breakdowns (live diagnosis of a client), case-study walk-throughs, story videos, client interviews, full courses, vlogs (QKnBoqLD2Cc [25:27–26:10]; RkKTKPdOK40 [55:33]).
2. Titles: market topics, not your offer; a number in the title "always works" for packaging (R2tmuzjs_iE [1:06:40]); niche the title so only the buyer can act on it ("sign $50,000 clients even if $10,000 clients feel out of range", R2tmuzjs_iE [56:19–58:26]).
3. Structure: script only the first 30 seconds (R2tmuzjs_iE [54:34–55:36]); 3/5/7/10 steps, 5 by default, each step depending on the one before (R2tmuzjs_iE [1:15:29–1:17:12]).
4. Check every minute: "is the next minute adding something to the prior minute" (YOuvbqU8liU [1:22:18]).
5. Repurpose: "every 20 minute YouTube video will give you three very strong ideas" for reels, carousels and stories (R2tmuzjs_iE [27:45–28:05]).
6. Cadence formula: value, value, credibility, value, value, credibility (t6jzUsmeUTU [50:05]).
**The numbers:** niche YouTube views of 5–16K count as high (MyeU41kG1SY [33:15]); a client's channel at ~700 views took her from $8K to $75K/month (Tr2WIG6-1VU [13:52–14:12]); 161 reels and 161 YouTube videos in ~3 years (jgpVvH4rTfI [1:51:34]).
**Mistakes he calls out:** without YouTube "you've a massive problem" (Cvo9sGBMLZ4 [21:16]); videos about your offer instead of what people care about (t6jzUsmeUTU [16:35]).
**Ask first:** Which video would a setter send to this exact pain?
**Source:** jgpVvH4rTfI [1:32:49–1:36:39], R2tmuzjs_iE [54:34–1:25:08], njWXZNfCm28 [04:50–05:11, 20:18]

### Hooks and sharpness (from his lessons list and ad videos)
**When to use:** "our content is flat".
**The idea:** Write for one real person, like a voice note. Boring is the only real mistake; say the thing everyone in the space thinks but won't post. Pain over persona. Faces beat graphics. Lower production, raise clarity.
**How to run it:** pick one real founder with one real problem (4h3lNc-MCbU [00:41–01:22]); niche until it scares you (4h3lNc-MCbU [05:09]); one honest client conversation gives better hooks than a month of a content calendar (4h3lNc-MCbU [09:21]); "a confused mind always always says no" (4h3lNc-MCbU [14:53]).
**Mistakes he calls out:** persona-plus-promise hooks (are you X, want Y in Z time) are "outdated" (xcpy678eggY [05:50]); polished production that looks like an ad gets skipped (4h3lNc-MCbU [15:35]); trends are "rented attention" (4h3lNc-MCbU [16:15]).
**Source:** 4h3lNc-MCbU [00:41–16:36], X7pn2j2tK9M [13:24–15:29], jPGZs6U6CDE [05:30–06:31]

### Case-study and breakdown content as acquisition (UNNAMED)
**When to use:** "what's our best-converting content?".
**The idea:** The best acquisition asset is a video walking through how you took someone from A to B, or a live breakdown of a real business. Indirect testimonials (a filmed session) beat a quick Zoom testimonial.
**How to run it:** film live diagnoses; package each client result into a reel, a story sequence and a long-form video (jgpVvH4rTfI [1:36:59]); proof posts carry name, start point, end point, time taken, one line in their words (Tr2WIG6-1VU [13:31–13:52]).
**The numbers:** testimonial cadence one every other day mirrored to a story slide, ~15 a month (X7pn2j2tK9M [37:47–38:27]).
**Mistakes he calls out:** "A screenshot alone is just noise. A screenshot with the story is proof." (jgpVvH4rTfI [1:41:53]); marketing your record month instead of your consistent number (RkKTKPdOK40 [56:14–56:54]).
**Source:** RkKTKPdOK40 [55:33–56:54], eJItxb8Hs-E [22:20–23:20], jgpVvH4rTfI [1:40:08–1:42:54]
