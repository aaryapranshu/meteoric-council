## The Closing Frame: Power, Blame and the Money in the Maybes
**Use when:** founder thinks closing is "pushy", or loses most prospects who seemed interested; any question about sales philosophy or ethics.
**Core idea:** Advertising splits people into Yes (lay-downs), No (unqualified) and Maybe (on the fence). Most interested people are Maybes. They aren't saying no — they're avoiding a decision because they've handed their decision-making power to something they blame. Closing hands that power back so they make a *genuine* decision, yes or no.
**How to run it:**
1. Treat "No" and "I'm avoiding deciding" as different outcomes. Only the first is a real no.
2. Do three things on every call: (a) understand what they want; (b) put what they want next to their options for getting it; (c) make sure they reach a real decision.
3. Feel fine about a genuine no. Feel bad about letting someone leave on the "avoidance treadmill" — nobody benefits.
4. Ethics test before using any close: the product will get them to their goal the way they want; you and the team will support them; it will work for *them*, not just others.
**Numbers & thresholds:** Hormozi says effective closing routinely lets sales teams double close rates (Closing playbook). Treat as his claim, not a benchmark.
**Worked example:** Jan 2017 Gym Launch launch: reps were losing to "need to talk to my husband", "need to think about it" and "too expensive". He drilled one objection per huddle (stalls first, then price) and the month went from losing money to break-even (from source).
**Mistakes he calls out:**
- Lumping Maybes into the No bucket — "profit-destroying".
- Believing certain prices "can't" sell without X — it's a smaller pool, not impossible.
- Selling unqualified people: that's an advertising problem, not a sales win.
> "The difference between help and manipulation is intention." — $100M Playbook: Closing
**Ask the founder:** What % of calls end in "let me think about it" vs a clear yes/no? Do you know your top 3 objections by frequency? Are bad-fit people reaching your calendar?

## Rules of Closing (compact)
**Use when:** coaching a rep or founder on call behaviour; reviewing a recorded call.

| Rule | What it means in practice |
|---|---|
| Repeat back what they said | Buys thinking time and proves you listened. |
| Acknowledge or agree, never disagree | Open every response with "totally understand / great point / thanks for bringing that up", then reframe. Disagreement creates hostility. |
| Get permission before getting real | "Can I be a coach instead of a friend for a second?" then deliver the hard truth. |
| Stack closes | If one angle misses, come back with another analogy. |
| Understand, don't memorize | Know the logic so you can say it in your own words. You only need 2–3 closes per obstacle. |
| Use nudges at the edge | One-liners for someone right on the line (see tables below). |
| "Yellows are golds" | Practise on less-qualified leads — people lie on applications, the slot is booked anyway, and reps get reps. |
| Volume negates luck | Do so many calls it would be unreasonable not to be great. |
| Objections ≠ no | "It's expensive" is often an observation. Say "I agree" and let them process aloud. Price shock isn't a no. |
| Never change price on the spot | Discounting to close moves you from closing to haggling and invites customers to bully you. Add value instead (see `03-money-models.md`). |
| Only ask when you think you've got them | If not, keep probing. |
| Defuse before the ask | Problems are easier to handle before money is mentioned. |
| Expect no | Plan for 3–4 "reasons" before a buy; swapping objections means you're closing correctly. |
| Prospect > sale | Whoever cares most about the prospect wins. Kind, not nice. |
| Insult a straw man, not them | Tell the hard truth about "someone like you" or your past self. |
| Once they say yes, stop talking | Everything else risks unselling. |
| Record every call | Watch game tape; fix one thing at a time. |

> "Selling happens before you ask for the sale. Closing happens after." — $100M Playbook: Closing
**Sources:** $100M Playbook: Closing (My Rules of Closing)

## Pre-Call Setup: What Happens Before the Call Decides the Close
**Use when:** close rate is low, prospects show up cold or skeptical, decision makers are missing, no-shows are high, or "every call is a fight".
**Core idea:** Selling starts before the call. The setter (or the booking flow) qualifies, pre-frames and builds proof; the closer inherits that frame. If they don't show, they can't buy.
**How to run it:**
1. **Qualify.** Check STAR — Situation (problem to solve), Timing, Authority, Resources (money). In a 2025 video he uses IBM's BANT (Budget, Authority, Need, Timing) for the same job. Ask on the application *and* again on the call before price. Cancel or reroute clearly bad fits.
2. **Get decision makers on the call.** Make it a booking condition. He often reschedules if all decision makers won't attend.
3. **Edify the closer and the business.** Setter frames the closer as the expert who's helped people "just like you", then books them live ("let me see if I can get you in... he confirmed, 4:00 works"). Sounds very different from "someone will reach out".
4. **Speed and proximity.** Contact within 5 minutes of opt-in; book same day / next day / day after; pull appointments forward when a slot opens; same-day shows best.
5. **Remind like a human.** Automated reminders 24h, 12h, 3h out (include name, date, time, the number you'll call from); manual reminders night before, morning of, 60 min prior.
6. **Send proof before they show.** Case studies matched to the prospect's situation and demographics, between booking and call, and between call 1 and call 2.
7. **Incentivize showing up.** Push (a small gift sent regardless, e.g. a coffee gift card — reciprocity) or pull (an A/B choice they get when they arrive: "shirt or shorts?").
8. **Prep.** Rule of thumb: ~10% of call length in research for repeat calls (6 min for a 60-min call). Read prior notes; open with something specific about them.
9. **Kill zombies up front.** Anything you hear early that will become an objection (spouse, time, special circumstance) — address it before price. Before price it's an *obstacle*; after price it's an *objection* and the clock is ticking.
10. **BAMFAM.** Book a meeting from a meeting; never end a call without the next one on the calendar.
**Numbers & thresholds:** Lead Nurture playbook (ALAN data, 4,000+ appointments/day): more available slots, fewer days out, more follow-ups and more lead replies each correlate with higher show rates. Availability (total time slots) was the strongest predictor of total sales in his analysis (YT 2024). He says a 20% lift in schedule rate is worth as much as a 20% lift in close rate (YT 2024).
**Worked example:** Solar portfolio company: one rep called every new booking immediately to qualify — if they had time, he closed on the spot; if not, he pulled a later-week slot into today. He sold ~4x the next rep; it was rolled out company-wide (from source, YT 2024).
**Mistakes he calls out:**
- Hiding availability to "look busy" — it only lowers convenience and show rates.
- No "off-the-call SOP": reps idle between calls instead of pulling up, confirming, re-booking.
- Letting the setter book loosely ("someone will call you").
- Asking for the sale without having uncovered why they're here.
**Ask the founder:** What's your schedule rate, show rate and close rate separately? How many days out do people book? Are decision makers on the call? What does the prospect see between booking and the call?

## CLOSER Framework
**Use when:** founder needs a call structure, a script, or wants to know why calls drift; "how do I run a sales call?"
**Core idea:** Six milestones, phrased as questions wherever possible. Wording varies; hitting the milestones in order is what closes. Works B2B and B2C, low and high ticket, per Hormozi.
**How to run it (with phrasing structures — adapt, don't recite):**
1. **C — Clarify why they're here.** Find the trigger and goal.
   - "What made you reach out / book / reply today?" → "What's the goal?" → "Why is that important to you right now?"
   - If they say "just want info": "Totally — what were you hoping the info would solve?"
2. **L — Label them with a problem.** Restate it in their words and get them to own it.
   - "So what I'm hearing is [problem], and it's costing you [consequence] — is that right?" They must say yes. He frames it as: you can't fix it until they admit it.
3. **O — Overview past experiences (the pain cycle).** Walk every previous attempt.
   - "What have you tried so far?" → "How long? How long ago? How did that go?" → "What else?" Repeat until options are exhausted.
   - Then: "It's not your fault — you had [part A] but were missing [part B]." This builds ammunition for the close and raises short-term motivation by expanding the gap between where they are and what they want.
4. **S — Sell the vacation, not the flight.** Pitch the destination, not the process.
   - "Can I share how I think we might solve this?" → "There are three things that make clients successful: [pillar 1], [pillar 2], [pillar 3]."
   - Each pillar gets one short story/metaphor that breaks a belief, not features, modules or jargon. Keep the pitch to ~3 minutes max.
   - If you have tiers, they're the same destination at different levels of service (swim / boat / commercial flight / private jet).
5. **E — Explain away concerns.** Ask for the sale; if anything but yes, use the closes below. He lists five concern types: timing, money, decision maker, stall, preferences.
6. **R — Reinforce the decision.** Post-purchase is where cold feet happen. Founder/rep welcome video by name, what happens next and who they meet, a gift or handwritten card, a fast first win. He added R years later because the sale continues through onboarding and check-ins.
**Numbers & thresholds:** Pitch ≤ ~3 minutes; three pillars (YT 2021). Cold-feet window: "24 hours post purchase" (LinkedIn, 2025, via research notes) vs "first 48 hours after the sale" (YT 2021) — treat the first 1–2 days as critical.
**Worked example:** Fitness pitch (from source): pillars = workouts, nutrition, accountability. Accountability is sold with a story: as a kid your parents made you brush your teeth; now you do it yourself — external accountability became an internal habit. Nothing about call schedules or app features.
**Mistakes he calls out:**
- Eight minutes in and the rep still doesn't know the prospect's revenue, clients or biggest pain — but has bragged about the company.
- Training reps on the product instead of the prospect.
- Skipping the pain ("sit in the pain"): if they haven't clarified why they need a solution, don't ask for the sale.
- Long pitches; technobabble that sounds like work.
> "there's only three things that ever come out of your mouth questions restatements and anecdotal stories" — YT "I Taught 116+ Salesmen My Closing Framework" (2021)
**Ask the founder:** What are the three pillars your best clients have that failed attempts lacked? What's the one story you'd tell for each? How long is your pitch today? What happens in the first 24–48 hours after someone pays?

## AAA Reframe: Acknowledge → Associate → Ask
**Use when:** a prospect asks a trap question ("how many certifications do your trainers have?"), throws any non-yes, or the rep sounds combative firing back overcomes.
**Core idea:** A reframe is the 1–3 sentences said after anything but yes, so the next ask keeps rapport. Top closers ask for the sale more times than anyone; reframing is what lets them ask again without being hated. The person asking questions controls the call.
**How to run it:**
1. **Acknowledge:** restate what they said ("So you're curious about our certifications..."). Shows listening, buys time.
2. **Associate:** link their question to the behaviour of your best customers ("Great question — our most successful clients ask exactly that; it means you're taking this seriously").
3. **Ask:** a question about their question ("Which certifications are you looking for specifically?" / "What are you hoping that solves?").
4. Answer only when you know the answer that fits what they want (lawyer rule: don't ask what you don't know the answer to).
5. Tough truths go through a **straw man**: (a) "Someone asked me that earlier today — can I share what I told them?"; (b) a past customer just like them ("You're just like Sarah, who..."); (c) appeal to an authority ("Our CEO told me something earlier today...").
6. Bank the labels you give ("you're clearly a family man") and bring them back in the close.
**Mistakes he calls out:**
- "Do you have any questions?" — invites objections and hands them the wheel.
- Answering blind questions: the prospect becomes judge, jury and executioner of your answer.
- Arguing: you never win a sale by winning an argument. "Be like smoke."
- Young reps using "coach hat" lines on older B2B owners — use straw men instead.
> "the person asking the questions is the one who's in control" — YT "After Closing 4000+ Sales, I Discovered a New Method..." (2024)
**Ask the founder:** Which questions do prospects ask that your reps answer directly? Do reps preface overcomes with acknowledgement?

## Onion of Blame: The Objection Map
**Use when:** classifying any objection; training reps to expect multiple objections; choosing which close to use.
**Core idea:** Every objection is blame cast outward to avoid deciding. Three layers, outer to inner: **Circumstances → Other People → Self**. People peel through layers; when the objection changes, you're getting closer to a buyer in power. Self-blame ("I need to think about it") is the layer closest to a sale.
**How to run it:**
1. Identify the layer and sub-type:

| Layer | Sub-type | Sounds like | Real issue |
|---|---|---|---|
| Circumstances | Time | "Too busy", "I'll start when I have time" | Priorities, not timing |
| Circumstances | Money | "Too expensive", "can't afford", "cheaper elsewhere", "can you do less?" | Usually value, rarely true inability |
| Circumstances | Fit | "Not right for me", "I don't like this detail" | Often a preference in disguise |
| Other people | Authority | "Need to talk to my spouse/partner" | Needs support, not permission |
| Other people | Trust | "I've been burned before", "what makes you different?" | Distrust from past experience |
| Self | Fear | "Works for them, not for me" | Fear of repeating mistakes |
| Self | Avoidance / stall | "Need to think about it", "send me a brochure" | Doesn't know how to decide |

2. Map CLOSER's five concerns onto it: timing = Circumstances/Time; money = Circumstances/Money; decision maker = Other People/Authority; preferences = Self ("results your way"); stall = Self/Avoidance.
3. Start with an all-purpose close to find the real concern; then use a layer-specific close.
4. Expect 3–4 reasons before a buy. Isolate, resolve, ask again.
**Mistakes he calls out:**
- Treating the first objection as final.
- Disagreeing with the blame instead of reframing it.
**Ask the founder:** Which layer do most of your lost deals stall in? Do reps hear the same objection after they've "handled" it (sign the close didn't land)?

## All-Purpose Closes (the 80/20 set)
**Use when:** any response that isn't yes, especially early; when you don't yet know the real concern.

| Close | Structure (in our words) | Use it for |
|---|---|---|
| What's Your Main Concern | "What's your main concern?" → if vague: "What are you afraid of happening?" → if still vague, name an absurd worst case ("...that I'll take your money to Vegas?"). They laugh, then tell the truth. | His most-used line. First response to any stall. |
| Reason | "The reason you're giving for not doing it is the reason you need to." No money → that's why. No time → that's why. Dependent on spouse → that's why you should own this. | Any blame, once rapport is high. |
| Hypothetical | "If this were perfect, would you do it? What's the gap between perfect and this?" Small detail → fix it and close. Nothing specific → "So it's not the program — what are you afraid of?" | Vague hesitation. He says it works "astonishingly well". |
| Zoom Out | "You want X. We sell X. Could over-analysing details be why it hasn't happened yet? The only question is whether this makes your goal more likely than going alone." | Detail-obsessed prospects. |
| 1 to 10 | "1 = not interested, 10 = ready now. Where are you?" 10 → close. ≤9 → "What would get you to a 10?" → solve → "Feeling 10-ish now?" | Gauging and isolating. |
| Best Case / Worst Case | Lay out the options: do it and win; do it and (with a guarantee) lose nothing; or walk out — the only option guaranteed not to get the result. No-guarantee version: "Take a risk and get closer no matter what, or take no risk and guarantee you don't get it." | His most-closed close, per a 2024 video. |
| Card Not On Me | Prevent it: on verbal yes, ask for ID first (wallet comes out), fill the contract, then "I'll trade you for the card you want to use." If it still happens: "No problem — pull up your banking app" and take ACH. | "Forgot my wallet". |

**Mistakes he calls out:** using these without the "totally understand" acknowledgement first; asking "what are you afraid of" before rapport exists.
**Sources:** $100M Playbook: Closing (All-Purpose Closes; Author Note: ACH) · YT "Sales Was Hard Until I Understood These 9 Concepts" (2024) · YT "After Closing 4000+ Sales..." (2024)

## Circumstance Closes: Time
**Use when:** "I'm too busy", "not the right time", "I'll start when things calm down".
**Core idea:** People always make time for what matters; busy seasons recur. Show them where their hours go and that starting while busy is the best test of permanence — especially if the offer saves time.

| Close | Structure | Nudge |
|---|---|---|
| Better To Start When You're Busy | Busy seasons will always come; if it can't work while busy, it won't last — and you want it to last. | "This is built to give you time back, not cost you time." |
| You're Gonna Get Busy Again | Even if you wait for a quiet period, you'll stop the moment it gets busy. Learn it while busy and it sticks. | "First thing we do is stop the stuff that isn't working." |
| Not Timing, Priorities | "What are you doing that's more important than this?" Either something drops in priority or the goal isn't important — and that's OK, but decide. | — |
| Smartphone | Story (his wife checked his screen time: ~20 hrs/week social). Everyone gets the same 24 hours; has your allocation got you where you want? | "We'll cut 90% of what isn't working to make room." |
| When/Then | "I'll do it when I have time" flips cause and effect, like "I'll see a doctor when I'm healthy." | — |

**B2B adaptation (illustrative):** "Q4 is crazy" → "Which quarter isn't? If the system only works when it's quiet, it's not a system. Implementing while you're stretched is exactly when it pays back."
**Pre-empt it:** ask early "How much time per week can you give this?" Then if time comes up later: "What changed between then and now?" (YT 2024).
**Sources:** $100M Playbook: Closing (Blame Time) · YT "Sales Was Hard Until I Understood These 9 Concepts" (2024)

## Circumstance Closes: Money (four flavours)
**Use when:** any price, budget, competitor-price or discount objection.
**Core idea:** Translate "price too high" into "value too low". Put the pain of now beside the life after purchase. Four flavours need different responses. Qualify for Resources up front — a truly unqualified buyer is an advertising problem.

| Flavour | Sounds like | Closes (structure) |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Not enough value | "It's too much" | **It's Good That It's A Lot** — a big investment means you'll take it seriously; cheap things get ignored; the real risk is leaving without the result. **Good Things Aren't Cheap** — "How many free PDFs have you downloaded and made no more money?" We stacked the odds instead of cutting costs. **Would You Believe Me If It Were Less?** — "If it were a third of the price, would you even believe it could do this?" **It's Not A Lot When You Think About It** — "If it got you +$10k/mo, worth it? Then it's not price, it's belief" → show proof ("here's 200 like you"). **If All It Did Was X** — "If it only did X, would it be worth it?" **Not What You Make But What It's Worth** — future earning, not today's income. **Spend It Either Way** — over 12 months the money gets spent regardless; decide where. **Pay The Price Either Way** — with years of your time or money today; buy time back at a discount. **Some Now Or More Later** — it only gets more expensive the longer you wait. **Future Favor** — "If 10 years ago you could have made this decision, would it be worth it? Do future-you a favour." |
| 2. Can't afford | "Not in my budget" | **Resourcefulness, Not Resources** — self-made people started with nothing; the difference was resourcefulness (Phil Knight asking vendors to fund payroll). **Had It Worse And Done Better** — "Has anyone in your situation achieved this? What's the difference between them and you?" **Everyone Starts At Zero.** Nudge: "If you needed life-saving surgery, would you find the money?" |
| 3. Cheaper elsewhere | "XYZ does it for less" | **Cheap Comparison** — "If we were the same price, who would you pick?" They list why you're better. "Exactly — that's why we're not the same price." Laugh, ask again. **Good Fast Cheap** — pick two; the most expensive option is buying cheap, regretting it, then hiring you late. **Cheap Or What You Need.** |
| 4. Haggling | "Can you do it for less?" | **We Could Do It For More** — "...we could do it for more (ha)" or quote a higher number. He says this works "insanely well". |
| Bonus: "It's not the money" | "It's not the money, I just don't think I'd use it" | **Not, Not The Money** — "If it were free, would you do it?" Yes → it is about money; lock in now, push the start date if needed. |

**Mistakes he calls out:**
- Offering a discount: the moment you say you'll do it for less, you give up every appeal to value.
- Not noticing that once *they* say "I'd do it for less", all non-price objections are gone — focus there.
- Trying to sell something far beyond someone's means.
**B2B note:** businesses are ROI-sensitive, not price-sensitive (Hormozi X post, via research notes) — anchor money closes on payback ("If this only recovered X hours a week, would it be worth it?").

## Other-People Closes: Decision Makers and "Burned Before"
**Use when:** "I need to talk to my spouse / partner / board / employees"; "I've been burned before".
**Core idea:** Prevent first — require all decision makers on the call. If it still comes up, they need *support, not permission*. Rely on past agreements: the partner already disapproves of the current struggle.
**How to run it (decision-maker sequence — they can say yes at any step; when they do, close):**
1. "What if they say no?" → About one in three say "I'd do it anyway" → "Great, if you're doing it either way, let's start."
2. "What part do you think they'd object to — the price, the program, or me?" → off-ramp to that obstacle.
3. Past agreements: "Do they approve of the situation you're in now? Then why would they stop you fixing it?" Also: "Have you bought things like this before? Did they support you?"
4. Role reversal: "If they needed this for their dream, would you support them? Then why not you?"
5. Support, not permission: "It's your life. If you hand them the decision and in two years you're [worse], you'll resent them — not fair to either of you. Explain it to them; ask for support."
6. Delayed close: sign now, delay payment (24–72 hrs) or use a cooling-off guarantee; "If they truly object, call me — I'll talk to them, then tear up the contract."
7. If rapport allows: a humorous hypothetical extreme of the partner forbidding the result; "Sometimes it's better to ask forgiveness than permission."
**Burned before — "Don't Let It Burn You Twice":** a bad past purchase burned you once; letting it stop a good decision burns you twice. There are good and bad providers in every category.
**Numbers & thresholds:** "1 out of 3" said they'd do it anyway (Closing playbook; repeated in YT 2024). He calls this sequence best for cheaper purchases relative to buying power. For large B2B multi-stakeholder deals, bring stakeholders in rather than force it (YT 2024).
**Mistakes he calls out:** forcing a life-changing household decision without partner buy-in; booking calls without decision makers.
**Legal flag:** the "three-day no-sweat" cancellation is framed as required in most US states — check local cooling-off law.
**Ask the founder:** Does your booking flow ask "who else is involved in this decision?" Who attends? How often does "talk to my partner" appear in lost deals?

## Self Closes: Preferences ("I want your result, my way")
**Use when:** "This isn't for me", "Can you change this part?", "I don't like [specific element]", or a very specific question you can't answer pre-sale.
**Core idea:** Their way of doing things probably produced their current problem. They either give up the preference or the result; help them pick which matters more.

| Close | Structure |
|---|---|
| Isolate And Solve (always first) | "What specifically would make it right for you vs not?" Fix real small issues. |
| We Vote With Our Dollars | Spending shows priorities; a new identity needs new priorities (his Sephora story; B2B version: people who want to be rich invest in skills). |
| Pain Of Change < Pain Of Staying | We change when staying the same hurts more. "If you prefer now over change, you're ordering more of what you've got — is that what you want?" |
| You Gotta Change To Change | What you've been doing got you what you have. Nudge: "We know it works this way; change the variables, change the outcome." |
| Change The Variables, Change The Outcome | "We could change that — but you'd likely get a different outcome, and I wouldn't feel ethical offering that." |
| Mechanic / Surgeon's Secretary | Unanswerable specific question: "Like asking the scheduler what's wrong with your car before anyone looks under the hood. Anyone who answers that before assessing you — run." |

**Sources:** $100M Playbook: Closing (Blame Their Own Preferences) · YT "After Closing 4000+ Sales..." (2024)

## Self Closes: Stalls and Rushed Decisions
**Use when:** "Let me think about it", "This is fast", "Send me an email", "I'll call you tomorrow", "I'm not sure".
**Core idea:** People avoid decisions because they fear mistakes. Show that not deciding is the biggest mistake — it guarantees they stay where they are. Decisions take information, not time.

| Close | Structure |
|---|---|
| What Would Make It A No? | Instead of pushing for yes, ask what makes it a no. Weak reasons reveal plain uncertainty — like every great customer before them. |
| Don't Repeat The Same Mistake | "No pressure — but could delaying this decision be how you ended up here?" |
| Cost Of Not Deciding | "The question isn't what it costs, it's what not deciding has cost you so far." People regret what they didn't do. |
| Another Year Of Almost | Almost hitting the number, almost hiring, almost... "What about a 'this is my year' year?" |
| Information, Not Time | "You won't sit in a rocking chair and think about it — life will take over and the pain will decide for you in a few days. Let's decide now with the information; I'm your source." |
| The Gameplan | Teach them how to decide: (1) Will this get you closer to your goal? (2) Do you have access to the money (or someone who does)? (3) Can we help you do it better than alone? Three yeses → start. (2021 version adds "do you like us / our values?") |
| This Isn't A Fast Decision | "You decided years ago; you're just acting now." Recap the steps they took to get to this call — "six inches from gold". |

**Guarantee closes (only if you offer one):**
| Close | Structure |
|---|---|
| Both Options Are Risk Free | "You can't make an informed decision until you try it. Try it risk-free; if I'm not what I said in 30 days, money back." |
| Decadere | "Decide" comes from Latin for "cut off". Which future are you cutting off? |
| You're Not Gonna Struggle Forever | If you'll do it eventually, do it now and get the benefit sooner. |
| Closer Or Further | Five years of the current path — closer or further from the goal? Be directionally right, not a sniper. Nudge: no single program changed his life; the decision to invest in the first one did. |

> "it doesn't take time to make decisions. It takes information." — $100M Playbook: Closing

## Ask Again (Looping)
**Use when:** a rep accepts the first no; close rate low despite good calls.
**Core idea:** If you don't ask, you don't get it. Resolve the concern, then ask again — unlimited times, provided each ask follows a genuinely resolved concern and rapport holds.
**How to run it:** No → "Why?" → resolve with a close/story → re-ask ("If we can do Thursday, does that work? Great, let's get you started"). Repeat. Never re-ask without resolving something — that just annoys.
**Mistakes he calls out:** ending calls "jolly" without addressing the obstacle; asking again with nothing new (annoying); asking before you think they're ready — instead say "I'm sensing hesitation around price, talk to me."
**Sources:** YT "Sales Was Hard Until I Understood These 9 Concepts" (2024) · YT "After Closing 4000+ Sales..." (2024)

## Reinforce the Decision (the R in CLOSER)
**Use when:** refunds, chargebacks, buyer's remorse, ghosting after payment, slow starts.
**Core idea:** The sale continues after payment. Customers judge the whole relationship by how they're treated in the first day or two. Close them again at onboarding and at the first check-in.
**How to run it:**
1. Within hours: personal video by name from founder/rep ("saw you just joined, you're meeting [name] tomorrow, watch for a gift").
2. Handwritten card or physical gift for expensive purchases.
3. Booked onboarding call before they hang up (BAMFAM).
4. Fast first win, ideally within 24 hours (see `07-retention-and-ltv.md` Onboarding).
5. Re-sell the value against *their* stated goals on the onboarding call.
6. Ask for a referral while enthusiasm is highest (see `07` Referrals).
**Mistakes he calls out:** going silent right after they pay — he says that is exactly where most businesses drop the ball.
**Sources:** YT "I Taught 116+ Salesmen My Closing Framework" (2021) · Hormozi LinkedIn CLOSER post (2025, via research notes) · $100M Playbook: Retention

## Proof Checklist (13 ways to make proof more compelling)
**Use when:** ads/outreach don't convert, prospects are skeptical, "what makes you different?", money objections, new business with little credibility, or building a case-study/testimonial system.
**Core idea:** The biggest obstacle to buying is risk that it won't work for *them*. Proof is data supporting your claims; belief is a continuum, and proof moves people along it. Anyone can copy a promise; nobody can copy your results. Collect the data first, then make claims the data supports — and if the proof is weak, fix reality (work for free, improve the product).
**How to run it — score every proof asset against the checklist (more boxes = stronger):**

| Stronger > Weaker | Make it happen |
|---|---|
| In-person > virtual | Demonstrate in front of them; fly best customers to events. |
| Live > recorded | Have customers speak live on webinars/stage near the pitch. |
| Raw > processed | Phone-shot beats polished; give customers a structure and an incentive to send them. |
| Show > tell | Capture breakthrough moments as they happen (final weigh-ins, first reaction to results). |
| Others say it > you say it | Build review requests into the customer journey. |
| Identical to them > opposite | Match proof to the prospect's age, gender, ethnicity, industry, size; show every avatar you serve. |
| Personal > generic | Specific before-pain and after-life details ("we were two months from shutting our doors"). Probe in interviews. |
| Big result > small > none | Find longest-tenured, biggest-result customers; pay or invest to capture them in every format. |
| New > old | Keep a weekly flow of fresh testimonials. |
| More > less | Proof page / wall / reel with hundreds; CTA every few testimonials. |
| Third-party verified > none | Awards, certifications, reviews from trusted platforms embedded on your pages. |
| Numbers > no numbers | Collect milestone data (signup, onboarding, 30/60/90 days); state how and when collected; chart it. |
| Metaphors > jargon | Translate your mechanism into something the audience already knows. |

**Collecting proof (6-point testimonial script, from Marketing Machine):** (1) internal struggle — rock bottom; (2) external struggle — metrics then; (3) skepticism — main concerns before buying; (4) what made you do it anyway; (5) external victory — metrics now; (6) internal victory — what you can do now. Get the hook first; have them say their name on camera. Trigger requests at milestones, offer unlocks/awards for videos, and capture at events.
**Deploying proof across the sale:** ads/outreach → pre-call (matched case studies after booking and between calls) → on-call (stories for each pillar; "here are 200 people like you" in money closes) → ghosted leads every 90 days (results of people who joined when they ghosted) → post-purchase (reinforces they decided right).
**Numbers & thresholds:** Reviewing ~5 years of Gym Launch ads: ~80% of winners had no education, weren't offer-focused, didn't feature him and told one story — the product works (from source). Proof page with 5,000+ testimonials and a CTA every 3–5. Examples he cites (time-bound, July 2024): 54.1% of people who start a paid Skool community make money; average Gym Lord adds $100k+ profit in first full year.
**Worked example (illustrative, B2B services):** Claim "we cut onboarding time" → proof: raw Loom of a client ops lead showing before/after dashboard (raw, show, other people, numbers), same industry and headcount as the prospect (identical), dated this quarter (new), plus a metaphor ("it's like hiring a night shift that never sleeps").
**Mistakes he calls out:**
- Ads about "us, our services, our unique mechanism" — the low performers.
- Hunting for proof to fit a claim instead of claiming the proof you have.
- Letting testimonials go stale; using faceless generic reviews.
> "Your promise is not a differentiator. Your proof is." — $100M Playbook: Proof Checklist
**Ask the founder:** How many testimonials did you capture in the last 30 days? Do you have proof from someone identical to your current target? Do you track customer metrics at 30/60/90 days? What's your single biggest result, and is it in every format?
**Pairs with:** Pre-Call Setup · Money closes · `07-retention-and-ltv.md` (Customer Journey: testimonial milestone)
**Sources:** $100M Playbook: Proof Checklist · $100M Playbook: Marketing Machine (6-point script) · $100M Playbook: Lead Nurture (proof in nurture)

## Sales Training and Metrics
**Use when:** scaling beyond the founder as closer; reps inconsistent; "same script, different results".
**Core idea:** Selling is a learnable skill; teaching it is the more valuable skill. Words are a small part — tone and conviction carry most of it. Reps must "breathe the script" so they're present with the prospect.
**How to run it:**
1. Daily training: in a 2021 talk he describes 60 min/day, 5 days/week — read script aloud, drill obstacles (think about it / partner / expensive), then review recordings.
2. Drill one part of the script per session (intro, close, spouse overcome); give one piece of feedback at a time; repeat correctly three times.
3. Weekly game-tape review; have the rep critique themselves first. Put sales and customer success on the same review so sales stops over-promising.
4. Conviction: re-read testimonials aloud to the team daily; owners fix product gaps so reps can believe. If there are no testimonials, the rep should find a product worth selling.
5. Track leading metrics: schedule rate, show rate, offer rate, close rate, calls-to-close, cash collected. Rank reps by show rate (work ethic) and close rate (skill).
6. Score leads and route the best leads to the best closers; train new reps on weaker leads.
7. Keep the script concise; strip add-ons reps picked up from coincidental wins.
**Numbers & thresholds:** Best salespeople listen about twice as much as they talk, per his portfolio's call analysis (YT 2024). Routing best leads to best closers 5x'd an office and was credited with $200M→$1B over five years at a timeshare company (anecdote, Lead Nurture playbook).
**Mistakes he calls out:** managers who won't role-play; long scripts; reps who blame lead quality publicly; not recording your hot streaks — when you're on fire, record and rewatch it; he says good closers try to get hot while great closers never get cold.
**Sources:** YT "I Taught 116+ Salesmen My Closing Framework" (2021) · YT "Sales Was Hard Until I Understood These 9 Concepts" (2024) · YT "The Ultimate Sales Training for 2026 [Full Course]" (2025) · $100M Playbook: Lead Nurture (Execution)

## Procedure: Build My Sales Script
**Use when:** "write me a sales script", "how should I run calls for [offer]", "my calls are a mess".
**Inputs to collect first (ask if missing):** offer and price; ideal customer (who stays longest and pays most); dream outcome; top 3 reasons past clients failed before you (the 3 pillars); top 5 objections by frequency; guarantee (yes/no, terms); proof assets (best 3 results, matched to segments); call length; one or multiple calls; who attends.
**Steps:**
1. **Qualifiers:** write STAR/BANT questions for the application and a 60-second on-call recheck. List disqualifiers.
2. **Pillars:** turn the three missing pieces into three pillar names (outcome language). Write one short story or metaphor per pillar that breaks the main false belief.
3. **Objection inventory:** map each known objection to its blame layer; pick 2–3 closes per objection from this file; write them in the founder's own words; pre-empt the top two as obstacles during the Overview.
4. **Assemble the script** (output this structure, filled in):
   - **0. Pre-call:** confirmation message + matched case study + reminder cadence; 5-min research checklist.
   - **1. Open (≤1 min):** specific observation from research; agenda + permission ("I'll ask some questions, and if it's a fit I'll show you how we'd help — fair?").
   - **2. Clarify:** 3–5 questions (trigger, goal, why now, why it matters).
   - **3. Label:** one restatement sentence with a yes-check.
   - **4. Overview (pain cycle):** "what have you tried / how long / how did it go / what else" loop; time/money/decision-maker pre-empt questions; "it's not your fault — you were missing [pillar]".
   - **5. Sell the vacation (≤3 min):** transition question; three pillars with one story each; destination tiers if any.
   - **6. Ask:** a direct ask with price ("Want to get started?"). Then silence.
   - **7. Explain away concerns:** AAA preface + 2–3 closes per top objection; all-purpose fallbacks (Main Concern, Hypothetical, 1–10, Best/Worst Case); ask again after each resolution.
   - **8. Payment:** ID → card trade or ACH; payment options (see `03-money-models.md`).
   - **9. Reinforce:** welcome video, next booked call, gift/card, first-24-hour win, referral ask ("who do you know who's as [compliment] as you who'd want this too?").
   - **10. If no:** BAMFAM next step; proof follow-up cadence; 90-day re-engage.
5. **QA the script:** Is it mostly questions? Does any section explain features? Is the pitch under 3 minutes when read aloud? Is every close phrased as a reframe, not a disagreement? Does it work if the prospect says "think about it" at minute 5?
6. **Drill plan:** one section per day of role-play; record the first 20 calls; adjust one thing at a time.
**Worked example (illustrative):** AI-ops agency, $15k setup + $3k/mo. Pillars: "find the hours" (process audit), "automate the boring" (build), "keep it running" (support). Story for pillar 3: "a car you buy but never service". Top objection "we'll build it in-house" → Preferences/Fit → Isolate and Solve, then Good Fast Cheap.
**Sources:** synthesis of the cards above
