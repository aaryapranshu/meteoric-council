## Money Model Fundamentals: CAC, Gross Profit, Payback, 30-Day Cash
**Use when:** any question about affording growth, ad budgets, or "is my business healthy?"
**Core idea:** A business is an arbitrage: what it costs to get a customer against what that customer is worth in gross profit. Older advice compares CAC with LTV. Money Models compresses the window: how much gross profit do you collect in the **first 30 days** compared with what it cost to acquire and serve that customer?
> "Money models is flipping that. You make more money getting a customer than it costs you to get them within the first 30 days." — Hormozi on *My First Million* (as quoted by Gavel)

**Definitions (use these exactly):**
- **CAC** = total acquisition cost in a period (ad spend + sales labour + commissions + tools) ÷ new customers in that period.
- **Gross profit (GP)** = revenue − cost to deliver (COGS). For services, COGS is the incremental cost of serving one more customer, mostly delivery payroll. It excludes rent, admin and HR.
- **LTGP** (lifetime gross profit; he also calls it LTV or CLV) = gross profit collected over the customer's life. He always uses *gross profit*, never revenue.
- **Payback period** = time until cumulative GP from a customer ≥ CAC.
- **30-day cash / 30-day GP** = gross profit collected from a new customer in their first 30 days.
**Why 30 days:** it's the interest-free float on a credit card. If a customer repays CAC plus COGS inside one card cycle, even a $500 limit funds growth. Faster collection means more cycles a month. He calls this speeding up the **cash conversion cycle** (YT, Dec 2024).
**Mistakes he calls out:**
- Copying industry billing norms ("nobody in my industry collects upfront"). He calls this belief one of the things that keeps owners poor.
- Measuring LTV in revenue.
- Counting new sign-ups when calculating churn. Churn = lost ÷ starting customers only.
**Ask the founder:** What is your fully loaded CAC? What do you collect in a new customer's first 30 days, and what does delivering that cost? How long until a customer has paid back CAC?

## Client-Financed Acquisition (CFA)
**Use when:** "I can't afford ads", "ads work but I run out of cash", "how much can I spend to get a customer?", or scaling paid acquisition.
**Core idea:** Get customers to pay for your marketing. If the first 30 days of gross profit covers **twice** the cost to acquire and serve a customer, each new customer funds themselves *and* the next one. Growth then stops being limited by cash.
**The rule:** **30-day gross profit ≥ 2 × (CAC + COGS)**. 2x is the *minimum* for CFA. He says he'd rather see far more ("if I can make this… 10 times").
- Naming and wording variants: "Client-Financed Acquisition (CFA)" is the course module name. In a Dec 2024 video he says "customer financed acquisition" and phrases the rule as 2 × (CAC + COGS) = **30-day cash collected**. Summaries phrase it as 30-day *gross profit*. Use gross profit to be conservative.
**How to run it:**
1. Compute CAC and front-end COGS per customer.
2. Compute current 30-day GP per customer.
3. Gap = 2 × (CAC + COGS) − 30-day GP.
4. Close the gap in this order: (a) price or prepay the front end (charge more, collect upfront, prepay the quarter or year); (b) add an **upsell** in the first 30 days, often within 48 hours; (c) add **downsells** so non-buyers still pay something; (d) only then work on CAC.
5. Once CFA holds, raise ad spend until 30-day GP per customer falls back to the 2x line.
**Numbers & thresholds:**
- Worked rule (YT, 2024): CAC $100 + COGS $50 → need $300 in the first 30 days.
- Origin story (secondary, consistent across summaries): gym CAC rose from ~$100 to ~$500 against a $99/month membership. Selling supplements in the first 48 hours closed the cash gap and became Prestige Labs.
- Ad-test budgeting from *$100M Leads* (secondary): budget about 2x the 30-day cash from one customer to test. If CAC is above ~3x the industry average, fix the ads. If CAC is normal but economics don't work, fix the money model.
**Worked example (2026 Scale or Fail):** A coaching business was told to sell a quarter upfront, about $9k down because the deep-dive is the most expensive part to deliver, then start monthly payments immediately with no gap that invites churn. Layaway was the downsell. His framing: $1,500 CAC on a $2,500 sale is fine, and so is $2,500 CAC on a $2,500 sale. With $9k collected upfront you can buy far more customers.
**Mistakes he calls out:** Treating CAC as the only lever. CAC only goes up over time ("CAC will never be cheaper than it is today", 2026), so the durable fix is making each customer worth more, faster.
**Ask the founder:** CAC? Front-end price and COGS? How much do you collect in the first 30 days today? Do you take any prepayment?

## LTGP:CAC Ratio (and the 3:1 vs 6–9:1 nuance)
**Use when:** "Is my LTV:CAC good?", "how much can I spend per customer?", investor or valuation questions, or a channel's economics.
**Core idea:** LTGP:CAC is "the fundamental economic unit" of a business: what you make from a customer (gross profit) for each dollar spent getting them. The bigger the gap, the more you can outspend competitors in every auction for attention.
**How to calculate LTGP (LTV playbook method):**
1. **Gross profit per transaction or month.** Service example: 10 clients per rep × $3,000 = $30k/month per rep. Rep costs $6k, so GP is 80%, or $2,400/client/month.
2. **Lifetime transactions or churn.** Export customer data and average transactions per customer. For recurring revenue, monthly churn = customers lost ÷ customers at the start (new sign-ups don't count).
3. **LTGP.** Transactional: GP × average transactions (example $80 × 4; the playbook prints $360 but the correct product is **$320**). Recurring: monthly GP ÷ monthly churn (example $2,400 ÷ 5% = **$48,000**).
- Quick back-of-napkin LTV (his 2022 X post): total sales $ ÷ total customers, then × gross margin.
**Thresholds (time-bound, cite with date):**
- **3:1** is the commonly cited floor attributed to *$100M Leads*. Below that, fix the campaign or add upsells.
- **Jan 2026 refinement (podcast Ep 990, "The Mathematics of Business", via episode summary):** 3:1 only holds for businesses with **no humans** in attraction, conversion or delivery (~5% of businesses, e.g., pure SaaS). Businesses with people involved need **6:1 to 9:1** to absorb training, turnover and inconsistency. **Default to 6–9:1 for service businesses, agencies and consultancies.**
- "30:1+ for wealth building" was seen only in one Instagram caption. It's unverified, so don't cite it.
**Worked examples (from sources):**
- A stylist business: $600 blended CAC, $10k annual LTV, a 16:1 ratio. His verdict: economics are strong, so pour volume in. She was spending only $3,400/year (YT, Jan 2025).
- "Permanent CAC" (YT, Dec 2024, illustrative): $100 to start a free trial, 1 in 3 converts ($300), 1 in 3 of those become long-term customers, so ~$900 per permanent customer against $1,200/year recurring.
- Sales hires as LTGP:CAC: a rep costing ~$200k who generates ~$1M of gross profit is a 5:1 return on talent (YT, 2026).
**Mistakes he calls out:**
- Running a human-heavy business on SaaS ratios.
- Capping the budget on a channel that returns $16 in 30 days for every $1. If the machine works, feed it.
- Revenue LTV instead of gross-profit LTV.
**Ask the founder:** How many humans touch attraction, sales and delivery? What are LTGP and CAC by channel? Do you know your churn or repeat rate?

## The Four Offer Types & How to Sequence Them
**Use when:** "What should my offer stack look like?", "where do I start?", or the founder has one offer and nothing else.
**Core idea:** Every money model is built from four offer types:
1. **Attraction** turns strangers into customers, ideally while covering CAC.
2. **Upsell** sells the next problem the first purchase creates, and makes the money.
3. **Downsell** turns a "no" into a smaller "yes".
4. **Continuity** produces recurring revenue that compounds LTGP.
**Build order (book, Part 8, via summaries):**
- **Stage I: Get Cash.** Install one attraction offer.
- **Stage II: Get More Cash.** Add an upsell, then a downsell for the people who decline.
- **Stage III: Get the Most Cash.** Add continuity.
- Perfect **one offer at a time** before adding the next. His warning: a bootstrapped business that launches from zero with a "finished" money model collapses. The money model grows with the business.
**Timing matters as much as the offer:**
> "It's not just how you sell, it's when you sell." — YT "8 Entrepreneurs Compete For $100,000" (2026)

- Ask several times: "The more times you ask the more likely they are to take the purchase offer" (YT, 2026). For a 3-month program: offer the next step at the halfway point, at two-thirds, and as a last chance at the end. If you only mention it at the end, take rates are low. One founder was getting 10%. He says a real pitch at the end alone usually gets about a third.
- Make renewals opt-out, not opt-in, wherever that's legal and disclosed.
**Mistakes he calls out:**
- Building all four at once.
- Adding offers that serve a different customer than the first one. The upsell must solve the *same* customer's next problem.
- Barely pitching at events or at the end of programs.
**Ask the founder:** What's your front-end offer and its take rate? What do customers ask for next? Where do people say no today, and what do they leave with?

## Attraction Offers
**Use when:** "How do I get more first-time buyers?", "my lead-to-customer rate is low", "I need cheaper customer acquisition", or the front end loses money.
**Core idea:** Strangers can't judge your value, but they understand price, so lead with a price that feels like a steal, ideally free, and structure it so the customer still covers CAC.
> "Strangers can only take your word on the value. But, they absolutely understand the price." — $100M Money Models (Goodreads)

**The six official sub-tactics (course module names):**

| Tactic | How it works | Use when / rules |
|---|---|---|
| **Win Your Money Back** | Customer pays upfront. If they hit stated criteria by a deadline, they get the money back, usually as credit toward the next purchase. | Programs where results depend on attendance or compliance (gyms, education, coaching). Criteria must be easy to track and include the actions that create results, such as required check-ins. Most people roll the credit into the core offer. |
| **Free Giveaways** | Advertise a grand prize with a stated value. Entry requires contact details and permission to follow up. Draw in 3–7 days with daily countdowns. Non-winners get a "consolation" discount on the core offer. | Building a list fast in a local or defined market. Limit entries by time or count. |
| **Decoy Offer** | Advertise a cheap or limited basic option. On the call or checkout, show the premium option side by side as the obvious choice. What they'd pay for the decoy can be credited toward premium. | Hormozi credits a decoy structure with early Gym Launch growth (summaries). The decoy must be real and deliverable. |
| **Buy X Get Y (Free)** | Frame the discount as free stuff: "buy one, get two free" beats "66% off". Y can be bigger than X, and prices can rise to fund it. Free items can differ from the paid ones. | Physical products, sessions, consumables. |
| **Pay Less Now (or Pay More Later)** | Start free or cheap today with the card on file and a clear yes/no result promised in a set time. Pay full price later unless they show up, try it, and cancel. Bonuses reward paying now instead. | Services with a quick provable result. In course notes this is paired with a satisfaction guarantee. |
| **Free with Consumption** | The front-end item is free *when* the customer consumes or buys something else that pays for it. | Businesses with a high-margin consumable or companion purchase. *Exact book definition not verified in this kit. Confirm against the course module before quoting specifics.* |

**Naming variants:** "Buy X Get Y Free" / "Buy X Get Y". "Pay Less Now" / "Pay Less Now or Pay More Later". "Decoy Offers" / "Decoy Offer".
**Later additions:** *$100M Lost Chapters* (Nov 2025) audiobook episodes are titled "Attraction Offer. Freemium." and "Attraction Offer. Free Pick Your Price." Summary of the freemium rule: the free tier must keep delivering value, be cheap to serve, and reveal a need the paid tier fills. Details beyond the titles are secondary. Some summaries add a "Free Goodwill" attraction offer. It isn't in the official module list (likely a mix-up with the *$100M Leads* review-ask chapter), so don't present it as canon.
**Worked examples (2026 Scale or Fail):**
- A sticker-art membership sold a $10 paid challenge at ~$37 CAC, ~$36 AOV and a 22% conversion into a $27/month membership. Test proposed: reposition the challenge as a **7-day ($1) trial of the membership**, so 100% of buyers enter continuity, then push the annual plan and upsell the tools needed for the challenge. Run it as a split test at the same $10 price so ads stay identical.
- An HVAC cleaner sold a $175 service (~$25 to deliver) through partners as *their* upsell. That turns a partner's customer into a lead for the core $3–4k job. Effective CAC ≈ cost to deliver ÷ upsell take rate (illustrative: $25 ÷ 1/3 ≈ $75 per customer).
**Mistakes he calls out:**
- Free with no path to the next purchase.
- Discounting the core offer instead of giving something away.
- Eight entrepreneurs episode: "give away something big instead of discounting."
**Ask the founder:** What's the cheapest thing you can deliver that proves value fast? Which next purchase does it naturally lead to? What does a free customer cost you?

## Upsell Offers
**Use when:** "What should I upsell?", the front end breaks even or loses money, AOV is low, or "how do I increase average ticket?"
**Core idea:** Every purchase creates or reveals the next problem. Sell the solution the moment the customer becomes aware of it. The first offer may be thin on profit, and the upsell makes up the difference. His favourite upsell of all time: *more of, or more help with, what they just bought, with faster results, less risk, less effort and less hassle, for more money.*
> "If customers like your stuff, they want to buy more stuff like it!" — $100M Playbook: Lifetime Value

**The four official sub-tactics:**

| Tactic | How it works | Rules & numbers |
|---|---|---|
| **Classic Upsell** | "You can't have X without Y." The core purchase creates a new need, and you offer it right away (insurance with the rental car, fries with the burger, supplements with the gym). | Book the next meeting inside every meeting. Offer warranties or insurance. Deliver the upsell fast. |
| **Menu Upsell** | A consultative close. **(1) Unsell:** tell them what they *don't* need, which builds trust. **(2) Prescribe:** tell them what they do need and why. **(3) A/B choice:** "Would you rather this or this?" (choice question, not yes/no). **(4) Card on file:** "Want to take care of that with the card on file? We've already got the hood popped." | His **favourite chapter** in the book (YT, Aug 2026). Best for transactional sales closed in one conversation. A roofing founder using it: customers "just taking every recommendation". Write personalised instructions as if they've already bought. |
| **Anchor Upsell** | Present the premium version first (sticker shock). If they react, offer a cheaper option that keeps the main feature and trims secondary ones. The core now feels like a bargain. | Book summaries: premium at **5–10x** core. Pricing playbook "Ultra High Ticket Anchor": **10x+**. Both work, so pick a premium you're happy to deliver. If 10% take a $5k option over a $500 core, LTV goes $440 → $890. |
| **Rollover Upsell** | Credit what they already paid (a previous purchase, a trial, a competitor's contract) toward a bigger, longer commitment. | Summaries: the upsell should be **≥4x the rollover credit**, one-time, with urgency. Target past purchasers and competitors' customers. Mid-program version: offer the next level halfway through the current program, not at the end. One founder did this and Hormozi called it "the Alex roll over", saying that structure has made him "probably $50 million" (YT, Oct 2026). |

**Stacking:** you can chain upsells (core → classic → menu), offering each only after the previous yes. Run each through the Value Equation (faster, easier, less risk).
**Numbers & thresholds:**
- LTV playbook case: adding an upsell closely related to the core product (85% of surveyed customers preferred it over the founders' unrelated idea) **multiplied LTV 2.2x** and allowed **5x ad spend** profitably.
- Quantity or quality upsells offered first on calls, then downselling to standard, "routinely" lift cash collected 20%+ (LTV playbook).
**Mistakes he calls out:**
- Upselling something unrelated because the founder is passionate about it. Survey customers or offer both and let them choose.
- Being too timid to make the offer. If someone is offended by a thank-you-page offer, the business doesn't care, because others will be glad it was available (YT, 2024).
- Pitching only once, at the end.
**Ask the founder:** What do customers buy or ask for right after buying from you? What problem does your product create? Do you have a premium version priced 5–10x the core?

## Downsell Offers
**Use when:** "People say no on price", "what do I offer people who can't afford it?", or the close rate is fine but there's a big pile of qualified-ish nos.
**Core idea:** Change what they pay or what they get, so a "no" becomes a smaller "yes". You capture revenue from people who would otherwise leave with nothing.
> "I forbid my sales team from selling a qualified person a downsell." — $100M Playbook: Lifetime Value

**The three official sub-tactics:**

| Tactic | How it works | Rules |
|---|---|---|
| **Payment Plans** | Same offer, different payment shape: split pay (even or uneven), scheduled payments, third-party financing, payments timed to paychecks, layaway (pay over time, start when paid). | Reward paying in full. Retry declined cards a few times around payday. Anchor with full pay first. |
| **Free Trials** (the book calls it **Trial with Penalty**) | Start free *if* they complete specified activities and show proof. If they don't, they pay. Required check-ins become upsell moments. | Card on file. The penalty makes free customers behave like paying ones. Naming variant: course module "Free Trials", book "Trial with Penalty". |
| **Feature Downsells** | Remove features for a lower price. Turn down quality, quantity, speed, availability, location, live vs recorded, DFY → DWY → DIY, personalisation, or guarantee strength. | Name the cheapest combination "minimum" and the main one something aspirational. Cutting the guarantee is a legitimate downsell lever. You can also barter a discount for advertising or referrals. |

**Why it raises money (LTV playbook):** four visitors, two buy a $5 burger and two buy nothing, makes $2.50 per visitor. Offer the two non-buyers a $2.50 smaller burger and it becomes $3.75 per visitor (**+50%**). The only risk is cannibalisation: people who would have bought the main offer taking the downsell. Hence the rule: downsell **only to people who don't qualify** for the main offer.
**Tension to flag:** *$100M Offers* says never discount the main offer to close (add bonuses instead). Money Models teaches payment-plan and feature downsells. They reconcile like this: never cut the *price of the same thing*. Change the *thing* (fewer features) or the *payment shape*. Make the qualified person say no to the full offer first.
**Mistakes he calls out:** Leading with the downsell. Discounting the full offer to a qualified buyer. Offering a downsell so good it cannibalises the core.
**Ask the founder:** Why do people say no: cash flow, perceived risk, or fit? What can you remove that cuts your cost more than their value? Do you take a card on file?
**Pairs with:** Upsell · Continuity · Crazy 8 (#7–8) · `02` Bonuses

## Continuity Offers
**Use when:** "How do I get recurring revenue?", one-and-done customers, churn questions, or "how do I get people onto longer commitments?"
**Core idea:** Recurring revenue compounds LTGP. Get people into continuity by making the long-term choice feel like the better deal, without hiding anything.

**The three official sub-tactics:**

| Tactic | How it works | Rules & numbers |
|---|---|---|
| **Continuity Bonus** | Give a bonus to anyone who signs up for the long-term or recurring option *now*. Sell the bonus, not the membership. | Use things you already have. Give tiers titles. |
| **Continuity Discount** | Discount for committing longer: free months, an upfront discount with a pushed-out term, or a discount that starts after the first 1–2 payments. | Option: a lifetime discount after the most common churn point. If they cancel early, a cancellation fee equals the discount they received. Do exit interviews. |
| **Waived Fee** | Charge a setup or onboarding fee on monthly signup (summaries: **3–5x the monthly fee**). Waive it for an annual commitment. If they cancel within the term, they owe the fee. | Justify the fee with real onboarding and acquisition costs. Pricing-playbook variant: have both a setup fee and an annual renewal fee so you can waive one to close. |

**Pricing heuristic for the continuity choice (book, Goodreads quote):** to get ~50% to choose continuity, price the standalone or non-continuity option **1.33x** higher. To get ~60%, **1.66x** higher. Another summary phrases it as people paying ~33% more to avoid continuity.
**Related plays from the Pricing playbook:**
- **Automatic continuity ("continued access"):** a 5–20% priced, low-work tail that starts automatically after the main program and is agreed upfront. A workshop speaker said it made him $750k in a year. Hormozi says it later made him ~$150k/month "of pure profit". Example math: $5,600 → $7,400 LTV (+32%).
- **Billing frequency:** Profitwell data, monthly churn **10.7%** on monthly billing, **5%** quarterly, **2%** annual. Sell 6–12 weeks first, then upsell the prepaid year.
**Business-model heuristics from 2026 videos:**
- SMB services: at **$1,500–3k/month**, average stick is **4–6 months**, which leads to a revenue plateau. Two models work, low or high, and the middle is a dead zone where businesses die. Either go cheap and near-zero-delivery (~$300–500/month "nuisance" services such as maps ranking or reviews, with **30–40-month** stick and low CAC) or go up-market to a better avatar.
- Education and info products churn by design (people graduate). Build the next level (continuity) in before they finish.
- Retention benchmark (Skool platform data, 2026): ~80% monthly retention on average, best groups 90%+, weak groups under 70%. Going from 20% to 10% churn **doubles** LTV.
**Worked example (Jan 2025 episode):** A $8,500 style transformation flowing into a quarterly or monthly styling continuity, with almost no churn. He left it alone ("don't mess with the continuity") and pushed volume instead.
**Mistakes he calls out:** Undisclosed or forced continuity ("Don't be a sneak"). Grandfathered lifetime deals. A gap between the front-end program and the first recurring charge (it invites churn, so start payments immediately).
**Ask the founder:** What do customers still need after the main result? What's the smallest, highest-margin piece you could keep delivering? What's your monthly churn, and when do most people cancel?

## The Crazy 8: Lifetime Value Levers (Monetisation)
**Use when:** "How do I make each customer worth more?", "we get customers fine but they don't spend much", or "what can I sell existing customers?"
**Core idea:** "Raise AOV or raise frequency" is too broad to act on. He drills his teams on eight specific levers and runs through all eight on every business. Whoever makes a customer worth the most can outbid everyone else for attention.
> "the cost of getting a customer can only hit zero… but how much money you make from each customer can go infinitely high." — $100M Playbook: Lifetime Value

| # | Lever | Examples & rules |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Raise prices** | The biggest single effect on gross profit. +20% at 10% margin is 3x profit. Test quarterly, starting low and nudging up. → file 02 |
| 2 | **Lower cost to deliver** | Raise the clients-per-employee ratio. Offshore talent (can cut 80%+). Productise for similar customers. Move DFY → DWY. Cap usage, then charge per use. Change lifetime to annual. Move in-person to remote. Cut recurring meetings quarterly and add back half-length. Prepay vendors in bulk for 10–20% off. |
| 3 | **Increase frequency (# of purchases)** | Three ways only: **add recurring** (plumbing membership, mug-of-the-month, course accountability calls), **reduce churn** (10% → 5% doubles LTV), **regular follow-up offers** (quarterly promotion to past customers and leads, value in between). |
| 4 | **Cross-sell something different** | A complementary item: snow removal for lawn clients, fries and soda, a community on top of a course. Added LTV = take rate × GP. 20% × $100 adds $20. Keep it close to existing operations. |
| 5 | **Upsell quantity (sell more)** | Bulk or prepay (12 months up front), more often (monthly → every 3 weeks = 1.33x), bigger (1 hour → 3 hours). Offer it *first* on calls, then downsell to standard. |
| 6 | **Upsell quality (sell better)** | Premium or newer version: faster response, more availability, more locations, free rescheduling, senior provider, live vs recorded, in-person, DFY, personalisation, better materials. Offer first, or upgrade in a second meeting. "Routinely" 20%+ lift in cash collected. |
| 7 | **Downsell quantity** | Fewer units, smaller, less often, *rather than nothing*. Only for people who don't qualify for the main offer. |
| 8 | **Downsell quality** | A worse experience (slower, more junior staff, DIY, no or weaker guarantee) for the same result. Only for people who don't qualify. |

## Build My Money Model (step-by-step walk-through for a founder)
Ask one block at a time. Don't advance until you have numbers, or the founder agrees to label a number as an estimate. Show the math at every step.

**Step 1 — Baseline the unit economics.**
Ask: core offer and price? Billing structure? COGS per customer (delivery payroll per client, materials)? CAC by channel (ads + sales comp + tools ÷ new customers)? Close rate? Monthly churn or repeat purchases? How many humans touch attraction, sales and delivery?
Compute: gross margin %, LTGP, LTGP:CAC, payback period, **30-day GP per new customer**.

**Step 2 — Diagnose against thresholds.**
- 30-day GP vs **2 × (CAC + COGS)**. The gap is the target.
- LTGP:CAC vs **3:1** (no-human business) or **6–9:1** (human-heavy). Cite Jan 2026.
- Gross margin vs **80%** (services).
- Close rate >50% (and especially >80%) means under-priced. Go to file 02 before adding offers.
- Churn: is it in the SMB 4–6-month "dead zone"?
Name the single biggest constraint: price, front-end cash, LTV or CAC.

**Step 3 — Fix the core price and payment shape first.**
Price raise test on new customers. Annual or quarterly prepay anchored first. 28-day billing. Processing fee plus backup card. Setup or waived fee. Annual increase clause. Re-run the Step 1 math.

**Step 4 — Stage I: choose ONE attraction offer.**
Pick from Win Your Money Back · Free Giveaway · Decoy · Buy X Get Y · Pay Less Now · Free with Consumption (plus Freemium or Free Pick Your Price from *Lost Chapters*). Selection rules:
- Results depend on customer compliance → Win Your Money Back or Trial with Penalty.
- Local or defined market, need a list fast → Giveaway.
- Price-shopped market → Decoy beside premium.
- Consumables or sessions → Buy X Get Y.
- Quick provable result → Pay Less Now.
Write it, script it, and set the success metric: take rate and its effect on 30-day GP.

**Step 5 — Stage II: add ONE upsell inside the first 30 days (ideally within 48 hours or at the first natural milestone).**
Ask: "What problem does the first purchase create? What do customers ask for next?"
- Transactional, single conversation → Menu Upsell (unsell → prescribe → A/B → card on file).
- A natural "can't have X without Y" → Classic.
- Clear premium version → Anchor (5–10x, shown first).
- They paid for a trial, front end or competitor → Rollover (credit toward ≥4x).
Plan when you'll ask (midpoint, two-thirds, end). Compute the new 30-day GP = old + take rate × upsell GP.

**Step 6 — Stage II: add ONE downsell for the unqualified "no".**
Payment plan / layaway → Trial with Penalty → Feature Downsell, in that order of least value lost. Rule: never offer it to a qualified buyer. Compute added revenue per lead.

**Step 7 — Stage III: add continuity.**
What ongoing value remains after the main result? Choose Continuity Bonus, Continuity Discount or Waived Fee. Optionally add automatic continued access at 5–20% of the core price. Price the non-continuity option ~1.33x higher to push ~50% into continuity. Disclose everything.

**Step 8 — Layer the existing-customer cash calendar.**
A quarterly Fast Cash Play (file 02): 10–50x price, 5–10% cap, ≤7-day sequence, ≥90% GM. Collect the 4Rs afterwards.

**Step 9 — Re-run the math and set the spend rule.**
New 30-day GP, LTGP and LTGP:CAC. If 30-day GP ≥ 2 × (CAC + COGS), raise acquisition spend until it hits the line. Track weekly: take rate per offer, 30-day GP per cohort, churn.

**Step 10 — Sequence the rollout.**
Install one offer per cycle (e.g., 2–4 weeks each). Measure, then add the next. Don't launch all four at once. Remind the founder that the model grows with the business.

**Output to give the founder:** a one-page model:
1. Offer ladder (Attraction → Upsell(s) → Downsell → Continuity → Fast Cash calendar) with price, timing and script line for each.
2. Before/after table: 30-day GP, LTGP, LTGP:CAC, payback.
3. The single next action this week.
Label every number as either theirs or illustrative.
