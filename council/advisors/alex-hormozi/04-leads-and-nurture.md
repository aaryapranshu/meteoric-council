## Lead Vocabulary & Funnel Math
**Use when:** any lead conversation. Set the terms first so the numbers mean something.
**Core idea:** A lead is someone you can contact. An **engaged lead** is someone you can contact *who has shown interest* in what you sell. Advertising exists to create engaged leads. Nurture turns engaged leads into shows. Sales turns shows into money.
**How to run it:**
1. Count raw contacts (leads) and people who raised a hand (engaged leads). Track them separately.
2. Define three nurture rates, as the Lead Nurture playbook does:
   - **Schedule rate** = engaged leads who book ÷ engaged leads.
   - **Show rate** = booked leads who show ÷ booked leads.
   - **Throughput** = engaged leads who show ÷ engaged leads.
3. Multiply through: leads × schedule rate × show rate × close rate × price = revenue. Find the weakest multiplier.
**Numbers & thresholds:** Playbook example: 100 leads, 50% schedule, 50% show = 25% throughput. Hormozi's claim: show-rate work routinely adds 20–40% more shown appointments (sometimes 200%+), which at 20% margins can mean 2–3x profit.
**Worked example (illustrative):** 200 engaged leads/mo, 40% schedule, 55% show, 25% close, $3k ticket ≈ $33k. Lifting show rate to 75% (no new ad spend) ≈ $45k.
**Mistakes he calls out:**
- Treating "leads" and "engaged leads" as the same number.
- Obsessing over close rate while ignoring lead-to-close. The real number is conversion on the leads you already have.
- Not seeing money you *didn't* make as money you *lost*.
**Ask the founder:** How many people did you contact last month? How many raised a hand? Of those, how many booked, showed, bought?
**Pairs with:** Core Four; Lead Nurture: Four Pillars; sales file (`05`) (close rate).
**Sources:** $100M Playbook: Lead Nurture · YT "Watch this to get your first 5 customers" (2023) · Research notes ($100M Leads definitions)

## The Core Four
**Use when:** "I need more customers", "no one is buying", "which marketing should I do".
**Core idea:** There are only four ways to let people know about your stuff. It's a 2×2: who you reach (people who know you vs strangers) × how (one-to-one vs one-to-many). If the founder isn't doing one of these daily, they aren't advertising.

| | One-to-one | One-to-many |
|---|---|---|
| **Know you (warm)** | Warm outreach | Post free content |
| **Strangers (cold)** | Cold outreach | Run paid ads |

**How to run it:**
1. Ask how many hours a day they spend on the Core Four. Usually the answer is near zero.
2. Under ~$1–3M/yr: block the first ~4 hours of the day for promotion, then run the business (at a gym-owner conference, nobody in the room did this; "this is why you're small").
3. Pick **one** channel. Choose by overlap with existing skills (team good at social → content; good on the phone → outreach). Don't spread across five.
4. Typical order for a beginner: warm outreach → content → cold outreach → paid ads. Warm is cheapest and builds skill; ads need money and proven conversion.
5. Stay on one channel until it works reliably before adding a second.
6. His growth staging: $0→6 figures = one product, one avatar, one channel. 6→7 figures = same, made consistent. 7→8 figures = add a second product (upsell). Multiple 8 figures = second channel. He admits exceptions exist (e.g. one product on many channels).
**Worked example (illustrative):** Agency owner at $25k/mo doing referrals only. Prescription: pick LinkedIn cold outreach (owner already sells well 1:1), 100 personalised touches/day, ignore content and ads for 90 days.
**Mistakes he calls out:**
- "X channel doesn't work for us." All advertising works. The honest version is "I don't know how to make X work yet."
- Running four channels at once badly.
**Ask the founder:** Which of the four did you do today, and for how long? Which one matches the skills you already have? Is there a competitor already winning on that channel? (If yes, it can work.)
**Pairs with:** Rule of 100; More, Better, New; each Core Four card below; file 06 for content and ads craft.

## Warm Outreach (the First 5 Customers play)
**Use when:** founder has 0–10 customers, a new offer or division, or no testimonials. "How do I get my first clients?"
**Core idea:** Everyone already has hundreds of contacts. Message them personally, *don't pitch*: ask if they know someone with the problem, and offer to help a few people free (or cheap) in exchange for feedback, a testimonial and referrals. Free customers buy you reps, proof and word of mouth.
**How to run it:**
1. **Build three lists:** every email contact, every social follower you can DM, every phone contact. Combine.
2. **Pick one platform** (the one with the most contacts). 3. **Personalise each message** with something you actually know about them (new job, new baby, a move). Thirty seconds of looking at their profile.
4. **Send 100 a day.** Up to 3 attempts per person (research notes on the book).
5. **When they reply, warm up with ACA:** **A**cknowledge what they said → **C**ompliment it → **A**sk a question tied to that compliment that leads toward your problem area.
6. **Ask for a referral, not a sale:** "Do you know anyone who [struggle] and wants [dream outcome] in [time]? I'm taking on five case studies free."
7. **If they want it themselves,** they're now an engaged lead. Offer it free with three conditions: they use it, give feedback, and leave a strong review if it's earned.
8. Loop back to the top of the list. Many contacts will hand you other names.
9. **Start charging once people refer.** Raise price in steps.
10. **Keep the list warm:** check in like a human, post results, provide value. Eventually they ask you.
**Script structure (fill-in; adapt, don't read robotically):**
- Opener: "Hey [name] — saw [specific personal detail]. [Genuine reaction]."
- After reply (ACA): "[Acknowledge]. [Compliment]. [Question that bridges to the problem you solve]?"
- Referral ask: "Random one — do you know anyone who's [struggle] and wants [dream outcome] in [timeframe]? I'm taking on [5] people free because I want case studies and testimonials. If I'm good, they leave a review; if I suck, they tell me how to get better."
- If they say no: a joking "anyone you *hate* come to mind?" sometimes surfaces the real answer.
- Short version if there's no room: "I help [avatar] get [dream outcome] in [time] without [effort/sacrifice], and I guarantee [X]."
- Ask for a three-way text intro on the spot. The friend doesn't have to remember, and the person introduced is less rude.
**Numbers & thresholds:**
- Price ladder, version A (2023 video): first five free → next five 80% off → 60% → 40% → 20% → full price → keep going above "full" if value supports it.
- Version B (2024 video): after a first test batch of 10–20, raise ~20% every five clients until conversion × price starts falling. The two versions differ in step size; both mean *raise in small, regular steps*.
- His first fitness clients: ~13 from posts + DMs to people he knew, paid via a donation to charity (2024 video).
**Mistakes he calls out:**
- Blasting a generic pitch. Personalise or don't send.
- Selling to friends instead of asking for referrals (the referral ask feels like a favour, not a solicitation).
- Resenting free clients. Frame it as *they're helping you*. With no track record, your time is worth zero today.
- Refusing free work on principle. If people won't take it even free, ask why: their reasons reveal hidden costs you can remove and later charge for.
**Ask the founder:** How many contacts across email/phone/social? One-line dream outcome? What will free work be traded for? Capacity?
**Pairs with:** Rule of 100; Marketing Machine (capture the testimonials); offers file (`02`) (guarantee); proof section of the sales file (`05` Proof Checklist).
**Sources:** YT "Watch this to get your first 5 customers" (2023) · YT "The Business Model That's So Simple…" (2023) · YT "13 Years of Marketing Advice in 85 Mins" (2024) · Research notes ($100M Leads, First 5 Clients module)

## Cold Outreach
**Use when:** warm list is exhausted, B2B sales, "write me a cold email/DM/call script", "nobody replies".
**Core idea:** Same one-to-one motion, but to strangers. Strangers don't trust you, so cold outreach has three problems: (1) you don't have their contact info, (2) you don't know what to say or offer, (3) not enough people reply. Fix each in turn.
**How to run it:**
1. **Build the list** — three ways:
   - Software: list tools filtered by your criteria (monthly fee, export).
   - Brokers: buy a list that matches your avatar; negotiate.
   - Elbow grease: scrape it yourself from Google Maps, Yelp, directories, Facebook groups, Reddit, forums. He prefers this long-term: it's a "virgin" list others aren't hammering.
2. **Lead with Big Fast Value (BFV).** Strangers need a bigger reason to say yes than friends. Have a free or near-free, high-value offer ready (often a lead magnet, audit or done-for-you sample).
3. **Personalise.** Five minutes of research per good lead: their site, profile, recent activity. Mention something specific early. People listen to someone who acts like someone they know.
4. **Borrow trust.** Name-drop a customer they know or a nearby result in the first seconds ("I work with the Johnsons next door…"). One free client can open a whole street or niche.
5. **Ask a question that wins either way**, e.g. "Do you have someone handling X?" Yes → "Are they doing it free? I will." No → bridge to your offer.
6. **Solve replies with volume, then automation.** Accept 100 or 1,000 no's per yes. Automate list building, dialling, sequencing and DMs where possible. Add people (setters) to multiply volume.
**Script structure (cold email / DM, fill-in):**
- Line 1, personal: "[Name] — saw [specific observation about their business, with a number if possible]."
- Line 2, proof match: "We had a [similar business] in the same spot. [Result] in [time]."
- Line 3, BFV: "I put together a [free audit/walkthrough/sample] showing how we'd apply it to [their company]."
- Line 4, low-friction ask: "Want me to send it over?" (Clear next step, one action.)
- Follow-ups: new angle or new proof each touch. Short. Multiple short messages beat one block.
**Script structure (door / cold call, fill-in):** look the part (visually signal what you do) → borrowed-trust opener ("I help [neighbour/peer] with…") → either-way question ("Do you have someone who does X?") → BFV offer → referral ask before leaving.
**Numbers & thresholds:** Rule of 100 applied: 100 cold reach-outs/day, automated where possible. Local area-code calling and "blue bubble" (real phone) texting raise pick-up and reply rates (Lead Nurture pro tips; tech will change).
**Worked example (illustrative):** IT services firm. Scrape 2,000 dental clinics in-state from Maps. Email: "Dr. Shah — your booking page took 9 seconds to load on my phone. A clinic in Austin fixed the same issue and booked 30% more new patients. I recorded a 3-min walkthrough for your site. Want it?"
**Mistakes he calls out:**
- Weak offer to cold traffic. Strangers need BFV, not "hop on a call".
- Quitting after a handful of no's. No is the price of yes.
- Only recycled lists; doing by hand what could be automated.
**Ask the founder:** Who's on the list and where do they gather? What free thing is valuable to a stranger in 10 minutes? Touches per day? Proof from a similar company?
**Pairs with:** Lead Magnets; Rule of 100; Pillar III Personalisation; proof section of the sales file (`05` Proof Checklist); file 06 (hooks — the first line of an email is a hook).
**Sources:** YT "The Business Model That's So Simple, Anyone Can Try It" (2023) · $100M Playbook: Lead Nurture · Research notes ($100M Leads cold outreach)

## Posting Free Content (Core Four #3)
**Use when:** "should I post content?", "how often?", "content isn't getting me clients".
**Core idea:** One-to-many to people who know you. Give so much value that they ask for more, free or paid. Slow and compounding; it also warms every other channel. Craft lives in file 06 (Hook-Retain-Reward, Content Strategy).
**How to run it:** Rule of 100 = ~100 minutes/day creating, publish ≥1/day (book, via research notes). Give far more than you ask (book-era guidance, secondary: TV runs ~47 min of show to 13 of ads, ~3.5:1). End with a CTA that offers *personalisation* ("want me to apply this to your business?"), not "more stuff". Put UTMs on links to see which pieces make money.
**Numbers & thresholds:** anecdotes he cites: two $1M+/yr businesses with <5,000 followers (2025); a registered-dietitian creator with ~5,800 followers making ~$1M/yr profit by posting only about insurance billing (2024).
**Mistakes he calls out:** posting weekly and expecting growth; judging by views; broad content that attracts non-buyers.

## Running Paid Ads (Core Four #4)
**Use when:** "should I run ads?", "ads stopped working", "what's a good CPL?", "how much should I spend?".
**Core idea:** One-to-many to strangers. Ads are an auction for attention: whoever makes the most gross profit per customer can outbid everyone and still win. Fix economics (back of the business) first, then optimise ads front to back (file 06).
**How to run it:**
1. **Unit economics first:** LTGP (lifetime *gross profit*, not revenue) vs all-in CAC (ads, sales labour, software). "Good CPL" benchmarks are irrelevant on their own.
2. **Cheapest wins first:** pixel + retargeting on every platform (his "shadow funnel") and bid on your own brand terms. He says he has never seen those lose money.
3. **Local:** lead-gen ads to a free or low-price entry offer (he suggests $19–29 for a skilled service), call every lead, take a card on file, sell the plan at the visit.
4. **Friction dial:** too many bad leads → add *good* friction (application, VSL, price on page); too few → remove friction; no friction and still bad → targeting/message is off.
5. **New channels take 3–12 months.** Track step by step (clicks → opt-ins → right people → shows → sales).
6. **Spend caps out = creative isn't good enough for colder awareness levels**, not a saturated market (file 06).
**Numbers & thresholds:** LTGP:CAC floor ~3:1 (book). Refinement (podcast Ep 990, Jan 2026, secondary summary): ~6–9:1 for businesses with humans in acquisition/delivery. Recover CAC within 30 days where possible. Rule of 100 = $100/day. Book (secondary): three phases — track money, lose money testing, print money; test budget ~2× 30-day cash per customer.
**Worked example (his numbers, flagged as made up):** $500 gross profit per bow, $20 leads, 1 in 5 buys → $100 CAC → 5:1. So $20 is a "good" lead cost only relative to that ratio.
**Mistakes he calls out:** CPL without gross profit; "Facebook is saturated" at a tiny share of competitors' combined spend; killing a channel after one month; no retargeting.
**Ask the founder:** Lifetime gross profit per customer? All-in CAC? CAC back in 30 days? New ads shipped last week?
**Pairs with:** file 06 (GOATed Ads, What to Measure); money-models file (`03`) and retention file (`07`); Lead Nurture (ads die when nobody calls the leads).
**Sources:** YT "Learn Paid Ads in 30 Minutes!" (2024) · YT "13 Years of Marketing Advice in 85 Mins" (2024) · $100M Playbook: GOATed Ads · Research notes (LTGP:CAC; $100M Leads paid-ads chapter)

## The Rule of 100
**Use when:** inconsistent lead flow, feast-and-famine months, "I don't know what to do each day", founder sets revenue goals and misses them.
**Core idea:** Make the *inputs* the goal. Do 100 primary advertising actions every day, for 100 days. He frames it as: "if you do 100 primary actions over 100 days you'll usually get the first result that you ultimately want" (YT, 2023).
**How to run it:**
1. Pick one Core Four activity.
2. Set the daily input:
   - Warm outreach: 100 reach-outs/day.
   - Cold outreach: 100 reach-outs/day (automate).
   - Content: 100 minutes/day creating; publish ≥1/day.
   - Paid ads: $100/day.
3. Score the day yes/no on the input, not on sales. No sale today still means 100 inputs today.
4. Count in hundreds. When someone says "I tried it", ask "how many hundreds?"
5. **Open to Goal** (book/course module): keep going until the day's number is hit.
**Numbers & thresholds:** In the Acquisition.com Scaling Roadmap, Stage 2 "Advertise" (headcount: just you) has the marketing constraint "lead flow is inconsistent". The fix named is to live by the Rule of 100 and prioritise advertising daily.
**Worked example (from source):** a client kept hitting $10–30k months, then coasting. Making daily activities the goal stopped the drop-offs.
**Mistakes he calls out:** output goals that let you coast after a good week; stopping when the calendar fills; doing outreach "when you need it".
**Ask the founder:** What's your one primary action? What number per day can you guarantee for 100 days? How will you log it?
**Pairs with:** Core Four; More, Better, New; scaling file (`08`) (Stage 2 Advertise).
**Sources:** YT "If I Wanted to Become a Millionaire In 2024…" (2023) · Acquisition.com Scaling Course, Stage 2 Advertise (third-party re-upload) · Research notes ($100M Leads, Rule of 100 / Open to Goal)

## More, Better, New
**Use when:** "how do I scale marketing?", "should I add TikTok/a new channel?", "growth has stalled".
**Core idea:** Scale in this order. **More** of what works until more stops paying. Then **better** (optimise the biggest drop-off). Only then **new** (new placement → new platform → new Core Four activity). New is last because its cost is guaranteed and its return isn't.
**How to run it:**
1. **More:** compare your volume with the leaders in your space. It's usually 10–100x what you do. Raise volume until something breaks.
2. Ask: "What would stop me from 10x-ing this right now?" Fix the first thing that would break, then add volume again.
3. **Better:** switch when one extra unit of "more" returns less than an improvement would. Example from source: going from 1 to 2 salespeople doubles capacity, 10 to 11 adds only 10%, but a +20–25% throughput tweak (show rate, headline, CTA) beats it.
4. Optimise the step with the biggest drop-off. Test one change at a time (book: one change per week per platform — secondary).
5. **New:** only when you can't do more or better (e.g. a local gym already hitting every person in its radius several times a week). Then try a new placement, then a new platform, then a new Core Four activity. Give it 3–12 months and track progress step by step.
**Numbers & thresholds:** Volume gaps he cites: a billionaire friend posting 1/day vs Acquisition.com's 50+/day; a portfolio CEO shipping 15 ads/month vs his 35/week when scaling (2024).
**Worked example (illustrative):** Coach at $60k/mo from IG content wants to "add YouTube". MBN says: double IG output first, then fix the DM-to-call step (biggest drop-off), *then* YouTube.
**Mistakes he calls out:** spending 80% of time on new things when 90% should go to more and better of what works; the "size of the pie" fallacy (thinking the market is saturated); judging a new channel after one month.
**Ask the founder:** What's working now, and how much more of it could you do? What's the biggest drop-off in your funnel? What would break first at 10x?
**Pairs with:** Rule of 100; GOATed Ads (more/better ads); constraints thinking in the scaling file (`08`).
**Sources:** YT "13 Years of Marketing Advice in 85 Mins" (2024) · Research notes ($100M Leads, More Better New)

## Lead Getters (people who do the Core Four for you)
**Use when:** founder is the bottleneck on lead gen, "how do I get referrals/affiliates", "should I hire an agency/SDR".
**Core idea:** Four types of people can run the Core Four on your behalf: **customers (referrals), employees, agencies, affiliates.** They give more leverage than doing it yourself, but you must first learn at least one Core Four activity yourself, so you can teach it and spot who's legit when someone says "that's not possible".

| Lead Getter | What they do | His tactics in the sources |
|---|---|---|
| **Customers (referrals)** | Tell friends; intros | Get them a win fast (book: first 48 hours, secondary). Ask at purchase and at "wow" moments. Ask "*who* do you know who's as awesome as you…?" (forces a name, not yes/no). Do three-way intros on the spot. Make the referral reward *social-capital positive*: give the friend something genuinely valuable (e.g. a free high-value treatment for them both), not a cash credit the customer doesn't care about. Book blurb: 7 direct referral methods = ~30% of his sales. |
| **Employees** | Run outreach, setting, content for you | Founder does it first, documents it, then trains. 3Ds = Document, Demonstrate, Duplicate (Lost Chapters; see team section of the scaling file (`08`)). Track and rank by show rate, close rate, lead-to-close. |
| **Agencies** | Run ads/content/outreach for you | A bad agency is a cost of doing business, not proof agencies don't work. Keep margin to try a couple. Know the work well enough to judge them. |
| **Affiliates** | Promote you to their existing audience | Skip super-affiliates. Collect many small businesses that already have your customers. Make it so good that it hurts: instead of 20% referral fees nobody acts on, let the partner sell a package of your service and **keep 100%** above a minimum price. You pay only delivery cost to get a pre-paid, qualified customer, then upsell. |

**How to run it (affiliate "peel-off" model):**
1. Peel one component off your core offer (e.g. 3 sessions, an audit, a starter kit).
2. Partner sells it to their customers for any price ≥ your floor and keeps all of it.
3. You deliver it (cost = your hard cost) and convert a share to your main offer.
4. CAC = delivery cost ÷ conversion rate. Recruit 100 partners.
**Numbers & thresholds (source example):** Massage package: partner sells 3 sessions for ≥$300 and keeps it all; your cost ~$90; 1 in 3 buys a $2,000 package → CAC ≈ $270 for $2,000.
**Worked example (illustrative, Fulcrum-type):** AI consultancy peels off a "2-hour automation audit". Accountancy firms sell it to their clients for ≥$500 and keep it. Audit costs ~$150 to deliver; 1 in 4 buys a $15k build.
**Mistakes he calls out:**
- Generic "I'll give you 20%" referral deals. Everyone agrees, nobody sends anyone.
- Brand matters to affiliates too: his friend wouldn't switch to his supplements despite 40% vs 10–20% commission, because the other brand was "cool" (Branding playbook).
- Outsourcing lead gen before you understand it.
**Ask the founder:** Share of customers by source today? Which businesses already serve your buyer? What piece of your offer could a partner resell?
**Pairs with:** Marketing Machine (customers as ad source); Branding (file 06); team/hiring file (3Ds).

## Lead Magnets
**Use when:** "my site just says book a call", "cold traffic won't convert", "what should I give away?", "how do I get more opt-ins without more ad spend?".
**Core idea:** A lead magnet is a mini-offer that **completely solves a narrow problem** and, by solving it, **reveals the next problem**, which your core offer solves. It converts more of the same traffic because most visitors aren't ready to buy. He puts it as: "when someone pays with time now, they're more likely to pay with money later" (YT, 2025).
**The deprivation logic (how "solve narrow, reveal next" works):** Sell at the point of greatest deprivation. Don't satisfy the hunger your paid offer satisfies (don't give away a second entrée). Satisfy the *preceding* need so the next one becomes urgent (the entrée creates room for dessert, and dessert is the expensive item). Book analogy: salty pretzels make you thirsty, and you sell the drink.
**How to run it (7 steps, book via secondary):**
1. **Pick the narrow problem and who it's for.** Choose one that comes right before your core offer.
2. **Pick the type** (all three "solve one problem and reveal others"):
   - **Reveal a problem (diagnosis):** audit, speed test, score, assessment. His favourite. Best when the problem *gets worse while waiting* (termites, posture, cash flow, tax), which builds urgency. Bonus: show the gap between where they are and where they could be.
   - **Sample / trial:** a taste, then removal creates deprivation. Limit uses, quantity or time. (Gym Launch gave 4 months of free agency leads to buyers of its monetisation system, then charged to keep the leads coming.)
   - **One step of a multi-step process:** first coat of paint, first laser session, first two videos of a course. Works best for complex offers.
3. **Pick the delivery** (can combine): software/tool (calculator, assessment, template), information (guide, mini-course, case study, interview), service (free audit, done-for-you sample, given only to qualified people), physical (book, merch to qualified people only).
4. **Name it and test the name** (the packaging moves response 2–10x more than the contents). Test via small ad split tests, or poll your audience/texts ("A or B?", only your avatar answers). He split-tested six headlines, plus images and subheads, for *$100M Leads*. Name formulas he gives:
   - Number + outcome + timeframe ("3 emails that turn cold leads into clients in 24 hours").
   - How to [good thing] without [bad thing], even if [insecurity].
   - The [adjective] [thing] that [result].
   - X mistakes that [are killing/keep you from] [goal].
5–6. **Make it easy to consume and genuinely good:** better than competitors' paid stuff; something you'd be willing to charge for.
7. **Make it easy to say you want more:** CTAs before, inside and after. CTA formula = clear (not clever) + exact next action + a reason to act now (scarcity, urgency, or *any* reason). Service businesses have real capacity limits, so state them.
**Qualify, don't fear freebie seekers:** add a qualifying drop-down/question. Send qualified people to the high-value magnet (e.g. a free service) and others to a cheaper one (e.g. a PDF).
**Numbers & thresholds:**
- Free-service math (source): ~$25 hard cost for something worth ~$250. 1 in 4 buys → $100 CAC.
- Merch to qualified CEOs: ~$3–4 shirt. 1 in 20 converts → ~$100 per CEO.
- His 2016 breakthrough: swapped a failing webinar for a free case-study video ("how we added 213 members and $112k to a small gym in San Diego"), and the calendar filled overnight. Sophisticated audiences see webinars as pitches.
**Worked example (illustrative, B2B):** AI agency. Magnet = "Free 15-minute Ops Leak Scan: the 5 manual tasks costing your team 20+ hours a week". It reveals the problem (diagnosis) and is delivered as a short form + a recorded walkthrough. The next problem it reveals is "we need these automated". CTA: "Want us to build the first one with you? 4 build slots this month."
**Mistakes he calls out:**
- "Lead magnets don't work." Bad lead magnets don't work, and beginners don't know theirs is bad.
- Solving the core problem for free (kills deprivation).
- Forgetting the CTA, or asking once. Salespeople who ask the most get the most deals. Add value between asks so you earn the right to ask again.
- Testing the contents more than the name/packaging.
**Ask the founder:** What problem comes right *before* the one you charge for? Can you diagnose it in under 15 minutes? What would make someone say "I need help with the next step"? How will you qualify who gets it?
**Pairs with:** Cold Outreach (BFV); Lead Nurture (call every opt-in in 5 min); offers file (`02`) (attraction offers); file 06 (naming = hooks).
**Sources:** YT "Watch This To Generate 1000s of Leads (In Any Niche)" (2025) · YT "13 Years of Marketing Advice in 85 Mins" (2024) · Research notes ($100M Leads: three types, four delivery mechanisms, seven steps)

## Lead Nurture: The Four Pillars
**Use when:** "people book but don't show", "leads go cold", "my setters/closers are slow", "how should we follow up?".
**Core idea:** Nurture is what happens after someone shows interest and before they buy. The Lead Nurture playbook targets one thing: **maximising 30-day show rates**. The playbook's epigraph: "If they do not show, they cannot buy." From ALAN (his appointment software; 4,000+ appointments/day) and his portfolio (~20,000 leads/day), four drivers correlate with shows:
1. **Availability** — how many slots exist and how far out leads can book.
2. **Speed** (to contact) — how fast you reach them and how soon the appointment is.
3. **Personalisation** — how relevant and useful the communication is *to them*.
4. **Volume** — how many times you reach out before giving up.
Then **Execution** — the culture that makes reps do the boring work.
**How to run it:** audit each pillar against the checklists in the next four cards; fix the worst pillar first. If show rate drops for one rep, check that rep's response timestamps first.
**Numbers & thresholds:** Third-party stats he cites: average response time ~42 hours vs his 42 seconds standard; reps give up after ~1.3 attempts on average and 44% stop after the first (InsideSales.com & RAIN Group).
**Mistakes he calls out:** believing show rates can't be changed; buying more leads before working existing ones.
**Ask the founder:** Current schedule rate and show rate? How fast is the first contact (minutes)? How many attempts before giving up? How far out can people book?
**Pairs with:** the four pillar cards below; sales file (`05`) (closing the shows).
**Sources:** $100M Playbook: Lead Nurture

## Pillar I — Availability
**Use when:** low schedule rate, "people say they'll book later", self-scheduler converts badly.
**Core idea:** If leads can't schedule, they can't show. Per his data analyst, availability (days × hours × slots per hour) was the *single biggest* predictor of throughput, ahead of speed. Diminishing returns exist, but gains held even at high availability.
**How to run it:**
1. **More days:** take appointments 7 days a week. Most competitors take bookings five days a week, so seven days makes you ~40% more available.
2. **More hours:** for US-national sales, 6am–6pm PT (= 9am–9pm ET) covered the most sales relative to hours.
3. **More start times:** four per hour (:00/:15/:30/:45) even if calls stay 30–60 min. Use separate 15-min qualifying calls and closing calls with separate calendars to remove awkward gaps.
4. **Three booking paths:** you call them (outbound), they call you (inbound), they self-schedule. Do all three.
5. **Self-scheduler page:** header = "Attention [avatar]", then headline, subheadline. Time slots visible immediately on mobile and desktop. Delete steps; never ask for info they already gave.
6. **Too many bad appointments?** Add *good* friction: video before the scheduler, sales letter above it, price on page, delayed scheduler reveal.
**Numbers & thresholds:** 20–40% (sometimes 200%+) more shown appointments from availability fixes, per his experience.
**Worked example (from source):** his early gym thank-you page moved from a generic "you'll get a text" to a photo of Leila asking people to text her a time (7 days, 7am–8pm). It converted far better: a face beat a faceless brand.
**Mistakes he calls out:** "I can't be open more hours" (really means "not worth it", and it usually is worth it); slots set for the business's convenience; making leads book at a bad time so they ghost and steal a slot from a buyer.
**Ask the founder:** Days and hours you take calls? Slots per hour? Can leads self-book from the opt-in page? What's on the page above the calendar?
**Pairs with:** Pillar II (gaps from 15-min slots become speed-to-lead time).
**Sources:** $100M Playbook: Lead Nurture

## Pillar II — Speed
**Use when:** slow follow-up, appointments booked a week out, leads "go cold".
**Core idea:** Three speeds: **to first contact**, **to first appointment**, **of response** between booking and the call. The faster you contact, the fewer attempts you need, so you work less and sell more.
**How to run it:**
1. **First contact:** gold standard is every lead contacted in under 5 minutes (extra credit: under 60 seconds). Staff for excess capacity; the calendar gaps from Pillar I fund it. Litmus test: at least once a day a lead should remark on how fast you were.
2. **First appointment:** limit booking to 3 days out (5 max). Same day > next day > day after.
3. **Pull appointments forward.** When no fresh leads are waiting, call self-scheduled leads to move them up. Five outcomes: no response (remove from calendar or double-book); unqualified (remove; give free resources or a downsell); qualified and free now (sell now, or hot-handoff to a closer); qualified and pulled forward; qualified and confirmed.
4. **Speed of response:** answer texts and questions immediately before the call. Use timestamps on every message and call to find slow reps.
**Pull-forward call structure (fill-in):**
- Permission opener: "[Name], it's [rep] from [company] on a recorded line — saw you booked [thing] for [day]. Calling to confirm a couple of details. Is now a terrible time?"
- Confirm WHO: "You're a [qualifier 1], right?"
- Confirm WANT: "And you're looking to [qualifier 2]?"
- STRUGGLE: "What have you tried so far? … What else?" → "So it's a priority to fix this, or [bad consequence] — fair?"
- Pull: "Good news: I think we can help. Better news: a slot opened at [today, time]… unless now works?"
- If it stays: "No problem, we just like offering more times. You're set for [day/time]. Any reason you couldn't make it?"
- Hot handoff (if a closer takes it): edify the closer (experience + people like them they've helped), confirm the time, then introduce by three-way text immediately.
**Numbers & thresholds (third-party stats he cites):** 391% more conversions when contacted within 60 seconds (Velocify). Contacting within an hour made qualification 7x more likely than an hour later and 60x more likely than after 24 hours (HBR). 78% buy from the first responder (Lead Connect).
**Worked example (from source):** gym leads asked which days they planned to work out; the rep used that day as the obvious day to come in. The same move works for any service: ask when they planned to do the thing and book then.
**Mistakes he calls out:** next-day replies to pre-call questions; letting people book a week out (ghosts kill rep morale and steal slots); slow reps hiding in averages.
**Ask the founder:** Median minutes from opt-in to first call? Furthest booking date allowed? Who answers texts after hours?
**Pairs with:** Pillar I; Pillar IV; sales file (`05`) (setter/closer split).
**Sources:** $100M Playbook: Lead Nurture

## Pillar III — Personalisation
**Use when:** low reply rates to follow-up, templated nurture, the wrong leads reach the best closers.
**Core idea:** Find out what each lead wants, prepare it for them, and tell them they get it when they show. That lowers their risk (they won't waste their time) and raises the cost of skipping.
**How to run it — six tactics:**
1. **Preferred channel:** start everywhere (call, text, DM, email). Continue wherever they reply first. Upgrade to a richer medium later. Reach out like you're genuinely trying to find a family member.
2. **Qualify:** application or questions before the call. Cancel appointments with clear non-fits to free time for likely buyers.
3. **Best leads → best closers:** (a) study your best customers' traits and actions; (b) ask those questions in opt-ins/applications; (c) score 1–5 or red/yellow/green; (d) route greens to top closers.
4. **Segment messaging.** Easy: 5 minutes of research on each good lead before contact (site, profile, recent news) and mention it early. Hard: build separate text/email sequences for each segment (revenue band, goal size), with case studies that match.
5. **Incentivise showing:**
   - **Push (gift):** send something small before the call whether or not they show (e.g. a $5 coffee gift card). It works through reciprocity, even when people know.
   - **Pull (A/B choice):** "Do you want the black or the pink shirt waiting for you?" Both options assume they show. Rules: they want it, it connects to the offer, and you visibly incurred a cost preparing it. Anything can be A/B (size, colour, start date, rep, format).
6. **Proof that matches them** (same age, gender, situation, size). Send it after booking and before the show, between the 1st and 2nd call, and every 90 days to ghosts (show results of people who started when they ghosted).
**Numbers & thresholds:** he cites HubSpot's 7x email ROI from segmentation, and a timeshare top rep whose best-leads-to-best-closers idea 5x'd an office (his retelling).
**Worked example (illustrative):** Coaching firm asks revenue band on the application; $1M+ leads go to the senior closer, get a case study from a $1–3M client and the A/B: "Want the hiring scorecard or the sales-comp template ready for our call?"
**Mistakes he calls out:** one channel, one attempt; templated messages that ignore what the lead told you; junior reps burning premium leads.
**Ask the founder:** What do your best customers have in common? Do you score leads? What could you prepare for them before the call?
**Pairs with:** proof section of the sales file (`05` Proof Checklist); sales file (`05`) (setter/closer routing); Pillar IV.
**Sources:** $100M Playbook: Lead Nurture

## Pillar IV — Volume (follow-up cadence, reminders, BAMFAM)
**Use when:** "how many times should I follow up?", "what reminders should we send?", second-call drop-off.
**Core idea:** Most attempts fail for boring reasons (bad timing, forgot, buried). They opted in, so they asked to be contacted. Volume has three parts: getting them scheduled, reminding them to show, and booking the next meeting during the current one.
**How to run it — scheduling cadence (front-loaded):**

| When | Action |
|---|---|
| Within 5 min of opt-in | Call. No answer → **double-dial** immediately (phones often block the first unknown call). Voicemail. Text right after. |
| Rest of day 1 | Double-dial + text two more times, a few hours apart. |
| Days 2–3 | Call twice a day (early, late). Text after the second call. |
| Days 4–7 | Call + text once a day (catches weekend responders). |
| After week 1 | Move to long-term nurture: free value with soft CTAs. If they re-engage, restart at the top. |

**Reminders:**
- **Automated** (say they're automated; people mind being lied to, not reminded): immediately (confirmation with date, time, who they meet, and the number/area code you'll call from), then 24h, 12h and 3h before. Use the lead's time zone.
- **Manual, from a real phone:** night before, morning of, ~60 min before. Several short messages, not one block. Keep the conversation going with Acknowledge-Compliment-Ask, and use replies to qualify or pull the time forward ("I've got 4pm today if that's easier — if not, no worries").
- **BAMFAM — Book A Meeting From A Meeting** (credited to Sharran Srivatsaa). Never end with "I'll send some times." Pick the next time on the call. Being vague about a next call is an objection; handle it. Checklist line: "BAMFAM: Book a meeting from a meeting. Never let a lead go to no-man's land."
**Numbers & thresholds:** Seven-plus attempts across channels. Including the call-from area code in reminders roughly doubles pick-up (his claim). A dialer makes reps 2–3x more efficient (his claim). BAMFAM fixed the first-to-second-call drop at an insurance company he bought.
**Worked example (illustrative):** reminder text: "You're booked with Priya at 2:00pm Thu (your time). We'll call from a 512 number. (Automated reminder — reply STOP to opt out.)"
**Mistakes he calls out:** quitting after one attempt; one long robotic message; "circle back later"; disguising automation as personal.
**Ask the founder:** Exact cadence today? What do reminders say? Does every call end with the next meeting booked?
**Pairs with:** Pillar II; Nurture Execution; sales file (`05`).
**Sources:** $100M Playbook: Lead Nurture

## Nurture Execution & Culture
**Use when:** "my team knows the process but doesn't do it", setters slack off, top closer won't work leads.
**Core idea:** Working leads is tedious, but the results aren't. Culture = the rules that make good behaviour normal. Reward available, fast, personal, persistent behaviour; reject slow, boring, fragile behaviour.
**How to run it:**
1. **Pay for it:** a small commission bump tied to show rate (roughly 5–30% of what a rep makes on a sale, depending on ticket).
2. **Status for it:** attention, affection and approval to top show-rate reps. When they say "I just followed the process," praise that too.
3. **Rank reps on three lists:** show rate (work ethic), close rate (skill), lead-to-close (everything). Good teams have the same names on top of all three.
4. **Spread the team mottos:** volume negates luck; yellow (less-qualified) leads are the new gold because they still close; be the "garbage man" who takes leads others won't, since every lead is free practice; book-a-meeting-from-a-meeting as a way of life; nobody is above the process.
5. Hire new reps as setters first. The best closers are usually the best setters.
**Numbers & thresholds:** Jacob story: team quota 150 calls/day, top rep 200, he was told to do 400; he finished top of 26 reps.
**Mistakes he calls out:** a high-close-rate rep who doesn't work leads is entitled and wasteful, so call it out; tracking close rate only.
**Ask the founder:** Do you rank reps by show rate and lead-to-close? Is anyone paid on shows? Who takes the "yellow" leads?
**Pairs with:** team/management file; sales file (`05`) (comp).
**Sources:** $100M Playbook: Lead Nurture

## Long-Term Nurture: Email
**Use when:** leads older than a week, "should I email my list?", "how often?".
**Core idea:** You already paid to acquire the contact. Email is cheap follow-up that reminds them you exist. He resisted it for years and now calls that a mistake.

| Lever | His rule |
|---|---|
| Cadence | ~3x/week seemed best in data he saw; more sends generally convert more (not a hard rule). |
| Format | Consistent template and series name (like podcast episodes), e.g. a recurring branded "money minute". |
| Content | Reuse proven winners (e.g. top tweets) as the immediate reward for opening. He writes or speaks his written content himself. |
| Subject + preview | Test subject lines in active split tests and log winners. Optimise preview text (it's the second headline). |
| CTAs | Two: a "continued interest" CTA and an "I want to buy" CTA. |
| List health | Make unsubscribing easy; mailing uninterested people hurts deliverability. Segment the list (he cites HubSpot: big ROI lift from segmentation). |

**Numbers & thresholds:** he cites industry email ROI of ~$35–45 per $1 (2024, time-bound).
**Pairs with:** Pillar IV (week 2+); file 06 (hooks — his email subject hooks).

## Marketing Machine (lead flow that runs without the founder)
**Use when:** "I'm the face of every ad", "I can't scale content", "how do I get marketing off my plate?", "is my business sellable?", "we need more ad creative".
**Core idea:** A business that depends on the founder's face is worth less and is fragile. Customers already create proof every day: sales calls, wins, reviews, events. Build checklists that **capture** that proof every week and turn it into ads, content and sales collateral. Hormozi's line: "The bottom of the list feeds the top of the list" — delivery activities (calls, events, community) feed the advertising.
**How to run it — the nodes (start with one; add one at a time):**

| Node | What you capture | Key steps |
|---|---|---|
| **Lifecycle** | *Before* clip (sales call), *during* clip (support call), *after* clip (upsell/milestone call) of one client | Consent language in contracts (and still ask). Record every call and turn off auto-delete. Put the client's name in calendar titles. Sales flags happy clients weekly. Marketing clips and labels the three moments. Local/physical businesses: capture "reveal" reactions (scale, new kitchen, veneers, "you got the job") as the hook. |
| **Social scrape** | Posts customers tag you in; your own top posts | Branded hashtag or tag. Grab stories before they expire. "Studs vs duds" folder: organic winners make good ads *and* outbound. |
| **Events** | Stage photos and video, pro testimonials, man-on-the-street clips | Separate recording room and crew. Pre-booked hit list on a clipboard. Handler bonus for extra captures. 10 core questions with 10+ answers each, cut into highlight reels. |
| **Chats, community, reviews** | Screenshots of wins in support chats, community "wins" posts, new 5-star reviews | Win keyword list (e.g. "closed", "profit", "leads"). Shared folder. Friday "wins of the week" post, Monday scrape. Screen-record scrolling through wins. Reward reps who submit (e.g. $100 gift card if a screenshot becomes a winning ad). |
| **Bonus unlocks** | Testimonial videos | Make a desirable paid bonus. Customers unlock it free by sending a <5-min video hitting the 6 points + posting stats in the group. |
| **Awards** | Four proofs per person | Aspirational milestone + a trophy people crave (his: sword and shield for "Gym Lords"). To redeem: (1) a results post, (2) an emailed video, (3) an unboxing video, (4) on-stage presentation. |
| **Competitions** | Raw phone testimonials | 1–2x/year max. Results template (start stats → moment of change → now stats). Winner = the video with the most conversions as an ad over 30 days. Big prize (e.g. 6 months free). Personally chase the top ~50. |

**The 6-point testimonial script (use for every capture):**
1. Internal struggle (rock bottom; what they hated, what they couldn't do)
2. External struggle (the numbers before)
3. Skepticism (what worried them about buying)
4. What overcame it (why they did it anyway)
5. External victory (the numbers now)
6. Internal victory (best moment; what they can do now; what they no longer have to do)
Plus: get several alternative **hooks** from them up front (the hook is the most important part), and have them say their name on camera.

**Implementation order if starting out (his):** competition → chat scrape → community scrape → review scrape → social scrape → bonus unlocks → award unlocks → events → lifecycle. The first gives a big influx; the next four use material you already have; lifecycle needs the most editing.
**Weekly operating rhythm:** marketing's start-of-week checklist pulls: names of clients with great results, social scrapes, chat screenshots, community wins, new reviews, bonus/award submissions, customer reaction videos. Events and competitions add a big batch every ~12 weeks if alternated.
**Numbers & thresholds:** At Gym Launch, 40 of the top 50 ads (80%) didn't have his face. Customer-made ads beat his best direct-to-camera ads. The system took him out of the ads for 12 months before the sale (closed at $46.2M). Pairs with the Proof Checklist finding that ~80% of winning ads were proof-led (file 06).
**Self-licking ice cream cone (2026 update):** engineer proof capture *into delivery itself* so content costs no extra time. Record client sessions, have AI pull the five most interesting moments from meeting transcripts, run audits for free and document them, use sweepstakes or reveal moments in product use. His comment on his own volume: "I'm usually not making content. We're just capturing it."
**Worked example (illustrative, AI agency):** record every kickoff (before) and 30-day review (after); a "1,000 Hours Saved" award redeemed with a stats post + 3-min video; Monday scrape of client Slack for "saved", "hours".
**Mistakes he calls out:** founder as permanent "camera slave"; collecting proof into folders and never running it; not rewarding staff who capture (they stop); waiting for "enough customers" (if few, do free work per Warm Outreach to get the first testimonials).
**Ask the founder:** Are all client calls recorded with consent? When does a client have their "wow" moment, and is anyone there to capture it? What do you already do (events, community, reviews) that could be scraped weekly? Who owns the checklist?
**Pairs with:** file 06 (GOATed Ads: testimonial/faceless formats; Proof in Ads; Hooks); proof section of the sales file (`05` Proof Checklist); Lead Getters (customers).
**Sources:** $100M Playbook: Marketing Machine · YT "The New Way of Making Content In The Age of AI" (2026) · YT "If I Wanted To Scale A Service Business In 2026…" (2026) · $100M Playbook: Proof Checklist

## Quick Procedures (for common requests)
- **"Write me a warm outreach message":** get avatar, dream outcome, timeframe, guarantee, free slots. Output: personal opener, ACA follow-up, referral ask ("taking on 5 case studies"), one-line elevator version. Remind: 100/day, 3 attempts, three-way intros.
- **"Write me a cold email/DM":** get list source, one specific observation per lead type, closest matching proof, BFV asset. Output the 4-line structure + 3 follow-ups, each with a new angle or proof. Remind: volume + automation fix reply rates.
- **"My show rate is bad":** collect the four pillar numbers. Fix in order: first contact <5 min → book within 72h → reminders with call-from number → A/B pull incentive → more availability.
- **"Design me a lead magnet":** find the problem just *before* the paid offer → type (diagnose/sample/one step) → delivery → 5 names from the four formulas → qualifier → CTA (clear + next action + reason now) → call every opt-in within 5 minutes.
- **"I'm the marketing bottleneck":** install one Marketing Machine node this week (competition or chat scrape), give someone the weekly capture checklist, and keep the founder on the 10% experimental hooks only.
