# 08 — Scaling Stages & Ops (Acquisition.com $100M Scaling Roadmap + people/ops)

**When to open this file:** "what stage am I in / what next", "stuck at N people or $X", "who do I hire first/next", "how do I train, delegate, manage, fire", "what's my bottleneck", "too many priorities", "when do I add a second product/channel", org structure, KPIs, meetings, finance/HR hygiene, founder role.
**Rule zero:** place the business in ONE stage (headcount first, symptoms second), name the single constraint, give the stage's bottom-line action, list what NOT to do yet.
**Pairs with:** offers file (Stage 4 niche/V2), leads file (Rule of 100, Core Four, referrals), money-models file (Stage 5 backend, LTGP:CAC), sales file (CLOSER, KPIs).

## Core thesis of the roadmap
**Use when:** any scaling question; frame before stage advice.
**Core idea:** Scaling needs three things. **Functions** are what must get done. **People** are who does it (ICs, managers, directors). **Operations** are the glue between them: meetings, communication, training. The stages are cut by **headcount, not revenue**: org structure and required functions are consistent across businesses, while revenue depends on the opportunity.
**How to run it:**
1. Count full-time employees. Freelancers and contractors don't count.
2. Place them in a band (master table). Confirm with that stage's symptoms.
3. Find the one constraint and prescribe the bottom line.
4. Study only the current stage, the one before (make sure it's finished) and the one after (so you don't build something you'll reverse).
**Numbers & thresholds:**
- Eight functions are tracked at every stage. **Product:** what you deliver. **Marketing:** telling people about your stuff. **Sales:** getting people to give you money for it. **Customer Service:** the "4 Rs", getting customers to retain, review, refer and resell. **IT:** gather, store, analyse and display information. **Recruiting:** find and hire. **HR:** lower the odds that employees or regulators sue you. **Finance:** report and manage money.
- Same headcount, very different revenue (course example): a restaurant and a software company each have 35 staff; one does $2M, the other $100M.
- Course claim: at ~500 employees, $100M/yr is "very likely". There are no official revenue bands. Course asides on revenue (approx): Stage 4 product pain at ~$1–3M/yr; Stage 5 ~$3M/yr, sometimes $10M; Stage 7 ~$1–2M/month.
**Mistakes he calls out:**
- Consuming every stage and trying to do it all. Quote: "a lot of companies actually die by indigestion, not starvation." (Lesson 1)
- Treating the stages as law. They are guidelines.
- Being ahead in one function and ignoring a lagging one. The lagging function becomes the weak link. The function you're ahead in is probably not your constraint.
**Ask the founder:** What did you build early that you're now undoing?

## Stage 0 — Improvise (0–1, Researcher)
**Use when:** no product yet; nobody has used it, even for free.
**Core idea:** You know nothing and have nothing. Research: give something away, learn what people value. Quote: "Our objective at this point is really to learn more than it is to earn." (Lesson 3)
**How to run it:**
- **Product:** make a free thing in one of four forms: software, media, service (knowledge- or time-based), physical. It should do one of three jobs: reveal a problem (assessment), free trial, or one free step of a multi-step process. Unscalable 1:1 is fine.
- **Marketing:** Core Four, starting with **warm outreach** (cheapest and fastest). Message everyone who knows you. 100–200 followers is already ~100 warm leads.
- **Sales:** ask their problem, offer to solve it free, and in return ask for feedback, then a review or referral. Hunt the **hidden (non-money) costs** stopping them: things they must start doing, things they must stop doing, risk, effort/time, delay. Remove everything that sucks about getting the result.
- **Customer service:** be available, respond fast, do any job. Ruthless execution is the only edge a worse product has.
- **IT:** computer, internet, phone, email: enough to sell and deliver.
- **Recruiting:** "recruit yourself". Build work capacity (long days for 6–12 months is the norm in their stories). ~20 focused hours makes you good enough at most skills; 10 hours of testing beats 10 years of reading.
- **HR:** form an entity (US: LLC/S-corp; a couple hundred dollars via attorney or online service).
- **Finance:** business bank account, separate business and personal cards, write off legitimate startup costs. Course: average small business costs ~$3,500 to start.
**Mistakes he calls out:** "I should post content instead" when the real block is fear of judgment; selling before you have proof; thinking about scale.
**Graduation:** people willingly try it free and you have feedback to build V1.
**Ask the founder:** Who outside friends has used it? What hidden cost stops the rest? Separate entity and account?
**Sources:** Scaling Roadmap Lesson 3 "Stage 0 Improvise"

## Stage 1 — Monetize (0–1, Starter)
**Use when:** free users, no revenue; "how do I get my first paying customer".
**Core idea:** Cross into being a business: one dollar through the pipeline. No process or scripts yet.
**How to run it:**
- **Product:** fix V1 until sellable. Ask non-buyers "what would it take?" and work back into features. Set the price first, then ask what would make them excited at that price (course example, illustrative: $3,000, 3 months, lose 20 lb). Price changes what you're willing to do, so charge a premium early and remove hidden costs for premium buyers.
- **Marketing:** proof beats promise. Alex won't sell before he has **10+ testimonials**. Proof strength ranking: in-person > virtual, live > recorded, raw > polished, show > tell, others say it > you say it, looks like the prospect > doesn't, specific with numbers > generic, new > old, lots > one, big and small results > none. Grab free ad credits ($50–500 per platform, as stated).
- **Sales:** warm-outreach beta offer: only taking ~5 people, 80% off, in exchange for using it, giving feedback and leaving a review if earned. **Raise price 20% every 5 sales:** free → 80% off → 60 → 40 → 20 → full, then keep going. Gym Launch's packages climbed from $6k to $50k+ this way. No yeses? Check (1) you're showing proof, (2) you're actually asking with conviction.
- **Customer service:** talk to every user. Tag each complaint as a result issue or an experience issue. Course claim: people stay with a good experience and a bad result more often than the reverse. Fix results with expectations, goals and a communication cadence.
- **IT:** free tools found through groups, forums, search.
- **Recruiting:** freelancers (Upwork/Fiverr-type) for skill or time gaps. Needs change too fast for a good full-time hire yet.
- **HR:** vendor agreement test: who does what, where, by when, cost, for how long, who says it's good, what happens if it isn't.
- **Finance:** payment processor plus at least two ways to get paid; confirm a deposit lands.
**Mistakes he calls out:** worrying about scale at zero customers; weak, old, generic testimonials; hoping a freelancer reads your mind.
**Graduation:** first paid sale deposited.
**Ask the founder:** How many testimonials with numbers? What did the last no say it would take? Can you take a card today?
**Sources:** Scaling Roadmap Lesson 4 "Stage 1 Monetize"

## Stage 2 — Advertise (0–1 + freelancers, Doer)
**Use when:** paying customers, but lumpy months; solo founder with contractors.
**Core idea:** Lead flow is inconsistent because you are. Advertise daily, be in the dirt, and make delivery good by hand. Quote: "valuable over scalable" (Lesson 5).
**How to run it:**
- **Product:** over-deliver manually until it's actually good.
- **Marketing:** **Rule of 100** every day. Outreach: 100 dials, DMs or emails. Content: ~100 minutes (post, DM everyone who engages, leave 50–100 top-quality comments on posts your audience reads). Ads: $10–100/day plus 100 minutes on creative. Never stop when you're full.
- **Sales:** one script for setting and closing (CLOSER). Pitch in **three pillars**, each with a one-line analogy, in 2–3 minutes; spend the rest on them. Run the pain cycle ("what else have you tried?") until exhausted. Two-step sale: the setter does only Clarify-Label-Overview and hands off while the prospect is hungry. Keep scripts human with **Acknowledge, Associate, Ask**. The five concerns are time, money, decision maker, stall, preferences.
- **Customer service:** **angry boat**: only one person can be in it, so get more upset on their behalf than they are and they de-escalate. Then **HEAR**: Hear (let them vent), Empathize, Apologize, Resolve with three options (refund, reschedule, upgrade at your cost) and let them pick. Testimonials via the **epiphany bridge** interview: internal struggle → external metrics → skepticism → why us, why now → internal and external victory.
- **IT:** lead-capture page, starter CRM, appointment reminders (manual is fine).
- **Recruiting:** offer overloaded part-timers full-time roles; post on social, Indeed, LinkedIn.
- **HR:** classify correctly. US: payroll employees (W-2) vs contractors (1099). Course rule of thumb on the IRS 20-factor test: ~5 "yes" answers means probably an employee.
- **Finance:** bookkeeping software, a CPA, quarterly tax saving, check the bank daily. Fast feedback beats precise reports here.
**Mistakes he calls out:** stopping advertising when busy; 20-minute pitches; backing down from upset customers; misclassifying workers.
**Graduation:** predictable new customers from daily volume; enough work for a full-time hire.
**Ask the founder:** What did you do yesterday for leads, and how many? Written script? Today's bank balance?
**Sources:** Scaling Roadmap Lesson 5 "Stage 2 Advertise"

## Stage 3 — Stabilize (1–4, Trainer)
**Use when:** first 1–4 employees; founder drowning; volume creating cracks.
**Core idea:** Workload exceeds resources, so get help and train it. The first hires set the bar and the culture; a wrong direction here costs a painful redo at 3–4× the size.
**How to run it:**
- **Product:** force-rank fixes by impact × investment × likelihood. Shortcut: fix the #1 reason people cancel. Longer term, find the **activation** point (Gym Launch: attending the first coach call). Prefer fewer, bigger wins over quick-win busywork like a website redesign. Put a money tag on the big problem (e.g. what halving churn is worth).
- **Marketing:** add long-term nurture for colder leads. Email at least weekly (3×/week segmented is ideal): value first, then a CTA, with a PS that trains the click. Belief-breaking email: wrong belief → why it's wrong → what's right → proof. Content: lists, steps or stories drawn from your past, last week, trends.
- **Sales:** define who you WON'T sell, by studying your worst customers. Gym Launch qualifiers: small fitness gym, ≥3 employees, ≥30 clients. Plus BANT. **Outsource setting/calendaring** first.
- **Customer service:** consistent onboarding = the fastest route to activation. Set goals and expectations, give a personal success path, get a quick first win, fit their workflow, set a feedback cadence, book the next meeting at the end of each call, then hand off to ongoing support.
- **IT:** cut unused tools. Basic pipeline numbers: opt-ins, scheduled, showed, offers, closed, cash, backouts.
- **Recruiting:** write the job ad like an ad: dream-job hook (why here), values, top 3 responsibilities, what's in it for them. A portfolio company went from ~10 applicants/week to ~30/day by changing the headline and first line. Screen résumés for achievements with objective proof. Target ~20% passing to the screening call; ~0% means the ad is wrong. Hire for the next 12–18 months, not for Stage 7 (overqualified big-company hires import the wrong habits).
- **HR:** payroll provider (Gusto/ADP/Paychex-type), especially with staff in several states.
- **Finance:** billing and collections process, bills inbox (ap@/finance@). Course: few roles pay for themselves faster than a collector.
**Mistakes he calls out:** fixing everything at once; founder still booking calls; Venmo payroll.
**Graduation:** trained hires carrying real work; team approaching 5.
**Ask the founder:** Top cancel reason? Who books your calls? What % of applicants pass your screen?
**Sources:** Scaling Roadmap Lesson 6 "Stage 3 Stabilize"

## Stage 4 — Prioritize (5–9, Manager)
**Use when:** 5–9 people; many customer types; Frankenstein offers; unqualified leads; no numbers.
**Core idea:** You said yes to anyone with money, and now you're everything to everyone. Niche to your best customers and build V2 for them. Quote: "you generalize before you specialize." (Lesson 7)
**How to run it:**
- **Product:** **80/20 the customer base**. The best 20% pay most, have the highest LTV, take the least effort and refer most. Pick them by data, not by preference or because you have the most of them. Course example: an agency owner wanted plastic surgeons, but law firms had ~2× the LTV and barely needed support. Rebuild the offer and price for that avatar. Value rises because of *who* buys (course ladder, illustrative: the same time-management course at $19 generic, $99 for sales pros, $1k for B2B outbound reps, $10k for outbound reps in power tools). Don't sprawl into doing everything for the client.
- **Marketing:** better free stuff with a real hard cost competitors can't match, gated by qualification. Message match from ad to close. Sharper callouts (e.g. firms with ≥5 attorneys). More creative volume. Add friction (Gym Launch: an employee, 30 members, a signed lease). Don't open a second channel.
- **Sales:** restore speed to contact. Weekly KPI sheet in Google Sheets: contact, schedule, cancel, show, offer, close rates, cash collected, per rep. **Show-rate system:**
  1. Thank-you page headline sells the call, with truthful scarcity only.
  2. Scheduler above the fold.
  3. 15-minute slots, 7 days, 9am–9pm. In ALAN data, availability had the #1 correlation with sales.
  4. Setters paid 3% of closed sales.
  5. Labeled automated reminders plus manual ones at 24h and 1h (rep-sent messages showed ~2× better than automated).
  6. Daily kudos for leading indicators (status motivates more than money).
  7. A show-up incentive.
  Show-rate lifts come easier than close-rate lifts and pay the same.
- **Customer service:** KPIs: NPS (scale −100 to +100), CSAT, logo and revenue churn, refunds and chargebacks. Goal: net negative churn. Keep notes per customer in a CRM ("one brain") and review them for ~5 minutes before every call.
- **IT:** one chat tool, a password manager, shared project management. If everyone doesn't use it, it fails.
- **Recruiting:** a 5–15 minute expectations screen (pay, hours, location), then one interview for culture, skill (the top 3–5 skills) and potential, then ~5 reference calls (1–2 hours). Candidates are better at lying than you are at detecting it.
- **HR:** employee handbook covering values, conduct, hours, pay, leave, performance, harassment, tech use and exit; every hire signs it.
- **Finance:** P&L and cash-flow statement (profitable on paper can still miss payroll), general liability insurance.
**Mistakes he calls out:** going wide instead of deep; fear of saying no to money; a second channel to dodge fixing the first; praising only closes.
**Graduation:** one avatar, a V2 offer priced to it, KPIs tracked weekly, handbook signed.
**Course case:** a 14-location medical-service portfolio company did ONE thing first: specialised and tripled price (LTV ~4×). Only then did it fix the upsell script.
**Ask the founder:** Which 20% of customers are best, and what share of revenue are they? Show rate and close rate last month? How many offers do you sell?
**Sources:** Scaling Roadmap Lesson 7 "Stage 4 Prioritize"

## Stage 5 — Productize (10–19, Director)
**Use when:** 10–19 people; customers have nothing else to buy; CAC caps spend; refunds from over-promising; first managers.
**Core idea:** LTV:CAC is too low. Sell a second, premium product to existing customers so you can outspend competitors. You now direct managers.
**How to run it:**
- **Product:** connect CS to product to hear what customers want next. **Backend ≈5× the core price, bought by ~20%** (the best customers are "fractal": 20% of them can spend 5× more). Illustrative: 100 × $1k plus 20 × $5k doubles revenue. Best upsells, in order: more of the thing, more help with it, a better or longer version. Eight levers on customer value: raise price; buy again/churn less; cross-sell; cut delivery cost; upsell quality; upsell quantity; downsell quality; downsell quantity. Downsells raise revenue per lead, not LTV.
- **Marketing:** raise the CAC ceiling (illustrative: $1k → $3k). Score leads by who they are, what they did and their source. Install attribution. CRO in order: headline/subhead → offer → image → bullets → proof → above-the-fold/page order; several small lifts compound. Track **30-day cash collected** per customer.
- **Sales:** sales aids (VSL, deck, calculator) move the repeated education before the call. That shortens calls and steadies close rates. Track **NPS, CSAT and refund rate by rep**: refunds usually trace to 1–2 reps who "sell hot". Rule: state the facts and tell the truth; if the truth isn't compelling, change reality.
- **Game tape (the "five A's"):** Arrange 60–90 minutes with the heads of sales, product, marketing and CS. Assemble one recording (lost or won). Assess what each department takes from it. Analyze: one change per department. Act within 7 days. Run weekly; daily in a crisis. No behaviour change means there was no point watching.
- **Customer service:** a CS playbook. Sort questions into 3–5 categories and template the answers *before* hiring more reps. An empowerment list of ~4 delight actions reps can take without approval, up to a cap. **Ascension path:** up to 5 ask points: right after purchase, 24–48h after first value, after the first big win, halfway, a last-shot. Rule: **right product, right time, right way.**
- **IT:** company-owned devices (~$2–3k per person, as stated); IP and data live on company systems.
- **Recruiting:** promote from within *deliberately*. Put internal candidates through the normal process, or run a 60–90-day trial where every job-description item is scored red/yellow/green and all must be green. Quote: "Do not put people in a role out of convenience, do it out of intention." (Lesson 8) **Employee referrals:** referred hires stay ~70% longer (as stated). Agencies charge ~20% of salary, so pay staff about half that (their bonus grew from $2k to $5k).
- **HR:** firing process. No surprises: warn with red/yellow/green language first. Plan coverage before the conversation. Time it for the least disruption (end of day, not before a big sales day). Use a script. Close loose ends by email (pay, benefits, devices). Judge fit as culture, skill and will.
- **Finance:** budget for big expenses; 6–12-month cash forecast; track write-offs.
**Mistakes he calls out:** no backend ("I hate money"); best IC promoted by convenience (Peter Principle); firing as a surprise.
**Graduation:** backend selling; higher CAC ceiling; game tape and per-rep quality metrics running.
**Ask the founder:** What do customers buy next, elsewhere? LTV vs CAC? Refund rate by rep? How was your first manager chosen?
**Sources:** Scaling Roadmap Lesson 8 "Stage 5 Productize"

## Stage 6 — Optimize (20–49, Leader)
**Use when:** 20–49 people; two products splitting attention; CAC up on cold traffic; close rate slipping; churn up; spend not producing growth.
**Core idea:** Grow by getting better, not doing more. Move from deciding by intuition to deciding by information. Quote: "if you try to perfect everything at once, everything becomes mediocre." (Lesson 9)
**How to run it:**
- **Product:** list every problem across both products. Rank by gain, cost and risk. The whole team fixes ONE at a time, then the next. Solve existing problems before chasing opportunities. **Pruning survey:** "If you could keep only one feature, which?" and "Which could we remove with no difference to you?" Quietly delete the bottom one. Course example: Gym Launch cut a 35-person tech-support team that ranked lowest, and churn and CSAT held.
- **Marketing:** a weekly **ad assembly line**: 50 hooks × 3–5 bodies × 1–3 CTAs = 150–750 variants. Most effort goes to hooks. Source hooks in this order: your ad winners, your content winners, others' ad winners, others' content, ad libraries. Bodies sell the value drivers; show the outcome, not the mechanism. End by showing the next page.
- **Sales:** a training system: best-call recordings, the perfect script, a tone guide. New-rep coaching: role-play twice a day in week 1, daily in week 2, weekly after; strongly consider cutting a rep who isn't productive by end of week 2. Team game tape: the rep critiques themselves first, others say only what went right, then the manager drills the gap. Cadence: daily huddle, weekly team meeting, weekly 1:1.
- **Customer service:** cohort churn chart by sign-up month, product, rep and CSM. Intervene 1–2 months *before* the churn point. Activation is the biggest churn lever ("five Ms"): map the journey, measure what the best customers do, make a hypothesis, monitor one test for ~90 days, modify the offer. Gym Launch clients who brought their operator onto calls retained ~2×. Never make cancelling hard.
- **IT:** every account on company email; one-click offboarding. Five protocols: firewall, encryption, MFA, patching, least-access. Cyber and business-interruption insurance.
- **Recruiting:** headhunt (LinkedIn Recruiter) and always ask "who do you know?" Senior roles get a **four-way fit**: expectations screen, culture, skill, and potential (the founder). Under 50 people the founder is in every management hire. Manager references: 4–5 calls of 30–45 minutes, with follow-ups on every answer. Expect 3–4 comp conversations; it's normal.
- **HR:** benefits (as stated: ~15–30% of payroll, often better retention per dollar than salary). US: required above 50 employees. Advanced comp tied to 1–2 metrics the person most controls. Illustrative bonus caps: manager ≤25%, director ≤75% of salary. Delay complex comp until hiring or retention actually breaks.
- **Finance:** gross margin and LTV:CAC per product; a department cost/return dashboard; balance sheet; quarterly taxes; collect AR; pay core vendors on time.
**Course case (anonymous portfolio company):** close rate 14.1% → 34.9%, ROAS 1.62 → 3.96, profit margin 5% → 30%, from funnel, script, nurture and journey fixes.
**Mistakes he calls out:** concurrent fixes; adding features; chasing opportunities over problems; announcing deletions; piling on reps in game tape.
**Graduation:** owners and improvement pipelines per product; stable CAC through creative volume; consistent closes; falling churn by segment.
**Ask the founder:** Who owns each product? New creatives per week? Churn by cohort, and in which month? Can you say how each department makes money?
**Sources:** Scaling Roadmap Lesson 9 "Stage 6 Optimize"

## Stage 7 — Categorize (50–99, Executive)
**Use when:** 50–99 people; product outdated and slow to improve; one acquisition channel; wasted leads; CS firefighting; team spending badly.
**Core idea:** Everything is scattered. Triage it into buckets (ATS for applicants, HRIS for staff, cloud for data, budgets for spend). Three leadership layers; the exec team is usually 1–3 people, hired where the founder is weakest (Gym Launch's first exec was a CFO).
**How to run it:**
- **Product:** **one problem, one team, one measurable objective**, in strict sequence. Five things at once take far longer than five in a row. Guard attention, not just time. Leave the working core alone: change guarantees a cost, not an upside.
- **Marketing:** NOW add the **second acquisition channel**, one of the hardest transitions in business. Expect ~6–12 months to profit. Fund it with near-free cash: a stronger referral process and nurture segmented by lead score (~6–7 segments). Risk ladder, lowest first: new audience on the same platform and placement → new placement → nearest-analog platform → other formats there → a new Core Four method. Gym Launch's move to outbound took a year to reach 50% of revenue. Staffing: don't split the channel-one owner, and never put a new hire on a new channel. Quote: "We don't put a new on a new" (Lesson 10). Use a zero-to-one person; the founder stays involved.
- **Referral plays** (from $100M Leads, as taught here): pay the referrer ~your CAC; two-sided split; ask on every sales call; trade discounts for referrals; quarterly referral events; promo cycles (e.g. double-bonus days); unlockable status tiers.
- **Sales:** coverage drives throughput. Open 7 days, match the hours to your buyer, use local numbers. New reps get the worst shifts; top closers pick theirs. Hire reps when the coverage math is positive. Lead-nurture checklist; kudos for show rate as well as close rate.
- **Customer service:** reactive to proactive. Map a journey line for each R. Fire time-, behaviour- and event-based triggers from the CRM. Do retain and review first; referrals and resells follow.
- **IT:** cloud, central backup, central hardware, folder and naming conventions.
- **Recruiting:** an ATS chosen for UX, integrations, automation, funnel analytics, and fit for 18–36 months of growth. Specialist recruiting firms for high-volume repeat roles. Vet them on placement rate and speed (the best candidates are gone in ~9 days) and trial one role first.
- **HR:** HRIS (as stated, ~$50–100k/yr) with performance management. Operationalise the culture that already works.
- **Finance:** a spend SOP (course example: named leaders may spend ≤$10k on defined categories without approval; above that goes up a set path). Budgets set after seeing real spend. Cards with hard limits. Marketing uncapped while $1 in returns more than $1. A 12-month opex projection run by a full-time finance hire.
**Mistakes he calls out:** five priorities at once; the channel-one owner running channel two; killing the second channel at month 6 as a "shiny object".
**Graduation:** funded second channel live; feature teams shipping; proactive CS; ATS, HRIS and budgets in place.
**Ask the founder:** % of customers from the top channel? What's the ONE company priority this quarter? Who can spend without asking, and how much?
**Sources:** Scaling Roadmap Lesson 10 "Stage 7 Categorize" · $100M Leads (referral plays)

## Stage 8 — Specialize (100–249, President)
**Use when:** 100–249 people; feature bloat; diluted brand; premium close rate down; journeys colliding; recruiters lowering the bar.
**Core idea:** No one can know everything and focus is too broad. Build dedicated people and teams per task, and allocate by pruning. You lead a team of teams.
**How to run it:**
- **Product:** **survey, prune, improve through deletion.** Pruned features become add-ons for the few who want them. Delete until you have to add ~10% back (Elon rule, cited). Map features on a value × willingness-to-pay grid: high/high is pricing power; high value/low WTP is table stakes; low value/high WTP becomes an add-on; low/low gets cut. Look to customers, not competitors.
- **Marketing:** own **1–3 words** in the market's mind. Repeat one message a hundred ways. B2B: free value big enough to pay for itself many times over before purchase. B2C: entertainment and on-value endorsers.
- **Sales:** score leads and route the best to the best closers (front end and ascensions). Automate metrics by platform, rep and product. Contact within 60 seconds. Run a pyramid: juniors learn on weaker leads.
- **Customer service:** map every purchase path (A→B, B→A…). Aim for ~3 parallel journeys, not 17 colliding ones. Tag customers by path. Cut low-LTV, high-effort journeys. Gym Launch: selling straight into the back end churned within a quarter.
- **IT:** embedded ops roles (MarTech, Sales/CS/Rev/Fin Ops), ticketing and SLAs per department. Don't try to spin internal tools into a SaaS valuation.
- **Recruiting:** the same 5–10 questions per role; panels that include people with no urgency to fill it; a scorecard of 5 skills plus each core value, scored 1–5. Founder does a ≥20-minute final (Alex stacks 5–6 in one morning a week) and probes where interviewers disagree. Interview skill is not job skill.
- **HR:** a performance tool: 1:1 notes, weekly feedback on managers, a weekly company pulse, weekly red/yellow/green goal tracking. Leadership training ~2×/month on what the values are and aren't. EPLI insurance. Who you hire, promote and fire is the real culture.
- **Finance:** founder leads a quarterly subscription cleanse. Get 3 competing quotes for every vendor and renegotiate. Put idle cash in yield (illustrative: 5% on $10M = $500k/yr). Internal audits: theft is most likely from inside. Founder reads the P&L monthly plus a 1–2-hour line-by-line session with finance, who annotate every change. Specialist tax roles.
**Mistakes he calls out:** adding features; copying competitors; fresh messaging every campaign; founder leaving recruiting too early.
**Graduation:** lean core with add-ons; routing by closer quality; a standard hiring bar; audited, renegotiated finances.
**Ask the founder:** Which features does <X% use? Do your best leads reach your best closers within a minute? Do you still meet final-round hires?
**Sources:** Scaling Roadmap Lesson 11 "Stage 8 Specialize"

## Stage 9 — Capitalize (250–500, Chairman)
**Use when:** 250–500 people; changing the cash cow makes it worse; growth plateau; attribution chaos; books not investor-grade.
**Core idea:** You're an investor allocating capital across divisions (each with its own marketing, sales and CS). Make the big bet on the next rising star.
**How to run it:**
- **Product:** use the BCG lens (stars, cash cows, question marks, dogs). Only maintain the cow. **Buy** when speed, licences or relationships matter, or for a platform roll-up; it's a good sign if customers are already shared. **Build** when it's core to the future (better long-term, same culture). Acqui-hiring a competitor's #2 can beat buying the company. Look 1–3 years out.
- **Marketing:** brand-first, omni-channel, judged on **blended ROAS** (a 20:1 direct-response number may be ~4–5:1 blended, as stated). Course story: New Balance shifted to ~70% brand spend and took ~18 months to pay off. When in doubt, go long-term. Every asset passes a compliance checklist signed off by a manager. Not advertising is still the bigger risk.
- **Sales:** specialise reps by product, prospect and territory, each with its own comp and training. Specialising shortens training.
- **Customer service:** specialised reps routed by tags and channel; a CS career ladder by tier; standardised notes so customers never repeat themselves.
- **IT:** ERP; a tech "czar" per function; IT as an internal vendor with intake and SLAs.
- **Recruiting:** the founder personally courts rainmakers. This can't be outsourced to a recruiter. It takes months to years.
- **HR:** strategic workforce planning over 12–18 months (course example: 315 → 500 people). Managers rate their people red/yellow/green to feed promote, upskill or hire decisions. A RACI per team exposes overloaded and underused leaders. At scale, monthly attrition needs constant backfill (Lesson 2 example: 5% a month at 250–500 staff is 12.5–25 people).
- **Finance:** **prep for sale** even if you're not selling: quality of earnings, ask investment bankers what's wrong, third-party audits, legal entity and capital structure review, hedging, credit lines (course example: $20M) or strategic partners.
**Mistakes he calls out:** turning the cash cow into the star; chasing ROAS alone; delegating top recruiting.
**Graduation:** the big bet is placed and financed from clean books.
**Ask the founder:** Where does the next 10× come from? Would you pass a QoE review today? Which top-3 hires are you personally courting?
**Sources:** Scaling Roadmap Lesson 12 "Stage 9 Capitalize"

## "Which stage am I in?" diagnostic
**Use when:** a founder describes the business and asks what to do, or says they're stuck.
**Core idea:** Headcount places them, symptoms confirm, and the lowest *unfinished* stage is the real constraint. A 25-person company with no sales KPIs fixes a Stage 4 gap first.
**How to run it:**
1. **Full-time headcount:** 0–1 → Stage 0–2 · 1–4 → 3 · 5–9 → 4 · 10–19 → 5 · 20–49 → 6 · 50–99 → 7 · 100–249 → 8 · 250–500 → 9.
2. **At 0–1, split by milestone:** nobody has used it → 0 · free users, no paid sale → 1 · paying but lumpy → 2.
3. **Symptom check, by stage:**
   - **3:** founder does everything, books own calls, cracks everywhere.
   - **4:** many customer types/offers; can't state show and close rates.
   - **5:** nothing else to buy; LTV:CAC caps ad spend; refunds by rep unknown.
   - **6:** spend rising but growth flat; close rate and churn worsening; two products starved.
   - **7:** one channel; leads unworked; CS firefighting; no departmental budgets.
   - **8:** bloat; best leads not routed to best closers; inconsistent hiring bar.
   - **9:** plateau; cash cow untouchable; books not investor-grade.
4. **Back-check:** is every earlier bottom line done? (Niche chosen? KPI sheet? Backend? Onboarding?) The first unfinished one is the constraint.
5. **Answer format:** "You're Stage N (Theme). Your role: X. Constraint: Y. Do: Z (2–4 steps). Not yet: [next-stage work]. You've graduated when: [signal]."
**Numbers & thresholds:** official headcount bands only. If they give revenue, ask headcount. Same revenue can sit two stages apart.
**Worked example (illustrative):** solo agency founder, 3 contractors, $40k/mo, wild months, books own calls. Stage 2. Constraint: inconsistent lead flow. Do: Rule of 100 on one channel, setter/closer script, daily bank check, a testimonial engine. Not yet: second channel, a CRM rebuild, managers. Graduate when month-to-month new customers are predictable and there's enough work for the first full-time hire.
**Mistakes he calls out:** self-placing by revenue or ambition; solving next-stage problems (a second channel at 6 people); watching every stage (indigestion).
**Ask the founder:** Full-time headcount? Biggest breakage this month? What do you still personally do daily? Which earlier bottom line isn't done?
**Pairs with:** One-constraint card; stage cards.
**Sources:** Scaling Roadmap Lessons 1–12 · personalised roadmap tool at acquisition.com/roadmap

## Find the one constraint (prioritisation)
**Use when:** "20 things to do", "X or Y?", new-initiative itch, team spread thin.
**Core idea:** Unlimited options, limited resources. Strategy = putting resources where the return is highest, which is the current constraint. Pick fewer, bigger wins, in sequence.
**How to run it:**
1. Stage → bottom line = default constraint.
2. Scan the 8 functions. Any function behind the *previous* stage is the constraint. A function that's ahead is not.
3. List the candidate fixes. Score impact, investment and likelihood/risk. Pick the big, hairy, high-impact one, not the quick win (Stage 3).
4. Put the whole team's attention on it until its graduation signal, then pick the next (Stages 6–7). Attention is the scarce resource, not time or people.
5. Put a money tag on it (what's it worth if solved?) to hold focus.
6. Write the "not doing" list.
**His YouTube diagnostics (same idea, outside the course):**
- Ask **"why can't we 10× this?"** about each function. He says portfolio founders answer "we could" about two times in three; the remaining one is the constraint.
- Ask for **the one thing that, if true, makes the other problems go away**. It's usually the thing you don't know how to do (hire a sales director, fix churn). Quote: "If it is the constraint of the business, it is your job." (YT "13 Years Of Brutally Honest Business Advice in 90 Mins", 2024)
- **Supply vs demand.** If delivery is full, more marketing does nothing. Supply fixes: change the delivery ratio (1:1 → 1:4 → 1:many), productize, raise price, hire. His 2026 coaching case (as stated): a 1:4 format roughly doubled capacity from ~60 to ~120 clients.
- Fix leaks (retention, referrals) before 10×-ing the front end. Do more of what works before adding new things.
- Let most fires burn. Almost nothing kills a business within hours (payment processing is his exception).
**Mistakes he calls out:** quick-win busywork; parallel initiatives; chasing opportunities before fixing problems; changing what works; a second channel before Stage 7.
**Ask the founder:** If you fixed one thing this quarter, which grows the business most, and what's it worth? What are you doing that belongs to a later stage?
**Pairs with:** diagnostic; leads file (More-Better-New).
**Sources:** Scaling Roadmap Lessons 1, 6, 7, 9, 10 · YT "13 Years Of Brutally Honest Business Advice in 90 Mins" (2024) · YT "13 Years of No BS Business Advice in 79 Mins" (2024) · YT "This Video Should Be Required Viewing for Business Owners" (2026) · YT "If I Wanted To Scale A Service Business In 2026" (2026)

## Founder role & time allocation
**Use when:** "what should I spend my time on", the founder is the bottleneck or won't let go.
**Core idea:** The founder's title changes every stage, and the calendar must change with it.
**How to run it (role → where the founder's hours go):**
- **Researcher (0):** user conversations, giving the free thing away.
- **Starter (1):** selling the first ones; building personal work capacity.
- **Doer (2):** daily Rule-of-100 volume, selling, hand-delivered service.
- **Trainer (3):** training first hires; hand off calendaring so phone time goes to good prospects.
- **Manager (4):** reading the weekly KPI sheet; saying no to non-niche customers; reviewing customer notes and complaints now that you're out of delivery.
- **Director (5):** building managers, running game tape across functions, choosing promotions deliberately.
- **Leader (6):** one company priority at a time; every management hire (under 50); decisions on data.
- **Executive (7):** triage; picking exec hires where you're weakest; hands-on with channel #2.
- **President (8):** weekly final interviews, monthly P&L line-by-line, quarterly spend cleanse, culture repetition.
- **Chairman (9):** capital allocation, the big bet, courting rainmakers for months.
**His own calendar (YouTube):**
- He separates maker time (long uninterrupted blocks) from manager time (many short meetings). One meeting in a maker's day costs far more than its length.
- His progression: maker work on early mornings, nights and weekends → mornings maker, afternoons manager → 2–4 full maker days plus one stacked meeting day.
- He spends the first 4–6 hours of the day uninterrupted on the highest-leverage work. Priorities go first in the day.
- He audits meetings: after each one, ask "do I need this again?" and "can a person or system eliminate it?" Price meetings by attendee cost.
- His 2022 revenue-based founder ladder (his own heuristic, not the official roadmap): around $0–1M, give up fulfilment; $1–10M, stop selling and build a sales team; $10–30M, give up marketing; $30–50M+, give up leading and become an adviser.
**Mistakes he calls out:** staying the Doer at 10+; copying what founders at later stages do now (Stage 2); handing off a function with no trained owner.
**Ask the founder:** What % of your week is in the role your stage needs? What recurring task could you document and hand off this week?
**Pairs with:** 3Ds; hiring sequence.

## Hiring sequence — who and when
**Use when:** "who first", "when to hire", "can't find good people", "should I promote X to manager".
**Core idea:** Hiring capability is built in steps. Match the process to the stage. Don't run Stage 7 recruiting at 4 people, or hire a VP with a Stage 3 process.
**How to run it:**
- **Stage 1:** freelancers for gaps, under written agreements.
- **Stage 2:** convert overloaded part-timers; job boards and social posts; classify correctly.
- **Stage 3:** the first leverage hire is **setting/calendaring**, which frees founder selling time. Job ad written as an ad, proof-based résumé screen, ~20% screen pass rate, hire for 12–18 months ahead, payroll provider.
- **Stage 4:** expectations screen → culture/skill/potential interview → ~5 reference calls. Handbook.
- **Stage 5:** first managers promoted by intention (normal process or a 60–90-day red/yellow/green trial). Referral bonus ≈ half an agency fee. Termination process.
- **Stage 6:** headhunt; four-way fit with multiple interviewers for senior roles; founder in every management hire; deep references; benefits and comp.
- **Stage 7:** ATS; specialist firms for repeat roles; first execs where the founder is weakest.
- **Stage 8:** structured questions, panels, scorecards, founder final fit check.
- **Stage 9:** founder recruits the top tier personally; workforce planning; RACI.
**His YouTube hiring rules:**
- **Hire one to hire ten.** Hire the sales director, not 40 reps. A bad hire at the constraint can cost about a year.
- **Hire for the constraint and ahead of your stage.** No inflated titles (no COO at 5 people).
- **Interview 10–20 people** for a senior role to build a baseline. Score each candidate on (a) how many and which metrics they use to describe their job, (b) whether they can say how their role makes the business money, (c) whether they'd raise the team's average.
- **Pay the market rate.** A-players at 25–50% more can produce multiples of a B-player's output.
- **Cheap in, fast out for entry roles.** He knows an A-player within ~14 days; cut duds during onboarding. The worst performer you tolerate becomes the bar.
- **Volume math (as stated):** ~100 LinkedIn reach-outs ≈ 1 hire.
- **Tension:** a 2021 video urges paying as little as possible and saying "no" to headcount requests by default, while 2024+ videos push premium pay for A-players. Reconcile it this way: don't add people without a problem, and do pay for outsized output.
**Mistakes he calls out:** trusting your gut on character; one reference; promoting your best IC by default; overqualified big-company hires early; founder exiting recruiting too soon; no-surprise firing ignored.
**Ask the founder:** Which task eats your highest-value hours? Is that role documented well enough to train? How many references did you call last hire?
**Pairs with:** 3Ds; delegation card; stage cards 3–9.
**Sources:** Scaling Roadmap Lessons 4–12 (Recruiting and HR sections) · YT "13 Years Of Brutally Honest Business Advice in 90 Mins" (2024) · YT "How to Sell Better than 99% Of People" (2024) · YT "If I could start over in business, I'd do this sooner" (2026) · YT "Recruiting and Hiring Tips" (2021)

## The 3Ds — Document, Demonstrate, Duplicate (training)
**Use when:** "how do I train people", "nobody does it like me", delegating without quality loss, writing SOPs.
**Core idea:** Train every role the same way. Write the job as a checklist, do it in front of the trainee, then have them do it in front of you. The checklist is the asset, not the founder's memory.
**How to run it:**
1. **Document:** every step needed to do the job well, as a checklist.
2. **Demonstrate:** do the job in front of them *following the checklist*. You'll catch yourself skipping or adding steps; fix the checklist first.
3. **Duplicate:** they do it in front of you. Correct them against the checklist.
4. Hand over ownership of the checklist. They keep it current.
**Source note:** neither the course nor the 63 YouTube transcripts use the "3Ds" name. The YouTube equivalents: document every function as a checklist so new people don't reinvent it; systemize delivery (one avatar, 1–3 deliverables) before building training; in sales, role-play with one correction at a time, repeated several times.
**Where the course applies the same pattern (without the 3Ds name):** sales script + tone guide + recordings → role-play → coaching cadence (Stage 6); CS playbook templates (5); game tape (5–6); spend SOP that moves "decisions out of your brain" (7).
**Worked example (illustrative):** handing off call booking. Document a 12-step checklist (contact speed, 3 qualifiers, booking, confirmation, reminders). Demonstrate 5 live bookings; 2 missing steps get added. The setter duplicates the next 10 while watched, then owns schedule and show rate on the KPI sheet.
**Mistakes he calls out:** training from memory; not updating the checklist after the demonstration; skipping the watched duplicate.
**Ask the founder:** Is the task a written checklist? Do you follow it yourself? Have you watched them do it?
**Pairs with:** delegation card; Stages 3, 5, 6.
**Sources:** $100M Lost Chapters (publisher blurb) · Hormozi training clip (via research notes) · YT "13 Years Of Brutally Honest Business Advice in 90 Mins" (2024) · Scaling Roadmap Lessons 5–7

## Delegation & management — why work doesn't get done
**Use when:** "my team doesn't follow through", delegated work comes back wrong, "should I fire them", managing first managers.
**Core idea:** Diagnose before blaming. Most misses come from missing information, skill or resources. Then hold the standard, and fire with process.
**How to run it:**
1. **Five reasons, in order.** He says he "usually draws a diamond" for this (YT, 2024). It is probably the *Lost Chapters* "Performance Diamond", but that's unconfirmed. Did they know you wanted it (written, clear)? Did they know how (skill; if not, train with the 3Ds)? Did they know by when (have them estimate)? Were they motivated (reward and consequence)? Was something blocking them? His example: slow office upload speeds pushed editors home, so he paid ~$120k for fibre. Podcast Ep 882 gives the same list, with "specific requirements" in place of motivation. Attack the reason, not the person.
2. Match the instruction to the person. The more skilled they are, the vaguer the direction can be ("fix churn"); juniors need steps. You can't keep absolute control and also get freedom.
3. Give each person a number they own. Tie pay to 1–2 metrics they control most. Nobody fully controls any one metric, so pick the one they influence most.
4. Install the operating rhythm for the stage: shared tools (4), KPI sheets (4), weekly game tape (5), huddle/team/1:1 cadence and coaching (6), department budgets and spend SOPs (7), performance tool, panels, monthly finance review (8), RACI and red/yellow/green org map (9).
5. Empower within limits: a list of actions staff can take without approval, with a dollar cap (Stage 5). Spending authority by SOP, not case by case (Stage 7).
6. Standards: warn with red/yellow/green before it's serious, and never fire by surprise (Stage 5). Leader test: imagine them gone; if nothing would change, that's likely what they're doing now (X post).
**Mistakes he calls out:** assuming laziness when the requirement was never stated; promoting the best doer by convenience; avoiding the hard conversation (Leila: people avoid the conversation, not what follows it); a new hire on a new initiative.
**Ask the founder:** Which of the five checks failed last time? Does each person own a number? If your manager left tomorrow, what breaks?
**Pairs with:** 3Ds; Stages 5–9.

## Contradictions & caveats
- **Headcount, not revenue.** Any "$X = Stage N" mapping is not official. The course's revenue asides are rough and stage-specific.
- **Second channel timing:** don't add one at Stage 4 (fix the first); add one at Stage 7. These are stage-specific, not contradictory.
- **"Acknowledge, Associate, Ask"** is taught in the course (Stage 2, live-conversation sales). Web research had marked it unverified; the course confirms it. Don't confuse it with ACA (Acknowledge, Compliment, Ask) from warm outreach.
- **US framing:** W-2/1099, the IRS test, payroll tax, ACA benefits above 50, EPLI. Translate the principle for non-US founders.
- **Third-party stories in the course** (New Balance, Gym Launch and portfolio figures) are as spoken, not verified.
- **3Ds** = Document, Demonstrate, Duplicate. The name comes from *Lost Chapters*, not this corpus. Treat the Diamond-to-five-reasons link as probable, not confirmed.
- **Lesson 13 (bonus)** has no framework: it's an ask to submit results, with a monthly random draw for a two-day visit to Acquisition.com HQ.
- Transcripts are a third-party re-upload with auto-captions; speaker (Alex or Leila) is often unlabeled. Attribute to "the course" when unsure.
