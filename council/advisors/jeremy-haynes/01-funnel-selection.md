# 01 — Funnel selection

**Open this file when:** the question is "which funnel should we run", webinar vs call funnel, whether to add DM ads, a low-ticket front end, a challenge, or a second funnel, or why a funnel that worked organically fails on paid.
**His order here:** sales-team strength → lead awareness/education needed → offer strength → testing capacity → preference. Funnel choice is "arguably one of the number one things to pick that determines your success" (AAzziyOKCx8 [23:45]).
**Caption note:** for U0xz2ht-8nQ and jK2dn9Dft5E the first caption pull was an auto-dubbed track. Numbers were re-checked against the original English captions, and timestamps are accurate to about ±30s.

---

### Funnel choice is decided by the sales team (his rule, unnamed as a framework)
**When to use:** any "which funnel" question, and any time a funnel that worked organically breaks on paid.
**The idea:** Organic content pre-sells people, so organic-fed closers get layups and go soft: "They're a cashier at a grocery store" in skill set (U0xz2ht-8nQ [02:18]). Paid cold traffic arrives curious and needs an education call. So the funnel has to match what the team can actually close. A team that only closes warm leads needs a webinar or heavy back-end systems. A team that can educate can run a VSL call funnel. A team used to cold calling can run lead forms.
**How to run it:**
1. Ask how the current team closes: organic leads only, or cold paid leads too.
2. Organic-only closers → live webinar (it creates layups) or a call funnel with the full back-end stack (`03`).
3. Strong, cohesive closers who can educate → VSL call funnel. "If your offer is stronger than your sales people, you can run with a VSL call funnel" (bPkM0TCGFQQ [09:43]–[10:05]).
4. Relentless dial-and-text team ("real salespeople", not "fat cat closers") → the Hydra (in-platform lead forms).
5. Then check education need: financially qualified but uneducated buyers need a content mechanism first, even if they're rich (bPkM0TCGFQQ [09:20]).
**The numbers:** None given as thresholds. Webinar-booked calls should close at 35%+ because they are layups (jK2dn9Dft5E [28:22]; P1v-AN3So3A [1:23:24]).
**Mistakes they call out:**
- Porting an organic call funnel to paid and thinking the funnel was the success. "Any funnel can work if the organic content is done right" (rZCe1U3ME4E [02:38]).
- Blaming leads as "too cold" when the team was trained on layups.
**Ask first:** Who closes today and where do their leads come from? Can they run an education call? Would they follow up 20 times?
**Source:** bPkM0TCGFQQ [06:39]–[10:05], AAzziyOKCx8 [21:31]–[23:01], U0xz2ht-8nQ [00:21], [13:20]–[14:03], rZCe1U3ME4E [01:54]–[03:24]

### The funnel tier list (S to D, mid-2025)
**When to use:** choosing what to test next, or sanity-checking a funnel someone is excited about.
**The idea:** He ranks funnels by how often they work across businesses, not by peak results. "Some of these still crush. They just don't crush at a high rate for every business."
**How to run it:**
1. **S:** call funnels (his go-to on entering any high-ticket business; works "more than half of the time by far"), live webinars ("resurrected"), challenge funnels (borderline A because they're exhausting).
2. **A:** book funnels, live events, low-ticket to high-ticket, DM ads (he argues DM ads are really B).
3. **B:** automated webinars, quiz funnels, IG shout-outs, the Hydra (lead forms).
4. **C:** lead magnets, mini-courses/"trust funnels".
5. **D:** none.
**The numbers:** Up to a third of high-ticket buyers watch more than one webinar before buying, which is why replaying the same auto webinar erodes trust (GOaaqiZ-k0M [06:10]). Challenge case: $292K ad spend, ~1.9x front-end on tickets, $3.4M in-challenge on a $10K offer, almost $4M total (GOaaqiZ-k0M [08:02]). Book funnel case: $43K spend → $322K upsells, 7% of buyers to a $12K upsell (GOaaqiZ-k0M [13:03]).
**Mistakes they call out:**
- Call funnels failing "typically because you have an awful show rate" and no modern back-end practices.
- Monthly challenges on 30-day promo cycles.
- Lead magnets that don't get a result fast, or that sabotage the high-ticket sale.
**Ask first:** What has your team already proven? Can you present one-to-many? How much testing bandwidth do you have?
**Source:** GOaaqiZ-k0M [01:10]–[35:43]

### The friction spectrum (and intent)
**When to use:** someone wants "higher intent leads" and plans to add friction to get them.
**The idea:** Funnels run from low friction (in-platform lead forms, DM ads) through VSL call funnels and webinars to the highest (paid multi-day challenges). Lower friction usually brings more volume and lower intent. He doesn't accept that you must add friction to get intent. Run low-friction funnels for volume, then raise intent with information delivered after the opt-in. Rich people favor low friction: "they don't have time, but they have money."
**How to run it:**
1. Place every funnel you run on the spectrum.
2. Use the four levels of interest: curious, general, highly interested, convicted. "typically convicted, anything above here, this is where deals get done" [09:57].
3. Move leads up "a single piece of information at a time" with back-end selling systems (`03`).
4. Run a portfolio across the spectrum (e.g. webinar plus call funnel concurrently, quarterly challenge).
**The numbers:** Hydra/capital-raise leads take ~20 contact attempts before first connection and ~12 meetings, over 3–6 months (fMiSOqBTBEA [03:05]). These are expectations he sets, not benchmarks for services.
**Mistakes they call out:**
- Defaulting to high-friction funnels to get intent.
- Letting leads talk to sales before they've been warmed. "You're going to have whiny... salespeople."
**Ask first:** How rich is the buyer? Who does the warming before the call?
**Source:** fMiSOqBTBEA [01:32]–[19:56]

### DM ads and Instagram DM funnels have a ceiling
**When to use:** someone wants to scale on Instagram DMs (organic or paid) as the main or only funnel.
**The idea:** DM funnels work, and some people hit $1M months with them, but he calls them "one of the best and absolute worst funnels". They are unstable: anyone can pay to get your DMs disabled, setters break messaging rules without knowing, and there are few analytics, so owners lack the guts to scale blind. Setter teams break past a handful. He keeps DM ads in his A tier only because they add a chunk of revenue (see Chunk method).
**How to run it:**
1. Treat DMs as one leg, never the only one.
2. If you run DM ads, never optimize for "message sent". Condition the pixel on a qualified booked call (`05` DM ads: two paths).
3. Track cost per *qualified* DM, not cost per DM.
4. Keep setter teams small and managed, or test AI setters.
5. Build a second funnel before you need it.
**The numbers:** Hard ceiling "right around like two to 300K a month" (GOaaqiZ-k0M [23:29]). Most DM operators land at $100–300K, and he has seen DM-to-$1M "literally twice" (U0xz2ht-8nQ [33:02], [35:24]). Setter teams of about "three to five" are the pattern before it breaks (U0xz2ht-8nQ [32:14]). A $200K/month DM client was restricted for 3 months (GOaaqiZ-k0M [24:39]). People pay $500–1,500 to take down accounts or disable DMs (AAzziyOKCx8 [50:33]). Counter-example: Kay, ~$1.5M/month paid-to-DM, $3K AOV, ~$500K/month spend, 3:1 (U0xz2ht-8nQ [33:43]).
**Mistakes they call out:**
- Setters following up too aggressively and breaking Meta's non-response windows, which gets the account restricted (GOaaqiZ-k0M [24:16]).
- Running a whole business on one IG account. A guest in bPkM0TCGFQQ lost DM ads to a ~2-week ban [56:16].
**Ask first:** What share of revenue comes through IG DMs? What happens if the account is restricted for 3 months? How many setters, and who manages them?

### Roll over what already works organically
**When to use:** an organic business ($200K to $2M+/month in his framing) is adding paid ads.
**The idea:** Paid is "nitrous oxide" injected into a working engine, not a replacement for organic. Start by moving the exact conversion mechanism that converts organically onto paid. IG DM/setter businesses run DM ads. Organic live-webinar businesses run paid live webinars. A marketer who recommends a different mechanism first is a "donkey alert".
**How to run it:**
1. Name the organic mechanism that converts today.
2. Launch it on paid to warm audiences first: 180-day web traffic, 365-day social engagers, YouTube viewers, customer lists. Video viewers only at high thresholds.
3. Replicate the organic content consumption with content ads (`04`) and back-end systems (`03`). Straight direct response is the worst start.
4. Set a gambling budget and a time frame long enough to survive the dip.
5. Graduate to cold once it works. Warm audiences cap out and decay.
**The numbers:** Client doing a couple million a month organically with live webinars: $25K ad spend per weekly webinar, results of break-even, a loss, ~$129K, break-even, and then scaling. That took ~3 months and ~$300K of spend before it worked (QwZ3X7GGSVw [34:07]–[35:37]). A call funnel alternative gives ~30 iteration cycles a month vs 4 for weekly webinars [35:37]–[36:22].
**Mistakes they call out:**
- Quitting because "leads are too cold". Paid "is going to suck" at first, universally.
- Introducing a new funnel type instead of the organic winner.
- A time frame far shorter than the years it took to build organic.
**Ask first:** What converts organically right now? Are your warm audiences big enough to retarget? What budget can you lose and still come back?
**Source:** QwZ3X7GGSVw [00:22]–[40:58]

### Live webinars (when and how often)
**When to use:** the sales team needs layups, the buyer needs education, or the founder presents well one-to-many.
**The idea:** Live webinars create buyers the way organic content does, by compressing trust into one session. They're best at high frequency to cold traffic. Automate only after the live version is proven, and never use "just in time" fake scheduling.
**How to run it:**
1. Run live first. Prove quality.
2. Promote no more than ~3 days out. Earlier opt-ins show far less.
3. Run weekly to cold and about monthly to warm, to avoid warm-audience fatigue (AAzziyOKCx8 [56:09]).
4. Book calls the same day or the next. Calls booked 4–5 days after the webinar show worse.
5. Move to scheduled automated webinars only once scaled.
**The numbers:** Test budget ~$5K–7.5K per webinar (P1v-AN3So3A [02:39], [06:12]). Live show rate ~20% average, 30%+ above average, 10–15% means problems [03:27]. Booking rate ~30% of those present at the pitch [04:15]. Booked-call show 70% minimum, close 35% floor [04:39]. Day-of opt-ins show 40%+, day-3+ opt-ins ~10% or less [50:04]–[51:13]. Webinar ROAS 3–5x when it works (U0xz2ht-8nQ [06:29]). For booked calls off a webinar, "you never want the AOV to be below $5,000", otherwise sell direct (jK2dn9Dft5E [28:54]).
**Mistakes they call out:**
- Under-spending on tests so the payoff never justifies the work (U0xz2ht-8nQ [07:54]).
- Recycling one auto webinar to repeat viewers.
- "Just in time" webinars: "violently bad" (P1v-AN3So3A [1:36:27]).
**Ask first:** Can the founder present for an hour and close for an hour? Can you run it weekly?
**Source:** P1v-AN3So3A [01:55]–[1:38:48], U0xz2ht-8nQ [02:52]–[09:41], GOaaqiZ-k0M [03:54]–[07:38]

### Low-ticket to high-ticket: the breakout strategy (his name)
**When to use:** adding a low-ticket front end, a book funnel, or an entry product under a high-ticket offer.
**The idea:** Don't invent a random low-ticket product. Break a real component out of the high-ticket offer (one group call, one masterclass, the AI tool) so someone who wants the piece probably wants the whole. Pick the component with the fastest time to result. Low ticket also "liquidates" front-end ad spend, so cash-burning funnels become profitable when blended.
**How to run it:**
1. List everything inside the main offer. Break out the piece with the fastest result.
2. Make it feel "overwhelmingly valuable for the price".
3. Funnel: sales page (headline, VSL, button, long copy) → checkout → 1–3 short upsells → application/booking → confirmation page.
4. Engineer ~3 aha moments fast (value emails, setter-clipped highlights, content retargeting to buyers).
5. Upsell in the aha window, or after the first real result. Setters start as accountability buddies, then switch to the upsell.
**The numbers:** Rules of thumb: sales-page click-to-purchase 3–5%, each OTO 10–20%, setter set rate ~50% of connected, show 70%+, close ~40% (25–30% floor) (lz4XrSxI1Y0 [46:22]–[51:41]). Book funnels average ~3-month cycles (U0xz2ht-8nQ [22:28]). Summarizing a book via emails and videos cut one cycle from 3 months to "a couple days at best" (lz4XrSxI1Y0 [41:01]).
**Mistakes they call out:**
- A low-ticket product with no congruency to the high ticket, or a slow one that sabotages the sale (GOaaqiZ-k0M [18:49]).
- Giving buyers AI shortcuts on day one, so the aha gets credited to the AI. Delay them 3–5 days (lz4XrSxI1Y0 [36:23]).
- Setters pitching before the buyer has consumed anything.
**Ask first:** What piece of the main offer gets a result in days? Can the front end at least break even?
**Source:** lz4XrSxI1Y0 [02:16]–[56:15], GOaaqiZ-k0M [18:26]–[21:12], U0xz2ht-8nQ [19:07]–[25:46]

### Chunk method: add a funnel when one taps out
**When to use:** one funnel has hit its ceiling short of the revenue target.
**The idea:** Rarely used. When a funnel's potential is exhausted, add a second funnel and stack the revenue streams. He crossed $1M/month this way: the bulk from Inner Circle, a smaller part from Jeremy AI. The ceiling is not a fixed number. (His phrase is "that chunk method approach", U0xz2ht-8nQ [35:24]. In GOaaqiZ-k0M [28:30] he says IG shout-outs "chunk" a couple hundred grand onto what you do.)
**How to run it:**
1. Confirm the current funnel really is at a ceiling (ROAS falling as spend rises, messaging exhausted).
2. Maintain it. Don't kill it.
3. Add the next-highest-probability funnel from the tier list, with its own testing resources.
**The numbers:** IG shout-outs usually add "a couple hundred grand" (GOaaqiZ-k0M [28:30]).
**Mistakes they call out:** Running multiple funnels without enough testing resources (U0xz2ht-8nQ [27:11]). Testing two at once halves the odds of both (bPkM0TCGFQQ [16:44]–[18:36]).
**Ask first:** Is it a real ceiling or an unfixed bottleneck? Who will run the second funnel's tests?
**Source:** U0xz2ht-8nQ [18:24]–[19:07], GOaaqiZ-k0M [25:45]–[26:08], P1v-AN3So3A [1:36:50]–[1:38:48]

### Challenges, live events and back-end events
**When to use:** a lump-sum revenue spike, or a back end for a business whose revenue is nearly all front end.
**The idea:** Challenges and live events make "generational" lump sums but are exhausting, need long promo cycles, and can't run at a steady pace. Treat them as a periodic sweep of the whole lead base. In-person events also make a high-margin back end for existing buyers.
**How to run it:**
1. Run them quarterly at most, usually 1–2 times a year.
2. Expect a 60–90 day "burn" promo period that loses money on tickets.
3. For a back end, survey existing buyers first, asking whether they'd be interested at a stated price range. Great ideas get great responses (bZe1YnKTBCE [1:19:57]).
**The numbers:** Live events take ~6 months to coordinate, 3 if tight (GOaaqiZ-k0M [17:18]). Serious challenges spend $90–120K on a virtual studio and speakers [08:51]. A back-end check from one consult: at $50K price, 10 buyers add $500K/month, under 3% of the client's monthly sign-ups (bZe1YnKTBCE [1:24:33]–[1:24:55]).
**Mistakes they call out:** Not giving challenges enough promo time. Waiting to "stabilize" before building a back end. He pushed back on that in one consult (bZe1YnKTBCE [1:23:07]).
**Ask first:** What share of revenue is front end? Would your best customers pay for in-person access?
**Source:** GOaaqiZ-k0M [07:38]–[18:26], U0xz2ht-8nQ [35:53]–[40:15], bZe1YnKTBCE [1:02:43]–[1:25:39]
