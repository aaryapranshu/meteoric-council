# 02 — Call funnels, VSLs and applications

**Open this file when:** building or fixing a book-a-call funnel: page layout, VSL or DSL, application questions, scheduler, booking window, closers, follow-up of unclosed leads, or scaling the funnel.
**Core principle:** call funnels create education calls, so "offload a majority of the salesperson's work from the salesperson" into marketing (rZCe1U3ME4E [06:56]).
**Show-rate systems** live in `03`. **Benchmarks and models** live in `06`.
**Caption note:** for Zr8m8euJ3zw and bZe1YnKTBCE the first caption pull was an auto-dubbed track. Numbers were re-checked against the original English captions, and timestamps are accurate to about ±30s.

### The call funnel: headline, VSL, embedded application
**When to use:** any high-ticket service sold on a call. It's his default funnel and "one of the best most predictable and reliable ways to hit a million dollars a month" with a solid offer (A7aqpkk57Ts [00:46]).
**The idea:** Direct-response ad → one page with a headline, a VSL and the application with the scheduler embedded right under it. Qualified applicants book with closers. Unqualified ones go to a separate page (down-sell or setter). Strip everything else off the page, because extra sections let people scan instead of watch. That is "an act of deterrence": low-interest people who won't watch aren't wanted.
**How to run it:**
1. Ad: talking-head direct response, hook/body/CTA. Describe the conditions of the ideal customer rather than asking whether they're at six figures (WEwwAvTY1B4 [15:35]–[16:22]).
2. Page: headline → VSL → embedded application → scheduler on the same page. Add testimonials or VSL-content sections only if play rate is too low.
3. Route: qualified → closer calendar and qualified confirmation page. Unqualified → separate unpixeled page, down-sell, or setters.
4. Fire the pixel only for qualified booked calls (`05`).
5. Keep messaging identical across ad, headline, VSL, application questions, confirmation page, emails and setters.
**The numbers:** Rules of thumb: CPM $30–80, unique link CTR ~2%, click-to-qualified-booked-call 3–5%, first-call show ~60% "nowadays", one-call close ~25% (zyfpGi9uEUU [20:00]–[20:25]; rZCe1U3ME4E [11:06]–[15:45]). The most common contracted stat is click-to-qualified-call, "usually that is well below 3%" (zyfpGi9uEUU [22:18]). Typical conservative cost per call $100–300, or $500–1,000 for long cycles, small TAMs and big AOVs (rZCe1U3ME4E [11:33]). Older range: cost per call $50 to $900 can all be fine depending on downstream stats (A7aqpkk57Ts [05:43]).
**Mistakes they call out:**
- A button that sends people to the application on another page: "you never put a button on the page that increases your cost per application artificially" (cscM_KmpBgY [10:41]).
- Using HubSpot/GoHighLevel forms and calendars just because you pay for them.
- Income claims and guarantees in ads, which "attract absolute dumpster diving turds" (WEwwAvTY1B4 [10:12]).
**Ask first:** What's your cost per *qualified* booked call? Is the application on the same page as the video? Do the ads and the VSL say the same thing?

### Offload the salesperson's work into marketing (his principle)
**When to use:** closers complain leads are cold, uneducated or unqualified.
**The idea:** On paid call funnels all the education lands on the closer unless marketing absorbs it. The marketer's job is "to make it as easy as they possibly can on the sales team" (A7aqpkk57Ts [24:31]). Build sales assets and trust assets ("almost one and the same") and the back-end systems in `03`, so the call becomes a sales conversation.
**How to run it:**
1. Ask closers which questions they're tired of answering. Those become breakout videos and emails.
2. Build trust assets for the sophisticated buyer's real questions. Looms are fine.
3. Have setters and closers send them before the call.
4. Collect articulate end-of-day feedback. "Leads unqualified" often means "too early in the sales process" (WEwwAvTY1B4 [30:05]–[30:49]).
**The numbers:** Inner Circle member running done-for-you Airbnb: 75%+ show on cold paid traffic, 40%+ close, ~$22K cash per sale, credited to a trust-asset page listing every deal with pro forma and video breakdown (rZCe1U3ME4E [04:12]; kZmPPS4512I [17:33]).
**Mistakes they call out:** Skipping assets and leaving all education on closers. Taking salespeople's feedback at face value without articulate reports.
**Ask first:** What do your closers repeat on every call? What would a skeptical buyer want to verify?
**Source:** rZCe1U3ME4E [01:54]–[07:20], WEwwAvTY1B4 [36:18]–[39:21], A7aqpkk57Ts [24:31]

### VSL best practices (2026)
**When to use:** writing, editing or diagnosing a VSL.
**The idea:** Viewers care about "efficiency and energy conservation", not about you. So open with a 60–90 second summary of the whole VSL (the "tweet version"), then expand each point. Add chapters so people jump to what they care about. Match the VSL to the ads that actually get spend, and never open cold traffic with "hi, I'm...".
**How to run it:**
1. First 60–90 seconds: summarize everything, including price if you show it.
2. Chapter it. He calls refusing chapters to force linear viewing "silly".
3. Thumbnail: a 5–10 second video clip, or an image showing what viewers care about most. Host on Wistia or Vidalytics, not YouTube or Vimeo.
4. Length: short for rich buyers, longer for the general public. The norm is 10–20 minutes.
5. Edit for fried attention: captions, jump cuts, visual edits.
6. Rebuild the VSL around the message of the 1–3 ads that actually get spend.
7. Every VSL needs a unique mechanism. Put your process on display, because the feed makes buyers "hyper aware and hyper sophisticated in real time" (rZCe1U3ME4E [49:27]).
**The numbers:** Ideal play rate 50%+ and engagement rate 50%+ (rZCe1U3ME4E [52:31]). 2–5 minutes "absolutely rip with rich people" (rL1FIHwg8PE [16:00]). Some general-public clients use an hour-long unedited podcast [16:23]. One consult: 80% play rate but ~9.5% engagement on an 8-minute VSL, the clue that the opening didn't match the ad promise (bZe1YnKTBCE [34:35]–[36:48]).
**Mistakes they call out:**
- Opening with a hook or intro instead of a summary.
- Hiding the play bar while showing the button, which lowers lead quality.
- Too much on the page, so people scroll past the VSL.
- A VSL left unchanged while the ads were rebuilt.
**Ask first:** What are play rate and engagement rate? What does the retention graph look like? Early cliff means fix the opening. Steep the whole way means replace the VSL (rZCe1U3ME4E [54:02]).
**Source:** rL1FIHwg8PE [01:30]–[18:38], rZCe1U3ME4E [49:27]–[54:24], bZe1YnKTBCE [36:02]–[48:41]

### Withholding the apply button
**When to use:** few visitors watch the VSL and someone suggests hiding the button until minute X.
**The idea:** Delaying the button can feed the pixel higher-intent leads, but it inflates cost per call in the short term. "What really determines this decision is your bank account" (rL1FIHwg8PE [04:55]). Forcing consumption on the front end mostly fails. Consumption happens on the back end.
**How to run it:**
1. Only withhold if you can afford a much higher short-term cost per call and still exit the learning phase.
2. Withhold if closers need pre-consumed content. Don't if they're "dogs" who close anyway or demand full calendars.
3. If you withhold the button, also withhold the play bar.
**The numbers:** Tests of 1–2 minute delays, some 20–30 (rL1FIHwg8PE [04:55]). Of "a little over 220" Inner Circle members, one (a 45-minute application delay) made forced consumption work. Everyone else "just gets a dramatically higher cost per call" (Zr8m8euJ3zw [11:05]).
**Mistakes they call out:** Forcing consumption on the front end (Zr8m8euJ3zw [06:04]).
**Ask first:** Can you afford doubled cost per call for weeks? What do the closers need?
**Source:** rL1FIHwg8PE [03:46]–[09:28], Zr8m8euJ3zw [06:04]–[11:06]

### The DSL: deck sales letter (his and Josh Troy's term)
**When to use:** VSL play rates are low, closers say leads didn't watch, or the niche won't watch video (often blue collar).
**The idea:** Replace the VSL with an embedded slide deck (Google Slides) that people scroll at their own pace. Put an executive summary of everything in the first five slides, then "the entire book" after. He frames it as a different conversion mechanism, not a VSL variant. To cut CPA in half you test mechanisms, not iterations: "you don't generally split test your way there" (cscM_KmpBgY [19:53]).
**How to run it:**
1. Turn the VSL into slides with words and visuals, or use a 5–15 minute mini-webinar structure.
2. Slides 1–5 = the whole pitch summarized.
3. Same page as the call funnel: headline → deck → embedded application.
4. Make the iframe responsive (ask an AI to fix sizing).
5. Treat it as its own funnel type and test variations.
**The numbers:** 70% of visitors reach slide 5 (cscM_KmpBgY [06:08]; bZe1YnKTBCE [29:35]). Upwards of 45% reach the end of a deck vs ~25% VSL completion (cscM_KmpBgY [20:38]–[21:26]). The order of effects: sales feedback improves, then cost per qualified call drops, then show rate rises, while close rate barely moves ("maybe 1 to 3%") [20:38]–[21:49].
**Mistakes they call out:** Raw Google Slides embeds cut off on mobile. Copying the template without understanding information flow.
**Ask first:** What's the VSL play rate? Do closers say leads didn't watch?
**Source:** cscM_KmpBgY [00:00]–[22:56], rZCe1U3ME4E [37:33], bPkM0TCGFQQ [1:08:32]–[1:09:17]

### Application design and order
**When to use:** writing or trimming the application form.
**The idea:** Typed answers add friction and intent, but also cost. Sales and marketing should agree on the questions that stay. The sweet spot is three typed questions. Order matters: open with an easy "layup" question that tells the right person they're in the right place.
**How to run it:**
1. Q1: layup question. Multiple choice, easy, signals fit, can carry conditional logic.
2. Q2: financial qualifier. Pick a point on the spectrum: from stating the price and requiring acknowledgement (plus income minimum, time commitment, proof of revenue on the call) to none at all. The sweet spot is a sophisticated question people won't lie on.
3. Q3: the third key question.
4. Then other multiple choice, then personal info last, one field per step (name, then phone, then email).
5. Then the scheduler, on the same page.
6. Validate questions against outcomes with AI. Drop the ones with no correlation to show or close.
**The numbers:** "Typically, three typed questions is the sweet spot" (rZCe1U3ME4E [59:02]). In a survey, 70% of Inner Circle members said they'd lie about income on an application, and 80%+ in a client's group of regular people [1:06:24]. Daniel Fazio's math question (deal amount minus expenses) predicted show and close well enough to drop other qualifiers [1:07:35].
**Mistakes they call out:**
- A layup question that signals the wrong audience. "Do you make more than 100K?" scares off a $2M/month buyer [1:00:38].
- All multiple choice, which makes lying easy.
- Name, email and phone on one step.
**Ask first:** Do you want volume or a small, high-quality calendar? Which answers have actually predicted closes?
**Source:** rZCe1U3ME4E [54:46]–[1:10:40]

### Keep the application and scheduler on one page
**When to use:** choosing form and calendar tools, or diagnosing drop-off between application and booking.
**The idea:** Every extra step and every second of load time loses qualified people. He still prefers Typeform + Calendly because they give the lowest cost per qualified call, says he has no financial tie to them, and admits both annoy him. iClosed is an improved option. All-in-one AI-scoring tools that add seconds of delay hurt.
**How to run it:**
1. Embed the scheduler right after the application.
2. Measure application-to-booking. Above ~16–35% drop-off, check speed and steps.
3. Book soon. ~24 hours is ideal, ~72 hours the max. Add time only if closers say leads arrive too cold.
**The numbers:** Separate application and scheduler: ~50% of qualified applicants don't book. Integrated: ~16% drop (rZCe1U3ME4E [1:12:41]). One consult: an AI-scoring widget pushed cost per call to $375 and 1 second of extra load cost ~$200 per call. Switching back gave ~$200 (bZe1YnKTBCE [39:42]–[41:30]). Booking window: Zr8m8euJ3zw [17:36] says "The sooner that they book, the better", 24h ideal / 72h max (newer, 2026). WEwwAvTY1B4 [30:05] said 2–3 days out can be ideal so there's time to deliver info (2025). Prefer the newer one unless closers complain leads are too cold.
**Mistakes they call out:** HubSpot or GoHighLevel separate calendars. Software that makes leads wait "more than realistically a second or two" (Zr8m8euJ3zw [16:51]).
**Ask first:** What % of qualified applicants book? How many seconds between submit and calendar?
**Source:** rZCe1U3ME4E [1:10:40]–[1:16:35], Zr8m8euJ3zw [11:50]–[18:00], A7aqpkk57Ts [10:20]–[11:52]

### Closer math and calendar capacity
**When to use:** planning a revenue target, sizing ad spend, or deciding when to hire a closer.
**The idea:** Work backwards from the goal to the number of closers needed, then hire ahead of the hiring cycle. Scaling a call funnel is two moves on repeat: "Fill the closers calendars, hire more closers" (rZCe1U3ME4E [1:45:20]).
**How to run it:**
1. Goal ÷ cash collected per sale = closes.
2. ÷ close rate = calls held. ÷ show rate = calls booked.
3. × cost per call = ad spend.
4. Calls booked ÷ 22 working days ÷ ~8 calls per closer per day = closers needed.
5. Compare with today. When calendars could be full within ~2 weeks, start hiring.
6. Keep calendars at ~80% capacity, never 100%, so there's time for follow-up.
**The numbers:** Worked example: $1M goal ÷ $10K = 100 closes ÷ 20% = 500 calls ÷ 60% = ~833 booked × $150 = ~$125K spend; 833/22 ≈ 38/day ÷ 8 ≈ 5 closers (rZCe1U3ME4E [24:58]–[28:35]). Closer hiring takes 1–2 weeks at best, 4–6 weeks on average (rZCe1U3ME4E [30:31]). In the older video a new closer ramps in ~3–4 weeks (A7aqpkk57Ts [26:49]). 8–10 calls a day is fully loaded (bZe1YnKTBCE [53:22]).
**Mistakes they call out:**
- Filling three closers before proving positive ROAS (rZCe1U3ME4E [19:35]).
- Waiting until calendars are maxed to hire, which stalls you for 3–4 weeks (A7aqpkk57Ts [27:34]).
- Running top closers at the edge of capacity with no time for follow-up.
**Ask first:** How many call slots a day are open? How long does it take you to hire and ramp a closer?
**Source:** rZCe1U3ME4E [24:14]–[31:20], [1:32:43]–[1:35:48], [1:45:20], jK2dn9Dft5E [30:55]–[36:46], A7aqpkk57Ts [25:38]–[27:56]

### Cleaners and "buy or die" leakage
**When to use:** closers' calendars are full and hundreds of no-closes, no-shows and reschedules go unfollowed each week.
**The idea:** When closers are fully booked, nobody works yesterday's leads. A "cleaner" is a full sales rep (not a junior role) who takes leads not closed within a set window, books them onto their own calendar, and works the long cycle. Cleaners never take fresh front-end bookings. Pair this with an AI agent in the CRM that moves leads through stages and sends messages based on what happened on the call.
**How to run it:**
1. Set an ownership window, e.g. 72 hours. The rep can flag a lead as likely to close later, and loses the flag if they abuse it.
2. After the window, the lead goes to the cleaner team. Split commission if you want.
3. Protect top closers for fresh calls.
4. Add an AI agent that reads transcripts and CRM activity and sends contextual follow-up.
**The numbers:** Kay (Inner Circle, $1M/month): +$82K in June from an AI agent following up 400,000 DM conversations (bZe1YnKTBCE [58:44]). The fully loaded closer level above applies.
**Mistakes they call out:** Treating cleaner as a lesser job. Letting closers keep leads forever.
**Ask first:** How many unclosed leads per week does nobody touch?
**Source:** bZe1YnKTBCE [53:01]–[59:49], [1:13:50]–[1:14:12], rZCe1U3ME4E [1:36:58]–[1:38:06]

### Call-funnel financial model and gambling budget
**When to use:** before launching or scaling a call funnel.
**The idea:** Model the funnel on conservative ("the upper end of the worst side of the range") inputs and cash collected, not revenue. The test budget is money you can "vaporize" and learn from: "a good gambler knows that you only technically lose when you run out of money to gamble with" (rZCe1U3ME4E [19:11]). Higher cash per sale tolerates worse stats everywhere else.
**How to run it:**
1. Rows: ad spend → cost per call → show → (2nd-call booking and show if two-call) → close → cash per sale → gross ROAS → fixed costs, team, commissions → net profit → net ROAS.
2. Use worst-case values. If it still works, go.
3. Stack the odds in the test phase: put your best closer on (maybe you), fill them, feed fast feedback to ads.
4. Prove ROAS small before filling every closer.
**The numbers:** Budget example: $15K / 30 days at $345 per call ≈ 43 calls ≈ 2 calls a day for one closer (rZCe1U3ME4E [17:41]–[18:27]). Show-rate modeling: ideal 60%, model 40–50%. Below 30% is "tech issue territory" [13:06]–[13:51]. Two-call close: 2nd-call booking 60%+, 2nd-call show 70–75%+, close 35%+ [13:51]–[15:45].
**Mistakes they call out:** Revenue instead of cash collected ("hippies from the 80s", rZCe1U3ME4E [09:15]). Over-leveraging the test.
**Ask first:** What's your worst-case cost per call and cash per sale? How much can you lose and come back?
**Source:** rZCe1U3ME4E [07:20]–[24:14], A7aqpkk57Ts [06:30]–[07:38], jK2dn9Dft5E [01:04]–[06:57]

### Scaling ad spend on a call funnel
**When to use:** the funnel is profitable and the question is how fast to push.
**The idea:** Scale by rules, not mood, and keep the closer math in step. Options: 10–30% a day; "surfing" (credited to Tim Burd), which means 10–30% several times a day while cost holds; or cost per X, adding cost-per-result × the extra results you want, with cost caps. At a ceiling, maintain and go find new messaging or a new funnel.
**How to run it:**
1. Pick the method. 10–30%/day is safe. Surfing and cost per X are aggressive.
2. Put a cost cap on before aggressive moves.
3. Re-run closer math at each step.
4. Watch for the messaging ceiling: costs rise as you reach colder, needs-convincing buyers, so update messaging to educate (Zr8m8euJ3zw [04:59]–[05:43]).
**The numbers:** Cost per X example: $100 per call × 10 more calls = +$1,000/day (rZCe1U3ME4E [1:43:29]). Horizontal scaling seen in a consult: duplicate a working campaign, change only the destination URL. It was done seven times at near-identical cost per call (bZe1YnKTBCE [49:25]–[51:11], guest-told).
**Mistakes they call out:** Cost per X without a cost cap. Believing small-scale ROAS will hold.
**Ask first:** Can sales absorb the extra calls this week? What's the cost cap?
**Source:** rZCe1U3ME4E [1:43:29]–[1:46:06], lz4XrSxI1Y0 [54:18]–[56:39], P1v-AN3So3A [1:35:37]–[1:39:12]
