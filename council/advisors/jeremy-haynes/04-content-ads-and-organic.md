# 04 — Content ads, retargeting and organic

**Open this file when:** the question is about running content as paid ads, retargeting, moving an organic business onto paid, or how a founder's organic content (YouTube, Instagram, personal brand) should be built to sell.
**His thesis:** "I would try to replicate the organic sales process via paid advertising" (a487OJkeiTc [00:00]). Paid compresses what organic does over months into a cash cycle you control.
**Speaker note:** gRvcbQbFwUs is a joint session with Jeremy Moser, who runs a done-for-you Instagram agency. The 3x3 format grid, the ManyChat comment vs DM flow, setter math and per-post chat-ID tracking are Moser's, not Haynes'. They're labelled where used.

---

### Replicate the organic sales process via paid ads (his core content-ad idea)
**When to use:** the sales team needs warm leads, organic buyers consumed lots of content before buying, or straight direct response isn't converting.
**The idea:** Organic buyers consumed an unknown amount of content before they bought. Cold direct-response ads skip that, so leads arrive curious and closers struggle. Content ads put the consumption back in, before or after the direct-response step. Everyone benefits except a "monster" sales team that routinely closes cold leads.
**How to run it:**
1. Decide whether the market already knows it has the problem.
2. Unaware market → *lead* with content (Venus Fly Trap).
3. Aware market → direct response first, then *follow* with content (Hammer Them in `03`, content cycle bins, the Forester).
4. Use organic winners as the content.
5. Measure content spend blended with direct-response spend. Split-test direct response alone vs the content setup.
**The numbers:** Content distribution gets reach "hundreds of times" cheaper than an off-platform conversion event (XpgHBp9SUEw [21:47]).
**Mistakes they call out:** Straight direct response when organic was content-driven (QwZ3X7GGSVw [08:07]).
**Ask first:** Can your closers close cold leads? Does your market know it has this problem?
**Source:** a487OJkeiTc [00:00]–[02:39], QwZ3X7GGSVw [06:56]–[15:17], bPkM0TCGFQQ [07:50]–[08:34]

### The Venus Fly Trap (his name, original 2015 strategy)
**When to use:** only when the market doesn't yet identify as a customer: a new category, a mass market, or a product that needs framing. Not for roofers, pizza or realtors.
**The idea:** A three-video sequence gated by view thresholds, then direct response. It's if-this-then-that email automation logic applied to video remarketing. Watch 25% of video 1 to see video 2, 25% of video 2 to see video 3, 25% of video 3 to unlock the direct-response ads. The third video's 25% view is the "trigger hair". "You are willingly choosing to add friction to your advertising process" (XpgHBp9SUEw [04:37]), so use it only when that friction buys framing.
**How to run it:**
1. V1: key education video with the promise front-loaded in its first 25%.
2. V2: a case study with results in the first 25%.
3. V3: another case study (different problem or customer type) or an objection-handling/trust video.
4. Engagement campaign, ad sets optimized for ThruPlay. Each step targets 25% viewers of the prior video and excludes 25% viewers of its own.
5. Launch all four steps at once and optimize for the people who move fastest.
6. Budget steps down gently: most on V1, less per step. If a step is starved, raise the prior step or the starved step.
**The numbers:** Default threshold 25% for reels up to ~90s, "anywhere from 20 to 30 times the average attention" [08:05]. Average attention per post 1.8 seconds [06:52]. Each step loses "upwards of 75 to 90 plus%" [27:34]. A 25% view is retargetable for ~25–50 cents [22:34]. Example at scale: ~$3,000/day V1, $2,000/day V2 (the V3 figure is garbled in captions) [19:53].
**Mistakes they call out:**
- Not accumulating enough people per step, so direct response never serves: "one of the most common mistakes" [19:30].
- Steps that fall off a cliff.
- Using it for markets that already know their problem.
- Retargeting 3-second or 15-second viewers, who only saw the hook.
**Ask first:** Does the buyer already know they need this? How many people reach step 3 per day?
**Source:** XpgHBp9SUEw [00:00]–[31:25], a487OJkeiTc [00:22]

### The Forester and content cycle bins (his names)
**When to use:** CPMs are very high, or cold direct response needs warming, at any funnel type.
**The idea:** A content cycle bin is one campaign with one ad set per piece of content. Each ad set excludes people who watched 3 seconds of that piece, so nobody sees the same piece twice. No CTAs, pure content. The Forester runs bin 1 to ice-cold audiences only to make them retargetable at a 3-second view, which costs about a penny. Then it hits that audience with direct response and a second, strategic content bin at the same time. Platforms make warm people cheaper to reach, so you "manufacture" warmth cheaply and create familiarity and a "swarm effect".
**How to run it:**
1. Bin 1 → the same cold targeting you'd use for direct response (lookalikes, broad, interest stack). The only goal is a 3-second view.
2. Custom audience: watched 3+ seconds of anything in bin 1.
3. At the same time, run (a) direct response to that audience through any funnel and (b) bin 2: testimonials, use cases, onboarding or first-experience videos, long-form expertise, stage talks, podcasts.
4. Best on Facebook, Instagram, TikTok and LinkedIn. Not recommended on YouTube.
**The numbers:** A 3-second view costs "a penny", sometimes a tenth or a hundredth, typically 1–2 cents (a487OJkeiTc [11:01]). He claims it has cut CPMs from $200–300 down to dozens, "chopped in half" [04:32].
**Mistakes they call out:** Picking bin 2 content without strategy.
**Ask first:** What are your CPMs? Do you have 20+ pieces of proof and education content?
**Source:** a487OJkeiTc [03:24]–[15:07], QwZ3X7GGSVw [18:19]–[20:56]

### Propaganda content (his framing)
**When to use:** shaping how buyers think before the decision, answering public attacks, or making content that pre-handles objections.
**The idea:** He calls himself "a propagandist": content that gives buyers ways to think and adjusts beliefs so they're more likely to buy at decision time. Most buyers are a swing vote between the always-sold and the never-sold. Cover the four quadrants: questions, the questions those answers raise, expectations, objections. A repeatable "content flywheel" format makes it sustainable.
**How to run it:**
1. List the beliefs the swing buyer needs before they buy.
2. Make direct pieces (answer a claim head-on with receipts) and indirect ones (wrap the view inside a topic they'll watch).
3. Run the mute test: watch with sound off, then listen with no picture. The visuals should sell as much as the words.
4. Make expectation-setting content showing how a good customer behaves. It also filters time-wasters.
5. If organic reach is small, pay to distribute it to people at each sales stage.
**The numbers:** None given as benchmarks.
**Mistakes they call out:** Relying on words without visuals. Obvious propaganda instead of invisible framing.
**Ask first:** What does the buyer have to believe before they'll pay? What attack or rumour is circulating?
**Source:** 051it6Mhp88 [00:00]–[36:21]

### Optimize content for income, not views
**When to use:** setting a founder's content strategy, choosing topics, or judging whether content is "working".
**The idea:** "I never optimize around views. I optimize around revenue" (9Xz7b0Gx-HA [00:22]). Every video is built for what the top-tier ideal client needs right now, usually sourced from a real client conversation or a pattern across clients. Views from outside the target are a negative signal because they train the algorithm toward the wrong buyers. Pitch often and naturally. He calls the advice to use fewer CTAs for more views absurd.
**How to run it:**
1. Define the viewer by a revenue threshold, not a journey (`Talk to the top of the umbrella` below).
2. Source ideas from client calls, group chats and DMs. When several clients hit the same problem, that's a video.
3. Put CTAs throughout: about one-third in, halfway, at the end (0fmguwV_dMg [1:28:24]).
4. Judge a premium channel by shares, DMs and buyers, not views or comments.
5. Decide how much to reveal by business model. Education businesses share 10–20% and sell the rest. A done-for-you agency should reveal nearly everything, make clear how hard it is, then offer to just do it for them (9Xz7b0Gx-HA [08:48]).
**The numbers:** His main channel averages ~2,000 views, most videos 2–5K, best ~20K (9Xz7b0Gx-HA [15:17]–[15:40]). A main ICP channel at 1K–10K views is normal, 20K is "a banger", and 500 views from 8–9-figure owners is a full room (0fmguwV_dMg [44:40], [46:09]). YouTube drives "over 90%", "about 95%" of buyers, against the other channels' ~10% at higher cost (9Xz7b0Gx-HA [33:05]). He says "probably 98%" of ideas come from client problems (gRvcbQbFwUs [57:11]).
**Mistakes they call out:**
- Making content for anyone below the target: "If you ever make anything for anybody else, you'll attract anybody else" (0fmguwV_dMg [07:14]).
- Evergreen videos with no CTA.
- Repurposing everywhere instead of posting natively (gRvcbQbFwUs [40:55]).
**Ask first:** Who exactly is the buyer, and what revenue are they at? Which videos did your last 10 buyers watch?

### Talk to the top of the umbrella; actively deter the wrong people
**When to use:** choosing the ICP for content, wording hooks, or when the audience is growing but buyers aren't.
**The idea:** "Personal branding is not just about attracting the right audience" but about "actively deterring the wrong people at the same time" (0fmguwV_dMg [00:00]). Talk to the highest level of the ICP and everyone below watches aspirationally. Talking down never appeals upward. One word can decide who arrives. A copywriting-course ad that mentioned J.K. Rowling filled up with wannabe authors.
**How to run it:**
1. Define the who by a minimum revenue level and staff size, never by "zero to nine figures".
2. Filter every idea through that buyer's problems, circumstances and desired outcomes.
3. Put a threshold number in the hook so people below it drop off ("When I got to 1,500 orders a day...").
4. Check vocabulary. Does the buyer already use your term? If not, rename it or define it fast.
5. Watch the silent filters: where you film, office, cars, clothes. They repel some buyers and attract others.
**The numbers:** He never talks to anyone below $100K/month (0fmguwV_dMg [02:17]). Most of his buyers do "a couple hundred grand a month" when they join [1:06:16].
**Mistakes they call out:** Defining the who by a journey. "Don't ever go out of your way to talk at a low level" (0fmguwV_dMg [1:06:38]). "Don't talk to the floor" (bPkM0TCGFQQ [16:22]).
**Ask first:** What's the floor of your ICP in revenue? What word in your hooks might attract the wrong crowd?

### Demonstration ("actual thing") content and the follower cameraman
**When to use:** building a founder's personal brand, or deciding what to film.
**The idea:** Content that films the real thing as it happens beats talking about it in hindsight "in almost all instances" (0fmguwV_dMg [18:18]). Film the key moments, then sit down later and narrate over the footage (the Last Dance or post-game interview format). What's normal at your scale is aspirational to people below you. So a trusted, senior camera operator should follow the founder and sort the footage into channels.
**How to run it:**
1. Map where key moments happen: office, car, client sessions, team meetings, events.
2. Hire a camera operator you fully trust. "They can't be dumb. They can't be entry level" [1:04:47].
3. Aim for more than 50% of output filmed "in the trenches" [1:21:57].
4. Post a cornerstone piece that shows the product (e.g. a real client session) once a week or every other week. His version is the "business breakdown" consult videos [54:55]–[55:39].
5. Don't film the same piece twice for two audiences. Sort at posting time.
6. Keep production simple. "The messaging is what people find valuable" [1:58:45].
**The numbers:** His setup for the first ~1.5 years was a tripod and an iPad, 2 hours a week, with an editing team at ~$3.5–4K/month (9Xz7b0Gx-HA [05:06]; 0fmguwV_dMg [1:58:23]). Now 13–14 hours a week of filming (9Xz7b0Gx-HA [42:29]).
**Mistakes they call out:** Getting comfortable with a camera too late, so key moments are lost. He and Tai Lopez lost an unfilmed launch-planning session (0fmguwV_dMg [21:17]–[22:51]). Talking-head-only brands leave demonstration value on the table.
**Ask first:** What do you do every week that your buyer would pay to watch?
**Source:** 0fmguwV_dMg [16:44]–[23:57], [53:44]–[56:02], [1:04:24]–[1:05:31], [1:20:05]–[1:22:20]

### Channel architecture: YouTube first, one theme per channel
**When to use:** deciding platforms, posting cadence, or whether to post shorts or off-topic content.
**The idea:** YouTube is the long-tail asset that feeds and nurtures every other channel. "The most beneficial asset in terms of social media, personal brand assets is YouTube" (0fmguwV_dMg [2:10:29]). The algorithm distributes by viewer type, so each YouTube channel keeps one theme. Mindset and broad content go on a second channel, shorts and clips on a third. Treat it like a TV network and never miss a scheduled episode. Early views are low, but they are people already in your sales process.
**How to run it:**
1. Main channel: long-form only, ICP themes only. In his case Monday instruction (most CTAs), Wednesday breakdown/demo, Saturday a test or reach slot.
2. Second channel for mindset or broad topics ("Lessons for My Kids").
3. Third channel for shorts and 2–9 minute "mids" (The Haynes Feed).
4. Instagram and X can mix audiences. YouTube can't: "On YouTube, do not do the cross" (0fmguwV_dMg [39:46]).
5. Post as often as you can sustain. Consistency over frequency.
6. Every YouTube link points to a call funnel application so the team can vet buyers (gRvcbQbFwUs [1:11:23]).
**The numbers:** Two videos a week for two years at 2 hours/week, then 3 main + 1 second-channel a week (9Xz7b0Gx-HA [02:08]–[02:30]). His best videos run 40–60 minutes, his worst ~15 (0fmguwV_dMg [2:08:31]). Being 4–6 weeks ahead made him miss timely lessons, so he now stays 1–2 weeks ahead [2:00:36].
**Mistakes they call out:** Shorts would leave the main channel "polluted and poisoned": "Poor people watch shorts. Rich people do not watch shorts" (9Xz7b0Gx-HA [22:27]). Missing uploads. Expecting fast distribution.
**Ask first:** Which channel do your buyers say they watched? Can you sustain the cadence for two years?
**Source:** 9Xz7b0Gx-HA [12:45]–[27:15], 0fmguwV_dMg [37:53]–[42:03], [1:56:30]–[2:10:52], gRvcbQbFwUs [09:49]–[11:20]

### Instagram: use it, never depend on it
**When to use:** Instagram is the main or only acquisition channel, or someone wants to scale IG DMs.
**The idea:** He posts heavily on Instagram, with a comment-word CTA on every post through ManyChat, and says "some people, IG is the conversion mechanism". But "Instagram is a terrible place to scale your business" (9Xz7b0Gx-HA [34:25]). Someone can pay to delete a channel or restrict DMs, and the profit sits in those DMs. He only expanded into IG once recurring revenue meant losing it wouldn't be catastrophic.
**How to run it:**
1. Use IG as a conversion and nurture layer, with YouTube as the asset.
2. Every post gets a comment or DM keyword CTA. Make keywords post-specific to track source.
3. In his flow: comment → ManyChat DM with a double opt-in button → asset → "let me know your thoughts" → only people who consumed it get pushed to a setter → call (gRvcbQbFwUs [1:06:17]–[1:08:11]).
4. Build a second funnel and channel before you need them.
**The numbers:** He posts 5 times a day (3 reels, 2 image posts) from one 1–2 hour shoot of 15 reels (9Xz7b0Gx-HA [37:02]). He says 8 a day in gRvcbQbFwUs [19:43] and kZmPPS4512I [22:35]. Moser (not Haynes): initial DM response rates fell from 80–90% to ~60%, targeting 10% of responders to a call (gRvcbQbFwUs [1:14:11]–[1:15:19]). Moser says setter teams start to break above about three for most people [1:16:27].
**Mistakes they call out:** Relying 100% on IG income. "I think scaling on Instagram is the scariest thing in the world" (organically; "Paid fine") (AAzziyOKCx8 [50:08]). Moser's warning: over-retargeting followers at $1,000/day burns them out (gRvcbQbFwUs [1:45:48]).
**Ask first:** What % of revenue would vanish if your account were restricted tomorrow?

### Pre-frame the future product (his "mastery level" tactic)
**When to use:** a founder will launch or raise the price of an offer later and wants demand waiting.
**The idea:** Warm the audience to what you'll eventually sell before they know it exists. Show yourself buying and valuing that kind of product. To sell time at extreme prices, make it obvious how busy, rich and in demand you are, so the price "makes sense" when it drops. Saying you have nothing to sell (the "stiff arm") lowers buyers' defenses and builds demand like water behind a dam.
**How to run it:**
1. Name the eventual product type: consulting, small group, done-for-you, events.
2. Post content of you consuming and praising that type.
3. Show the product in action (cornerstone content).
4. Decide in advance which public stances are hard rules and which you can compromise later.
**The numbers:** Some personal brands he has worked with sell 15-minute slots at $25,000. One buyer pays $100K up front for four 15-minute quarterly slots (0fmguwV_dMg [50:40]–[51:25]).
**Mistakes they call out:** Building an audience of the wrong people, which makes the eventual monetization "a catastrophic failure" (0fmguwV_dMg [07:14]).
**Ask first:** What will you sell in 12 months, and does today's content make it feel inevitable?
**Source:** 0fmguwV_dMg [28:46]–[34:49], [46:31]–[52:33], [59:28]–[1:01:43]

### Story-video structure: hook → story → payoff → CTA
**When to use:** scripting a founder's long-form or reel around a lesson.
**The idea:** Don't open on a topic (e.g. announcing "today I'll talk about AI"). Open on a specific, emotional moment the ICP recognizes ("I'm staring at the ads manager..."). Find the point where the story really starts. Use context clues (scale numbers, a spouse and kids, a team) so the right viewer knows it's about them. The payoff answers "what do I do now?", and sometimes the payoff is the CTA.
**How to run it:**
1. Hook on a moment, with a threshold number if you can.
2. Story with context clues. Don't assume they know your references.
3. Payoff: the lesson and what to do.
4. Bridge to the CTA: "here's what you should do just in case."
5. Stop once the payoff lands. Don't pad.
**The numbers:** None given.
**Mistakes they call out:** Topic openers. Generic stories without context clues. Leaving the viewer hanging.
**Ask first:** At what moment does this story actually start?
**Source:** 0fmguwV_dMg [1:33:35]–[1:47:59], [1:55:20]–[1:56:07]
