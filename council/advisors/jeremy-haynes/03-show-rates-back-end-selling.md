# 03 — Show rates and back-end selling systems

**Open this file when:** show rate is low, leads no-show or ghost, close rates fell suddenly, sales cycles are long, closers say leads arrive cold, or there's a reputation problem (Reddit, Trustpilot, AI answers).
**His stance:** "we have to after they book the call, sell them on why they need to show up" (WEwwAvTY1B4 [22:51]). And when operators say they did everything, they usually did ~10%: "Don't gaslight yourself" (Z5LG8QtpBTs [20:11]).

---

### Scanner mode → justification mode (his terms)
**When to use:** explaining why front-end consumption is low, or why booked leads don't show.
**The idea:** Before the key action (opt-in, booking, purchase, DM) people are in energy-saving scanner mode, making snap "is this for me?" calls. After the action they switch to justification mode: is this worth the hour, and is now the time? That's when they actually consume, and they "aggressively" look for information. If you don't give it to them, they find it elsewhere in a frame you didn't choose. So education starts the moment of the action.
**How to run it:**
1. Keep the front end lean (`02` page layout).
2. Load the back end: confirmation page, emails, setters, Hammer Them.
3. Every back-end asset answers two things: this is for you, and now is the time, with real reasons.
**The numbers:** The ad retention graph is "always a cliff" after the first 3 seconds (sYofhOZxx4Q [04:56]). Paid needs cash back within ~30 days, while organic can carry a 3–6 month cycle (sD55I_RH5ho [04:31]–[04:54]).
**Mistakes they call out:** Assuming people who booked are sold on showing up (rZCe1U3ME4E [1:18:29]). Believing that most consumption happens on the front end: "That's just not how it works" (QwZ3X7GGSVw [22:30]).
**Ask first:** What does a lead receive in the first hour after booking?
**Source:** sD55I_RH5ho [01:52]–[04:31], sYofhOZxx4Q [04:10]–[06:30], rZCe1U3ME4E [1:17:00]–[1:19:37], AAzziyOKCx8 [33:18]–[34:24]

### The five back-end selling systems
**When to use:** any show-rate, close-rate or cycle-length problem on a paid funnel. He calls them mandatory for call funnels.
**The idea:** He says he built them "out of rage" at salespeople who only want layups. The design test: "how could I get a team of monkeys to jump on these phones and be able to close these deals?" (AAzziyOKCx8 [23:22]). Together they "condense the trust cycle": higher show and close rates, shorter cycles, more paid-in-full.
**How to run it:**
1. **Confirmation page best practices.** Main urgency or due-diligence video, then breakout videos, then testimonials (cards below).
2. **Value-dense email sequences.** Real buyer questions, not reminders.
3. **Setter / pre-call best practices.** Education and assets before the call, Four Seasons tone.
4. **Hammer Them.** Paid short- and long-form content retargeted to people between booking and call.
5. **AI manipulation mastery.** Make Google AI Overviews, ChatGPT, Claude and Gemini say accurate, positive things. He keeps the details for paying members.
Do all of them. Your demographic consumes some unknown mix of the channels, so repeat the same messaging across all of them.
**The numbers:** Show-rate lifts up to ~30%, averaging ~17%. Two clients who only did the confirmation page saw 32% and 30% (QwZ3X7GGSVw [26:38]–[27:01]). Done fully, expect "at least 60 to 70%" show on call funnels (WEwwAvTY1B4 [35:54]). Community members improved show rates by 20, 30, some 40% (Z5LG8QtpBTs [24:43]). One key action can lift show "upwards of 30%", but usually it's ~10% plus a few small ones: "it can be one thing, but rarely is it one thing" (Z5LG8QtpBTs [02:15]–[02:39]).
**Mistakes they call out:** Trying one piece, seeing no lift, and quitting. "You take the action until you get the result" (Z5LG8QtpBTs [11:46]).
**Ask first:** Which of the five are live today, and what are their consumption stats?
**Source:** sD55I_RH5ho [05:17]–[06:49], sYofhOZxx4Q [00:00]–[01:54], Z5LG8QtpBTs [00:00]–[03:01], rZCe1U3ME4E [1:16:35]–[1:30:48], U0xz2ht-8nQ [14:03]–[16:12]

### Confirmation page: one main video, then breakouts
**When to use:** building the page people land on after booking or opting in.
**The idea:** The old page (thanks, check your email, accept the invite) is dead: "Nobody responds to any of that stuff anymore" (sD55I_RH5ho [00:23]). The headline acknowledges the action and points to the main video. The main video is always one of two things: an urgency video or a due-diligence video. Below it sit short breakout videos, each titled with the single question it answers. Testimonials, if any, go below the breakouts, because breakouts outperform them.
**How to run it:**
1. Headline: "you're in", plus a pointer to the main video.
2. Main video, chosen by the rule in the next card.
3. Breakouts, 1–3 minutes each, one per row. Topics come from only two sources: what the sales team is tired of or struggles to answer, and what you want leads framed on before the call.
4. Write copy so reading only the bold lines still lands the point.
5. Chapter every video. Use image thumbnails when there are many videos.
6. Load it heavily for niches that watch video. For niches that don't (blue collar reading emails from the truck), go light and lean on email.
**The numbers:** Benchmark: main and breakout videos at 50%+ play rate and 80%+ engagement (sD55I_RH5ho [31:52], [32:38]). "If you're not getting an 80% plus engagement rate, you know with certainty that you're saying the wrong things" [45:45]. Co-living webinar client: 19 breakouts lifted show ~4%. Adding an AI-researched 8-minute urgency video on top lifted it 19% and pushed every breakout to 50%+ play [13:14]–[18:53]. (He tells this story three ways: +19% in sD55I_RH5ho and AAzziyOKCx8, "20-something" in sYofhOZxx4Q [26:17], ~14% in Z5LG8QtpBTs [09:53]. Quote it as "double-digit".) His own cold webinar: 44% show [35:47].
**Mistakes they call out:**
- Copycats saying one video is enough: "the silliest thing I've heard" (sD55I_RH5ho [22:40]).
- Flooding the page with cherry-picked testimonials, which reads as fake.
- Inauthentic scarcity ("five slots left").
**Ask first:** What are the play and engagement rates on each video? What do closers keep explaining?
**Source:** sD55I_RH5ho [07:12]–[46:31], WEwwAvTY1B4 [20:09]–[29:19], P1v-AN3So3A [32:08]–[42:37]

### Urgency video vs due-diligence video (choosing the main video)
**When to use:** deciding what goes at the top of the confirmation page.
**The idea:** Look yourself up first. If search or AI shows negative or misleading results (Reddit threads, a one-star Trustpilot review, an AI Overview quoting the wrong price), make a due-diligence video. If results are clean, or leads give time-based excuses, make an urgency video. Rich buyers mostly need urgency. Regular buyers mostly need safety (AAzziyOKCx8 [36:37]–[37:23]).
**How to run it:**
- **Due diligence:**
  1. Google "[company] scam", "[company] reviews", "[your name] scam", "[your name] reviews". Ask ChatGPT the same.
  2. Screen-share and walk through the results one by one, adding frame and context.
  3. Answer the real fear: "how bad could it get?" Show refunds, the dispute rate, and what you did when a deal went bad.
  4. "You have your own news station": state what happened and how to think about it.
- **Urgency:**
  1. Find a real, third-party-verifiable reason to act now that fits their worldview (e.g. zoning rollbacks in 13 US cities for a co-living offer).
  2. Use an AI research agent: give it the situation and ask for real-world reasons someone would prioritize this now versus later.
  3. Script it to ~8 minutes.
- **The grandfather rule:** "If your grandfather knows about it, it's typically a safe thing to do" (sD55I_RH5ho [20:48]). For real estate, stocks or sales, the founder is a vessel for a bigger opportunity, so use urgency. If you're selling your own ideas, people must believe in you, so use due diligence.
**The numbers:** His own due-diligence video shows a 0.31% dispute rate, down from ~1.5% (sD55I_RH5ho [10:12]). A slandered wellness client's call-funnel show rate fell to ~18%, and a due-diligence video with the documented communications made it "rocket" (sYofhOZxx4Q [22:55]–[25:10]). An AI Overview quoted a $17K–$26K offer as ~$5K from a decade-old podcast (sYofhOZxx4Q [09:13]).
**Mistakes they call out:** Leaving search results unaddressed. Playing defense against slander: "Play some offense" (sYofhOZxx4Q [21:46]). Withholding price while AI answers with the wrong number.
**Ask first:** What does Google, Reddit and ChatGPT say about you right now? What excuse do no-shows give?
**Source:** sYofhOZxx4Q [06:54]–[30:32], sD55I_RH5ho [08:20]–[22:18], AAzziyOKCx8 [27:33]–[30:14], [34:44]–[37:46]

### Reputation is a "nuclear-level threat" to show rate
**When to use:** show or close rate dropped suddenly with no obvious cause.
**The idea:** Even one negative Trustpilot review or a Reddit thread can crater show rates, and both feed AI answers. He calls Trustpilot "the devil" and prefers Reddit as the modern review system for info and services: start a thread and ask every buyer for an honest review, good or bad. Show the downside openly ("here's how bad it could get"). A slightly imperfect rating (4.7) is trusted more than a perfect 5.
**How to run it:**
1. Search "[brand] Reddit", "[brand] scam", and ask ChatGPT and Google AI about you.
2. If haters rank, seed honest buyer threads.
3. Address it in a due-diligence video and an email.
4. Build tiered proof: best, average and below-average results, even 1–2 star reviews with what you did about them. "Those crush with regular person audiences for show" (AAzziyOKCx8 [38:55]).
**The numbers:** Dubai Inner Circle member, ~$25K service: show and close dropped, and two Reddit threads full of non-buyers ranked for the brand. He sent them to hundreds of clients, a little over 200 posted, and ~2.5 weeks later show and close rose above the previous benchmark (V7TUmeWHyao [14:33]–[18:19]). A prospect with Trustpilot/Reddit problems sat at 28% show (m4wYl6hrFIg [05:27]).
**Mistakes they call out:** Having a Trustpilot at all. Leading with Google or Trustpilot reviews as proof for non-local businesses.
**Ask first:** When did the drop start, and what ranks for your name?
**Source:** sD55I_RH5ho [42:19]–[44:38], AAzziyOKCx8 [34:44]–[38:55], V7TUmeWHyao [07:14]–[18:19], Zr8m8euJ3zw [19:47]–[20:53]

### Value-dense email sequences
**When to use:** writing pre-call or pre-webinar emails.
**The idea:** "The emails are not reminders. The emails are framing and education content" (A7aqpkk57Ts [15:01]). Interest is not static. It decays the moment life and the newsfeed resume, and every good email bumps it back. Weave the reminder in as one line inside a value email. Repeat the confirmation-page messaging, because you don't know which vehicle each lead prefers.
**How to run it:**
1. Email 1 teaches immediately after booking.
2. Topics: what the lead would research anyway, like testimonials, verifiable third-party proof, objections, the post-purchase process and timeline, expectations, the good and the bad.
3. Turn your best-performing confirmation video into emails with AI (transcribe it, give it your past emails for style).
4. Start at 2–3 a day and add one at a time while open rate holds. Roll back when it drops.
5. Check deliverability. Open rate is your proxy for inbox placement.
**The numbers:** Up to 6 a day (Z5LG8QtpBTs [15:36]; rZCe1U3ME4E [1:24:08]). 2024 example: one every 2 hours from 6am to 6pm, 12 in 48 hours, open rates 50–80% (A7aqpkk57Ts [14:12]–[15:24]). Open-rate target 40–60% (zyfpGi9uEUU [14:17]; P1v-AN3So3A [47:45]). Below that you're probably not in the primary inbox even if the deliverability score says ~100% [48:56]–[49:41]. Email link CTRs are "typically single digits", so put the value in the email, not behind a link (Z5LG8QtpBTs [14:49]).
**Mistakes they call out:** "Most email sequences are absolutely whack" (Z5LG8QtpBTs [14:03]). Selfish reminders like "make sure you show". Starting at 6 a day for a demographic that finds it annoying. Mind regulations by state and country.
**Ask first:** What's your open rate? How many emails between booking and call, and what do they teach?
**Source:** Z5LG8QtpBTs [12:53]–[18:39], A7aqpkk57Ts [13:47]–[20:20], rZCe1U3ME4E [1:22:39]–[1:25:21], P1v-AN3So3A [42:37]–[49:41]

### Texts, and the "Four Seasons" setter standard
**When to use:** defining what setters or closers do between booking and call.
**The idea:** The setter's job is not to "selfishly" qualify. It's to have the education call before the call, so the closing call becomes a sales conversation. Reach out like a five-star hotel concierge: warm, helpful, anticipating needs, and "assume the show up". Send real assets and a selfie video, by hand, from a real phone.
**How to run it:**
1. Within the first few hours of assignment, the closer or setter texts from a real iPhone with a short selfie video.
2. The first text offers a valuable asset and asks their preferred channel (his phrase: an "engagement bait text", Zr8m8euJ3zw [29:13]).
3. Confirm the calendar invite and link arrived (the most common sticking point), offer help, and send case studies of people like them ("I took a moment to look you up...").
4. Texts carry reminders, not value: on opt-in, ~24h before, the morning of, 1h, 10 minutes, at start, plus "having trouble joining?" (webinar version, P1v-AN3So3A [45:22]).
5. If the team says they have no capacity for this, replace them (Z5LG8QtpBTs [19:48]).
**The numbers:** Manual iPhone texts have a "doubledigit impact on show rates" (as captioned) vs automated blue-text services (QwZ3X7GGSVw [28:55]). Text open rates 80–90%+ (P1v-AN3So3A [47:45]).
**Mistakes they call out:** "He's sending the most selfish one-sided reminder text that you can imagine" (zyfpGi9uEUU [15:47]). Green-text bots. Texting the Zoom join link for webinars, which raises show but lowers conversion because people watch on phones (P1v-AN3So3A [48:09]).
**Ask first:** What does the first message after booking say, and who sends it from what number?
**Source:** Z5LG8QtpBTs [19:01]–[22:24], WEwwAvTY1B4 [39:21]–[40:57], kZmPPS4512I [12:55]–[13:17], QwZ3X7GGSVw [27:46]–[29:16]

### Hammer Them (his name): content retargeting between booking and call
**When to use:** any funnel with a gap between action and sale: booked call, webinar opt-in, low-ticket purchase, or a DM conversation.
**The idea:** Paid distribution of strategic short- and long-form content into the feeds of people who just took the action. Assume they're being swarmed by competitors' ads after they click, and add yourself to the swarm. Content should answer the four quadrants: questions, second-layer questions, objections, expectations. "People don't opt out of watching content" (QwZ3X7GGSVw [25:08]).
**How to run it:**
1. A dynamically updating audience of people who booked (CRM → custom audience).
2. Engagement or video-view campaign. One video per ad set, each excluding people who watched 3+ seconds of that video, so they rotate through the library.
3. Window: average book-to-call time (~72h). If under ~150 people, widen it (e.g. 30 days).
4. Load 30–50 short-form and 20–30 long-form pieces. Use organic winners.
5. At volume, split by stage: setter stage gets authority, results and urgency; closing stage gets testimonials, money, partner and expectations. Failed closing calls go to a 30-day bucket.
**The numbers:** Frequency target ~15–20 in the window (WEwwAvTY1B4 [32:25]; Z5LG8QtpBTs [23:11]). Minimum ~150 people per audience because match rate is never 100% (rZCe1U3ME4E [1:26:09]). 2024 version: ~25 pieces, average frequency 11 in 48 hours (A7aqpkk57Ts [20:42]–[21:28]).
**Mistakes they call out:** Short-form only, below the frequency target, content nobody asked about, "I put some basic videos out there that I guessed on" (Z5LG8QtpBTs [24:19], his mock of a client).
**Ask first:** How many booked leads a week? What content answers their top five questions?
**Source:** WEwwAvTY1B4 [32:02]–[35:09], rZCe1U3ME4E [1:26:09]–[1:28:52], QwZ3X7GGSVw [15:17]–[18:42], A7aqpkk57Ts [20:42]–[23:47]

### Sales assets and trust assets ("high trust consumables")
**When to use:** long-cycle, expensive offers where buyers need to verify claims.
**The idea:** Key moments in the sales process either create a lot of trust or get missed. "You are providing the research", otherwise leads find it themselves (V7TUmeWHyao [10:21]). Words from the company are "meaningless". Third-party data and hard numbers carry weight. The order matters as much as the asset.
**How to run it:**
1. Map the lead's path. At each step, ask what question is top of mind.
2. Build the asset for it: third-party data, a vetted study, an investor-level spreadsheet walkthrough, a deal list with real financials and a video breakdown, a vision video.
3. Sequence them through emails, Hammer Them and setter texts, not as a random "carpet bomb".
4. Looms are fine. Production value doesn't matter.
**The numbers:** Storage fund: 156 of 157 past investors reinvest, shown via a spreadsheet video (V7TUmeWHyao [21:48]). Done-for-you Airbnb deal list: 75%+ show, 40%+ close on $20K+ paid in full (kZmPPS4512I [17:33]).
**Mistakes they call out:** Showing assets the lead doesn't care about yet. Relying on reviews.
**Ask first:** What would a skeptical buyer need to see to believe the claim? What's the question right after that?
**Source:** V7TUmeWHyao [01:55]–[26:50], WEwwAvTY1B4 [36:18]–[39:21], kZmPPS4512I [15:37]–[18:44]

### Show-rate dig list (when show rate is the bottleneck)
**When to use:** bottleneck analysis says show rate is the stat to double.
**The idea:** He makes no excuses here: "if you struggle with a with a show rate issue you are not selling people on showing up" (A7aqpkk57Ts [17:40]). Go through the stack in order. Don't gaslight yourself about what's already in place.
**How to run it:**
1. Deliverability: primary inbox, open rates 40–60%+, real calendar invites (Zoom Webinars for webinars).
2. Confirmation page: play and engagement rates. Under 50% play, change thumbnails or topics. Under 80% engagement, change what you say.
3. What setters actually send. Secret-shop your own team: "When's the last time you secret shopped your sales team, right?" (zyfpGi9uEUU [16:55]).
4. Whether calls and texts land (iPhone unknown-sender filtering, real numbers).
5. Reputation search.
6. Booking window too long (see `02`).
7. Consumption vs messaging: if nothing moved after identical messaging everywhere, either it wasn't consumed (check the stats) or the messaging is wrong. Treat these differently (sD55I_RH5ho [47:16]–[49:56]).
**The numbers:** Show-rate norms: call funnel ~60% ideal on cold paid; 70% with serious optimization; 80% if very well set up (bZe1YnKTBCE [25:38]). Below 30% means a tech issue (rZCe1U3ME4E [13:06]).
**Mistakes they call out:** Chris (Inner Circle) claimed best practices but had implemented none (zyfpGi9uEUU [15:25]–[16:32]).
**Ask first:** Show rate by booking-to-call gap? Open rates? What did the last 10 no-shows receive?
**Source:** zyfpGi9uEUU [14:17]–[17:16], sD55I_RH5ho [45:01]–[49:56], Z5LG8QtpBTs [00:00]–[02:39]
