# 06 — Math, offers and diagnosis

**Open this file when:** you need benchmarks, a financial model, a bottleneck diagnosis, an offer for cold traffic, a recurring high-ticket offer or price rise, or a founder-level call (bet size, indecision, either/or, plateau).
**His rule:** "If you don't understand math, you definitely don't understand marketing" (jK2dn9Dft5E [00:00]).

---

### The data hierarchy (his term)
**When to use:** any "what's broken" or "what should we fix" question.
**The idea:** Look at a few top stats first: ROAS and cash collected per booked call (or net margin on paid traffic). Only if a top stat is bad do you open the stats that make it up, and only if one of those is out of range do you open its sub-stats. Sub-stats only matter when their parent is off.
**How to run it:**
1. Top: ROAS, revenue/cash per booked call.
2. Middle (call funnel): CPM → link CTR → clicks-to-qualified-booked-call → show → (2nd call booking, 2nd call show) → close → AOV.
3. Middle (webinar): CPM → link CTR → opt-in → webinar show (peak concurrent) → retention to pitch → booking rate → call show → close → AOV.
4. Sub-stats only for the weak one. Opt-in or page conversion bad → VSL play rate, engagement, application drop-off per question, booking rate, booking window. CTR bad → thumb-stop, drop-off, ad-to-page congruence. Show bad → `03` dig list.
5. Page conversion = qualified booked calls ÷ total clicks to the first page. A page-view-to-application metric "would give you an illusion that the page is converting in that range when it's not" (bZe1YnKTBCE [24:55]–[25:16]).
**The numbers:** See the benchmark table below.
**Mistakes they call out:** "it's always like some random vibe-driven decision instead of just a cool, calm, calculated decision" (zyfpGi9uEUU [01:08]). Too many sub-metrics in the model. Paying attention to low-level "meaningless" data.
**Ask first:** What are ROAS and cash per booked call this month? Is the reported data real?

### Bottleneck analysis and "the doubles game" (his terms)
**When to use:** after laying out the chain, to pick the one thing to work on.
**The idea:** One constriction holds back everything downstream. Of the stats that are out of range, fix the one that is easiest to double (or for CPM, halve). Don't chase a stat 2% off its benchmark while another sits at half. "The attention deserves to go to where specifically is bottlenecked now" (zyfpGi9uEUU [12:22]).
**How to run it:**
1. Write every stat next to its range.
2. Circle the out-of-range ones.
3. Ask "which one of these two seems easier to double?" (zyfpGi9uEUU [11:09]).
4. If you're confident how to fix it, dig into its sub-stats. If not, "just seek out data".
5. Exception: a terrible close rate with everything else on benchmark can be lead quality or messaging, not closers. Check upstream before blaming sales (jK2dn9Dft5E [23:40]–[24:02]).
6. If you've run out of ideas, "you've just run out of data to think with" (rZCe1U3ME4E [1:43:06]).
**The numbers:** Worked example: CPM $40, CTR 2%, page conversion 1.5%, show 60%, close 25%, AOV $4K. Doubling page conversion to 3% halves cost per call and doubles ROAS (rZCe1U3ME4E [1:41:33]–[1:42:44]). Consult example: page conversion 0.8% vs 3–5%. Two doublings would take ROAS from ~2.8 toward ~6 (bZe1YnKTBCE [27:03]–[27:48]).
**Mistakes they call out:** Walking in convinced "it's my ads, I need to film" before running the numbers (zyfpGi9uEUU [21:55]).
**Ask first:** Which stat is furthest below its range in relative terms?
**Source:** zyfpGi9uEUU [00:00]–[23:51], jK2dn9Dft5E [14:11]–[30:34], rZCe1U3ME4E [1:38:06]–[1:43:29], 4fae9a7mbp8 [01:53]–[02:37]

### Financial model on worst-case inputs and cash collected
**When to use:** before launching, before scaling, or when the team's reported stats don't match the bank.
**The idea:** One sheet per funnel, using worst-case inputs, to find the minimum test budget and how far each stat can slip before it stops making money. Use cash collected, never revenue or contract value. Then plug in the stats the team reports. If the model's ROAS doesn't match real ROAS, someone's numbers are wrong. "You want to operate in reality" (rZCe1U3ME4E [09:15]).
**How to run it:**
1. Call funnel: spend → cost per call → show → (2nd call) → close → cash per sale → gross ROAS → minus fixed, team, commissions → net profit → net ROAS.
2. Webinar: spend → CPL → show → booking → call show → close → cash. Set call fields to 100% for direct checkout.
3. Stress-test one stat at a time.
4. Compare the model with the bank. Mismatch means a data problem.
**The numbers:** Example: $20K spend at $450 per call = 44 calls, × 60% show = 27, × 22% close = 6 sales × $14K cash ≈ $4 gross per $1, ≈ $2.5 net per $1 ("excellent") (jK2dn9Dft5E [04:46]–[06:13]). Halving show from 60% to 30% halved ROAS [06:35]. Kadisha's team stats implied 8:1 while reality was 2:1: "She's making decisions in a false world" (jK2dn9Dft5E [13:26]; sQ2diSyCd1w [1:04:41]).
**Mistakes they call out:** "Don't be one of those dummies optimizing around revenue" (P1v-AN3So3A [05:03]). "If your marketer is not doing [ __ ] like that you have the wrong marketer" (A7aqpkk57Ts [07:15]).
**Ask first:** What's cash collected per sale, not contract value? Does the model reproduce last month's real ROAS?
**Source:** jK2dn9Dft5E [01:04]–[13:50], rZCe1U3ME4E [07:20]–[13:51], A7aqpkk57Ts [06:30]–[07:38]

### Offers for cold traffic: two cliffs and a bridge
**When to use:** building or fixing an offer that has to convert strangers, or leads get colder as spend scales.
**The idea:** Build for cold, because what works cold works warm but not the reverse. His messaging SOP: one cliff is the buyer's current problems and circumstances, the other their desired outcomes. The offer is the bridge, and buyers only care about the bridge once both cliffs are described. Start from the buyer's current story (life stage, what they've tried, how they got burned), then map each stack item to a problem, circumstance or outcome. As you scale past in-market buyers into people who "need convinced", the messaging stack has to educate.
**How to run it:**
1. Research the buyer's narrative (AI agent on Reddit, X, IG, TikTok, plus real conversations).
2. List problems, circumstances, desired outcomes in their words.
3. Map every stack item to one of them.
4. Pick the majority hook: what the most financially qualified segment wants most. Avoid minority hooks.
5. After launch, AI-mine call recordings for objections and stalls. Big slices of the "pie chart" go into the core stack, small ones become post-commitment add-ons.
6. Solve the next problem after purchase. Deals die when buyers can't see what happens next.
**The numbers:** A client grew from ~$100K to ~$1.6–1.7M/month, then hit a ceiling that campaigns, angles, funnels and sales changes didn't fix until the offer changed (kiWQ3M6fiH4 [02:32]–[03:14]).
**Mistakes they call out:** Treating the offer as fixed. Making up the buyer's story. A bad offer shows up as friction everywhere: higher cost per call, lower show and close, longer cycles. "Stop watching this video and just go make yourself a great offer" (A7aqpkk57Ts [01:31]).
**Ask first:** What has your buyer already tried and been burned by? What objection kills the most deals?

### Guarantees, income claims and skeptics
**When to use:** someone wants to add a guarantee, income claim or big credential to ads or the VSL.
**The idea:** He treats guarantees and income claims as low-sophistication tactics that attract the worst leads. With sophisticated buyers, a guarantee creates objections instead of confidence. Big credibility claims create skeptics, who then ask why someone that big would help them hit a small target. Deter skeptics on purpose, and attract richer buyers with honesty and realistic expectations.
**How to run it:**
1. Strip income claims and guarantees from the front end.
2. Describe the conditions of the ideal customer instead.
3. Show the downside openly (due-diligence video, tiered testimonials).
4. Optimize for buyer types 1–3 on his risk spectrum (pioneers, groupthink followers, groupthink-plus-safety). "You actively want to deter skeptics at all costs" (Zr8m8euJ3zw [02:36]).
**The numbers:** A $300K/month, $10K-offer client running "10K a month in 90 days guaranteed" ads saw lead quality improve as soon as the messaging was fixed (WEwwAvTY1B4 [09:25]–[14:02]).
**Mistakes they call out:** "A guarantee does not create confidence" (WEwwAvTY1B4 [10:58]). Promising outcomes you can't control: "I can guarantee you I'll give you the sauce, but I can't guarantee you'll do [ __ ] with it" [13:16].
**Ask first:** Who does your current claim attract? What would a skeptical buyer ask first?
**Source:** WEwwAvTY1B4 [08:15]–[16:22], Zr8m8euJ3zw [01:25]–[02:50], sD55I_RH5ho [26:52]

### Recurring high-ticket offers
**When to use:** moving from one-off programs to retainers or memberships, or pricing a recurring offer.
**The idea:** Recurring works only if you deliver more value than the fee inside every 30-day cycle. "The main thing that you have to optimize around is the speed to you producing the result" (eZ2u4giDkvY [07:17]). Most sellers are "ongoing contribution" models (you must add fresh value monthly), not "ongoing access" models (rent, software). Price "fair" for the current result and raise it as certainty, results and member quality grow. Charging more raises consumption and results.
**How to run it:**
1. Define the monthly result and track value delivered per client.
2. Stack consumables that refresh on a cadence (calls, SOPs, community, tools).
3. Start lower and raise in steps as results compound.
4. As demand outruns delivery, add friction: annual agreements, stricter qualification, denying people near the bar.
5. Expect the audience to shift as price rises. Update messaging and speed up delivery.
**The numbers:** His Inner Circle went from $1,500/month in 2019 (with a $20K/month income floor) through $2K, $3K, $4K and $5K (with a $100K/month floor), then $6K for ~60 days ("Far too much" demand, ~15 then 21+ joins a month), then $10K/month (eZ2u4giDkvY [09:38]–[12:39]). He reports 3.4–3.7% annualized churn on it [00:44], self-told. A course repriced from a couple hundred dollars to $5K went from half to "100% of people logged in" [21:17]. "200 people at 5K a month is a million dollars a month" [15:17].
**Mistakes they call out:** Raising price "overnight" with "a flick of a switch". Not tracking what clients actually get. Stretching people who can't afford recurring payments.
**Ask first:** What result does a client get in month 1, 2 and 3? How do you measure it?
**Source:** eZ2u4giDkvY [00:00]–[24:40]

### Price signals and close-rate tells
**When to use:** deciding whether to raise prices, show prices, or restructure tiers.
**The idea:** Richer buyers read a higher price as a value signal. A very high close rate means the price is too low and the offer is hot. Showing price, even on the application, gets fewer, better calls when you sell to rich people. Hiding price is "usually the folks that sell to regular people". In tiered offers, beware anchor bias: pitching a tier that includes something the buyer lacks makes them feel overcharged or disqualified.
**How to run it:**
1. Check close rate. At 60–70%, raise prices (bPkM0TCGFQQ [27:42]–[28:04]).
2. Rich ICP: state the price early (his Inner Circle VSL states it straight away, rL1FIHwg8PE [01:52]).
3. Pitch the flagship. Mention extras as a side bonus ("hey, by the way, we also train our key staff", bPkM0TCGFQQ [30:19]) rather than as the tier's identity.
4. Check that AI answers quote your real price (`03`).
**The numbers:** Guest with $15K/$30K/$45K tiers and ~$16K average cash collected at a 60–70% close rate (bPkM0TCGFQQ [01:07]–[02:14], [27:42]).
**Mistakes they call out:** Pricing below every competitor after secret-shopping them (kZmPPS4512I [20:16]). Withholding price while AI quotes a wrong one.
**Ask first:** What's your close rate on qualified calls? Who are you priced against?
**Source:** bPkM0TCGFQQ [27:42]–[30:42], lz4XrSxI1Y0 [16:47]–[18:22], [28:01]–[29:52], eZ2u4giDkvY [12:17]–[12:39]

### Founder calls: reasons, either/or, highest-impact work
**When to use:** a founder is stuck, plateaued, indecisive, or wants to pause one part of the business to fix another.
**The idea:** He diagnoses plateaus as a ceiling, a missing strategy or offer, or "because of me" (comfort). "It's always reasons. Reasons back behavior" (bPkM0TCGFQQ [49:32]). If naming your reasons doesn't change your actions, they aren't strong enough. He rejects either/or thinking (sales OR fulfillment): "Sales to a highly successful business is breathing" [00:00]. Stay on the highest revenue-per-hour work, which for a founder-led agency is content, sales calls, pipeline follow-up, and creative outreach, and don't "pull themselves out of the trenches a little too early" (sQ2diSyCd1w [45:32]).
**How to run it:**
1. Validate reality (the model vs the bank).
2. Name the plateau type.
3. Never pause acquisition to rebuild fulfillment. Run both.
4. If indecision is the blocker, pick the easiest action that gets you moving (bPkM0TCGFQQ [1:06:43]).
5. In a contraction, reach out early instead of isolating.
6. Founder-as-closer is fine up to roughly $200–400K/month with 1–2 sales staff (sQ2diSyCd1w [29:28]–[30:11]).
**The numbers:** Ben (Inner Circle) coasted ~$115K/month for over a year, then reached $180K the next quarter after finding a reason (bPkM0TCGFQQ [51:00]–[55:30]).
**Mistakes they call out:** "A classic 100k to 300k a month mistake is this idea of either/ors" (bPkM0TCGFQQ [31:04]). Taking two weeks to build a 30-minute opt-in page. Delegating the highest-$/hour work too early.
**Ask first:** What would you do this week if you had to double revenue? What's stopping you?
**Source:** bPkM0TCGFQQ [31:04]–[1:18:20], sQ2diSyCd1w [24:04]–[1:13:53]

### Creative outreach that "can't be efficient for you"
**When to use:** cold outbound for high-ticket services, especially when templated DMs and emails get ignored.
**The idea:** Inboxes are flooded with templated pitches, which makes it "easier than it's ever been to actually stand out" if you personalize hard (sQ2diSyCd1w [35:14]–[35:37]). "It can't be efficient for you. It's one of those actions that's inefficient" [35:37]. Research each prospect for a few minutes and send something that proves you understand them.
**How to run it:**
1. Research 2–5 minutes per prospect.
2. Send something specific: a custom video, sample work (e.g. three finished thumbnails), a physical package with a handwritten note.
3. Use AI agents to read the prospect's socials for preferences.
**The numbers:** His COVID-era physical video-pitch folders got "80% plus" responses (sQ2diSyCd1w [35:14], self-told). A templated scraper campaign at ~30–40 a day got a low response (guest-reported, [31:49]).
**Mistakes they call out:** "Everybody always defers to their own efficiency and selfishness" [47:51].
**Ask first:** What would make this one prospect stop and reply?
**Source:** sQ2diSyCd1w [31:49]–[55:39]
